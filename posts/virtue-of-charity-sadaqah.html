The Virtue of Charity: How Sadaqah Lifts Lives and Multiplies Blessings

R Razia Azeem · 20 Oct, 2026

Most of us treat giving as a loss. Islam treats it as an investment — one the Quran promises to multiply hundreds of times over, and one that purifies the giver's heart. This very week, Indonesia's leadership called for stronger zakat institutions to fight poverty; here is what the Quran and Hadith have taught about charity all along.

The word sadaqah comes from the Arabic root sidq — truthfulness: your giving is a proof of your faith, because you hand over what you love while trusting in a reward you cannot see.

And giving is back in the headlines. On 19 October 2026, Indonesia's coordinating minister for law and human rights, Yusril Ihza Mahendra, called for stronger regulations and institutional frameworks for zakat management to support poverty alleviation and boost public welfare (Islamic Law in the News Roundup, 19 Oct 2026). A modern government, in other words, reached for the same solution Islam designed fourteen centuries ago.

1. Sadaqah and Zakat: Two Streams of Giving

Zakat is the obligatory third pillar of Islam: 2.5% of qualifying savings held for a full lunar year, due from every Muslim whose wealth crosses the nisab threshold. It is not a donation — it is the right of the poor over the wealth of the rich, and Allah names its recipients Himself (see section 4).

Sadaqah is everything voluntary: any amount, any time, for anyone in need. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Your smiling in the face of your brother is charity... your removal of a rock, a thorn or a bone from the road is charity for you" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1956, graded hasan). A date, a coin, a smile — all of it counts.

Zakat is the foundation; sadaqah is the endless building on top of it. Neither replaces the other.

2. Allah's Promise: Seven Hundred Times and More

مَثَلُ الَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ أَمْوَالَهُمْ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ حَبَّةٍ أَنبَتَتْ سَبْعَ سَنَابِلَ فِي كُلِّ سُنبُلَةٍ مِّائَةُ حَبَّةٍ ۗ وَاللَّهُ يُضَاعِفُ لِمَن يَشَاءُ ۗ وَاللَّهُ وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ

Quran 2:261

Translation of the meaning: "The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah is like a seed of grain which grows seven spikes; in each spike is a hundred grains. And Allah multiplies [His reward] for whom He wills. And Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing."

Seven spikes, a hundred grains each — seven hundred times the original — and even that is only the starting figure, because "Allah multiplies for whom He wills." Elsewhere Allah calls charity a qardan hasanan, a "goodly loan" to Him: "Indeed, the men who give charity and the women who give charity and have loaned Allah a goodly loan — it will be multiplied for them, and they will have a noble reward" (Quran 57:18).

The Prophet ﷺ even swore by it: "The wealth of a slave (of Allah) shall not be decreased by charity" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2325, graded sahih). Giving for Allah is the one "spending" that cannot make you poorer.

"Charity does not decrease wealth, no one forgives another except that Allah increases his honor, and no one humbles himself for the sake of Allah except that Allah raises his status."
— Sahih Muslim 2588, narrated by Abu Hurayrah (RA)

3. Charity Purifies Wealth — and Washes Away Sins

The word zakat itself means purification and growth. Allah commands: "Take, [O Muhammad], from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase" (Quran 9:103) — it cleanses the wealth it comes from, and the heart it leaves.

It also cleanses the giver's record: "charity extinguishes sins as water extinguishes fire" (Sunan at-Tirmidhi 2616, graded hasan sahih).

And the amount never disqualifies you: "Protect yourselves from Hell-fire, even if it is with one half of a date" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1417). So do not wait until you are wealthy to become charitable.

4. The Eight Doors: Who Has a Right to Receive

Allah did not leave zakat's recipients to guesswork. Quran 9:60 names eight categories: the poor (fuqara), the needy (masakin), those who collect and administer it, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, freeing captives, those burdened by debt, in the cause of Allah, and the stranded traveler.

That list is a complete welfare blueprint — food, work, debt relief, emergency aid. This is exactly what Indonesia's minister reached for this week: well-managed institutional zakat as a tool against poverty.

Sadaqah carries no such restriction — it may go to anyone in need, but begin close to home: "Giving charity to a poor person is charity, and (giving) to a relative is two things: charity and upholding the ties of kinship" (Sunan an-Nasa'i 2582).

5. Sadaqah Jariyah: The Gift That Outlives You

Some charity never stops working. Abu Hurayrah (RA) narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said: "When a person dies, his deeds come to an end except for three: continuing charity (sadaqah jariyah), beneficial knowledge, or a righteous child who prays for him" (Sahih Muslim 1631).

A well in a thirsty village, a masjid share, a gifted Quran, a planted tree — the reward keeps flowing after you are gone. Start one sadaqah jariyah project in your lifetime, however small.

6. Five Ways to Start Giving Today

  • Give something every day — even a coin. The half-a-date hadith (Bukhari 1417) means regularity matters more than size.
  • Give secretly — "If you conceal them and give them to the poor, it is better for you, and He will remove from you some of your misdeeds" (Quran 2:271).
  • Begin with family and neighbors — your needy relatives earn you charity and the reward of kinship (Nasa'i 2582).
  • Calculate your zakat yearly — review your savings on the same date each lunar year; 2.5% of what has been held for a year is the right of the eight recipients of 9:60.
  • Plant one sadaqah jariyah — Muslim 1631. One ongoing project: a water cooler for a masjid, sponsoring a child's education, planting trees.

Whatever you spend, He will compensate — "and He is the best of providers" (Quran 34:39). Give for His sake. For daily duas, browse the Dua Library; on answered dua, see 5 Keys That Open the Door of Accepted Dua.
