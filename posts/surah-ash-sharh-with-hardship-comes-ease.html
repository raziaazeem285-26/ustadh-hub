Surah Ash-Sharh: With Hardship Comes Ease

Quran

Eight verses. That is all it takes for the Quran to answer the hardest question a believer ever asks: when the pressure will not lift, does Allah still see me? Surah Ash-Sharh — the Expansion, also called Al-Inshirah, the Relief — answers with a question of its own: "Did We not expand for you your chest?" For the surah that came just before it, read Surah Ad-Duha: Your Lord Has Not Forsaken You.

1.The Surah That Follows Ad-Duha

Surah Ash-Sharh is the 94th surah of the Quran: eight verses, revealed in Makkah, in the thirtieth juz. In the order of revelation it is the twelfth — revealed immediately after Surah Ad-Duha and before Surah Al-'Asr. The two belong together so closely that some early scholars held them to be a single surah. Ad-Duha reassured the Prophet ﷺ from the outside: "Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor is He displeased with you" (Quran 93:3, in meaning). Ash-Sharh turns the reassurance inward: it is not only that Allah has not left you — He has opened your chest, lifted your burden, and raised your name.

And the weight was real. The surah came down in the early Makkan years, when the Prophet ﷺ had gone from being called al-Amin, the trustworthy, to being slandered as a sorcerer, a liar, a madman — a man preaching alone to a hostile city. That is the moment Allah chose to speak about the expansion of the chest. The promise of this surah was not given to a man in comfort. It was given to a man under pressure — and it is given to us in the same way.

2."Did We Not Expand for You Your Chest?"

The surah opens with the question that gives it its name:

أَلَمۡ نَشۡرَحۡ لَكَ صَدۡرَكَ

Quran 94:1 — "Did We not expand for you your chest?"

Sharh means to open, to lay open, to make wide. Ibn Kathir explains: "We illuminated it and made it wide and spacious." The commentators understand the expansion on two levels. The first is literal: on the Night Journey, Jibril opened the Prophet's chest, washed it with Zamzam water, and filled it with wisdom and faith from a golden tray (Sahih al-Bukhari 349). The second is spiritual, and it is the one this verse promises to every believer: a heart made wide enough to carry its burden without breaking.

That second level is described elsewhere in the Quran, in the same word:

فَمَن یُرِدِ ٱللَّهُ أَن یَهۡدِیَهُۥ یَشۡرَحۡ صَدۡرَهُۥ لِلۡإِسۡلَـٰمِۖ

Quran 6:125 (opening clause) — "So whoever Allah wants to guide, He opens his chest to Islam."

And again: "Is one whose chest Allah has opened to Islam, so he is upon a light from his Lord?" (Quran 39:22, in meaning). A wide chest is not a personality trait. It is a gift from Allah — and one you can ask for. When Musa (AS) was sent to Pharaoh, he did not ask for an army. He asked for this:

قَالَ رَبِّ ٱشۡرَحۡ لِی صَدۡرِی

Quran 20:25 — "My Lord, expand for me my breast."

"And ease for me my task, and untie the knot from my tongue" (Quran 20:26-27, in meaning). Before the hard conversation, before the hard day — ask for the expanded chest first. The task does not shrink. You grow wide enough to carry it.

3.The Burden That Weighed Upon His Back

After the chest comes the burden:

وَوَضَعۡنَا عَنكَ وِزۡرَكَ

Quran 94:2 — "And We removed from you your burden."

ٱلَّذِیۤ أَنقَضَ ظَهۡرَكَ

Quran 94:3 — "Which had weighed upon your back."

The word for burden here, wizr, is a heavy load — the kind that bends the back of the one who carries it. The commentators explain it in two ways. The first is the weight of the mission itself: preaching one God to a city of idols, standing alone while the mockery and the threats piled up, carrying a trust no one else could carry. The second is the sins Allah forgave him — "that Allah may forgive you your faults" (Quran 48:2, in meaning). Whichever reading you take, the point is the same: the weight is lifted by Allah, not by the Prophet's own strength.

The world says: get stronger shoulders. The Quran says: the shoulders are Allah's concern, and so is the weight. Your part is the trust — see our article on Tawakkul: Trusting Allah After You Have Done Your Part. The burden was removed the moment Allah decided to remove it, and the man who had carried it walked on lighter — not because the road had changed, but because the load had.

4."And We Raised High for You Your Mention"

The third blessing is the one that answers the mockery directly:

وَرَفَعۡنَا لَكَ ذِكۡرَكَ

Quran 94:4 — "And We raised high for you your mention."

The Quraysh had tried to erase his name. They called him a sorcerer, a liar, a madman — and their plan, as far as they could see, was working: the man isolated, his followers few, his message rejected. Allah's answer was not to defend the Prophet's reputation in the streets of Makkah. It was to raise his mention beyond anything they could touch. The commentators explain (Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi; al-Baghawi reports it from Ibn Abbas) that his name is joined to Allah's own name in the adhan, the iqamah, the tashahhud and the khutbah: no adhan, no prayer, no sermon is complete without Muhammad ﷺ mentioned alongside Allah. Whenever Allah is remembered, His Messenger is remembered with Him.

That is the highest honour Allah gave to any human being — and it carries a lesson for us all. Your reputation is not yours to defend. It is Allah's to raise or to lower. The Prophet ﷺ never answered slander with slander or campaigned for his own name. He did the work, and Allah raised the mention. If people speak against you unfairly, do the work, and leave the mention to the One who raised it for His Prophet.

5."For Indeed, With Hardship Comes Ease"

Then comes the promise — the two verses this surah is most loved for. Allah says it twice, word for word, and the repetition is the emphasis:

فَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلۡعُسۡرِ یُسۡرًا

Quran 94:5 — "For indeed, with hardship [will be] ease."

إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلۡعُسۡرِ یُسۡرࣰا

Quran 94:6 — "Indeed, with hardship [will be] ease."

Read the promise carefully: the word that matters most is the smallest one — with. The ease does not only come after the hardship. It comes with it — alongside it, inside it, at the same time. The hardship and the ease share the same days. That is why the believer in the hardest week of his life can still find, in the middle of it, a prayer that lands, a friend who calls, a verse that arrives at the right moment. The ease was never scheduled for later. It was travelling with the hardship all along.

And the Arabic carries a second promise inside the first. In these two verses, "hardship" is definite — al-'usr, one hardship — while "ease" is indefinite — yusr, an ease, and because the word is repeated, two eases. Hasan al-Basri, as recorded by Ibn Kathir, drew the conclusion: "One hardship cannot overcome two eases." One hardship, two eases — the arithmetic is always in your favour. Allah says the same elsewhere, in the plainest words: "Allah will bring about ease after hardship" (Quran 65:7, in meaning).

What does the ease look like? Not always the removal of the hardship. Sometimes the ease is the patience itself. The Prophet ﷺ said: "How wonderful is the affair of the believer! There is good for him in everything... if he is afflicted he is patient and that is good for him, and if he is given prosperity he is thankful and that is good for him" (Sahih Muslim 2999). And even the pain has a purpose: "No fatigue, nor disease, nor sorrow, nor sadness, nor hurt, nor distress befalls a Muslim, even if it were the prick he receives from a thorn, but that Allah expiates some of his sins for that" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5641). The hardship is not wasted. It is counted, weighed, and answered — with ease.

6."So When You Have Finished, Devote Yourself"

The surah closes with a command — the only command in it — and it is a command about what to do after the work is done:

فَإِذَا فَرَغۡتَ فَٱنصَبۡ

Quran 94:7 — "So when you have finished, devote yourself."

وَإِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَٱرۡغَب

Quran 94:8 — "And to your Lord direct your longing."

Fa-idha faraghta fansab — when you have finished, stand up and strive. The commentators explain it as the rhythm of the believer's day: when you finish your prayer, turn to supplication; when you finish one task, turn to the next act of worship; when the work of the world is done, do not collapse into idleness. And the last verse gives the direction: wa ila rabbika farghab — to your Lord alone direct your longing, your hope, your desire. Not to people. Not to the outcome. To your Lord.

This is the same rhythm the Prophet ﷺ lived every night — the subject of our study of Surah Al-Muzzammil: The Night Prayer That Built a Mission: the day's work finished, the night given to prayer, the longing directed to Allah alone. The surah of relief ends, fittingly, not with a promise but with a practice. The promise is that the ease is already with you. The practice is to turn to the One who promised it — every time you finish anything at all.

7.Five Lessons for Your Hardest Day

1. The ease is already inside the hardship. Do not wait for the hardship to end before you look for it — the patience you did not know you had, the prayer that finally lands, the friend who calls at the right hour.

2. A wide chest is a gift you can ask for. Musa (AS) asked for it before facing Pharaoh, and the Prophet's chest was opened before the Night Journey. Before the hard conversation, before the hard day, pray his dua: "My Lord, expand for me my breast, and ease for me my task" (Quran 20:25-26, in meaning). The task does not shrink — you grow wide enough to carry it.

3. Your name is Allah's to raise. The Quraysh tried to erase the Prophet's name, and Allah joined it to His own in every adhan on earth. Stop defending your reputation — do the work, and leave the mention to the One who raised it for His Prophet.

4. Finish, then turn. After every task — the meeting, the chore, the prayer itself — turn to Allah for a moment before you move on. The day is built of these turns.

5. Hardship is the sign of the mission, not its failure. The Prophet's chest was expanded because the burden was heavy. Allah does not expand the chest of someone with nothing to carry. If your load feels heavy, that is not proof Allah has left you. It may be proof He is preparing you to carry more.

"For indeed, with hardship [will be] ease. Indeed, with hardship [will be] ease." — Quran 94:5-6

Eight verses. Read them slowly tonight — the question, the burden, the mention, the promise twice, the practice at the end — and let the promise do its work. If this surah steadied your heart, share it with someone whose chest feels tight today, and remind them of the arithmetic: one hardship, two eases. The ease is already with them. It always was.
