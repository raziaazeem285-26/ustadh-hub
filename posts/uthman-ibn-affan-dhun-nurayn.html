Uthman ibn Affan (RA): The Generous Caliph Who Gave Us One Quran

R Razia Azeem · 26 Nov, 2026

Between the strength of Umar and the courage of Ali stands a caliph history remembers for something quieter — generosity and modesty. Uthman ibn Affan (RA) ruled the Muslim world for twelve years, and in that time he did two things no other ruler in history has matched: he spent a fortune of his own wealth so the Ummah would never thirst and never march unarmed, and he gave every Muslim on earth the same Quran. The Prophet ﷺ named him among the ten Companions promised Paradise: "Abu Bakr is in Paradise, 'Umar is in Paradise, 'Uthman is in Paradise, 'Ali is in Paradise..." (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3747, Sahih). This is his story — and what it asks of us. For the caliphs before him, read Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (RA): The Closest Companion and Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA): The Justice of Al-Farooq.

1.Dhun-Nurayn: The Man of Two Lights

Uthman was called Dhun-Nurayn — the Possessor of Two Lights — because he married not one but two daughters of the Prophet ﷺ. His first wife was Ruqayyah, who died in 2 AH, in the days of the Battle of Badr, while Uthman stayed behind in Madinah nursing her. After her death, the Prophet ﷺ gave him her sister Umm Kulthum in marriage; she died in 9 AH. No other man in history married two daughters of a Prophet. The title is not a decoration — it is a measure of how close the Prophet held him, and how the family of the Prophet trusted him with its own daughters, one after another.

He was among the very first to accept Islam, one of the early few who believed before the message was preached openly. And he was among the foremost emigrants — one of those Allah describes in the Quran:

وَٱلسَّـٰبِقُونَ ٱلۡأَوَّلُونَ مِنَ ٱلۡمُهَـٰجِرِینَ وَٱلۡأَنصَارِ وَٱلَّذِینَ ٱتَّبَعُوهُم بِإِحۡسَـٰنࣲ رَّضِیَ ٱللَّهُ عَنۡهُمۡ وَرَضُوا۟ عَنۡهُ وَأَعَدَّ لَهُمۡ جَنَّـٰتࣲ تَجۡرِی تَحۡتَهَا ٱلۡأَنۡهَـٰرُ خَـٰلِدِینَ فِیهَاۤ أَبَدࣰاۚ ذَ ٰ⁠لِكَ ٱلۡفَوۡزُ ٱلۡعَظِیمُ

Quran 9:100 — "And the first forerunners among the Muhajireen and the Ansar and those who followed them with good conduct — Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him, and He has prepared for them gardens beneath which rivers flow, wherein they will abide forever. That is the great attainment."

Uthman stands inside that verse: a forerunner, one of the first, and one of those Allah is pleased with. Everything that follows in his life — the wealth, the giving, the Quran — flows from that first decision to believe early, when believing cost everything.

2.The Well of Rumah

When the Muslims settled in Madinah, water was scarce, and the best well in the city — the well of Rumah — belonged to a man who sold its water at a high price. The poor could not afford it. The Prophet ﷺ announced: "Whoever buys the well of Rumah will have Paradise." Uthman bought it — at a steep price, for it was the finest well in Madinah — and made its water free for every Muslim. Years later, when rebels besieged his house, Uthman reminded them of it from his rooftop: "Don't you know that Allah's Messenger ﷺ said, 'Whoever will buy and dig the well of Rumah will be granted Paradise,' and I bought and dug it?" They attested whatever he said (Sahih al-Bukhari 2778).

Think about what he bought. He did not buy a palace or land for himself. He bought water — the one thing nobody can live without — and then gave it away. His wealth never sat still. It moved, immediately, into the hands of people who needed it. That is the first lesson of Uthman's life: wealth is not what you keep. It is what you put where it keeps working after you.

3.The Army of Hardship

In 9 AH came the expedition to Tabuk — the longest march of the Prophet's life, in the fiercest heat of summer, against the might of Rome. It was called the Army of Hardship, and the Prophet ﷺ stood on the minbar urging the Muslims to equip it. Uthman stood up and said: "O Messenger of Allah! I will take the responsibility of one hundred camels, including their saddles and water-skins, in the path of Allah." The Prophet urged again. Uthman stood again: "Two hundred camels." Again: "Three hundred camels, including their saddles and water-skins, in the path of Allah." Then the Prophet ﷺ descended from the minbar saying: "It does not matter what 'Uthman does after this, it does not matter what 'Uthman does after this" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3700). The same promise is recorded in the words Uthman himself quoted at the siege: "Whoever equips the army of 'Usra will be granted Paradise, and I equipped it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 2778).

Three hundred camels, fully equipped — the transport, the saddles, the water-skins — given in a single sitting, in public, without hesitation, and then again and again as the appeal continued. Allah describes such spending in the Quran: "The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah is like a seed of grain which grows seven spikes; in each spike is a hundred grains" (Quran 2:261, in meaning). Uthman planted his wealth the way that verse describes — and it is still growing: every Muslim who learned the Quran from a copy he provided, every traveller who drank from a well he freed, is part of that harvest.

4.The Modesty the Angels Share

Uthman's other defining quality was the opposite of his wealth — his shyness, his modesty. Aisha (RA) reports that the Prophet ﷺ was lying in her apartment with his thigh uncovered when Abu Bakr sought permission to enter. He entered and talked, and the Prophet did not stir. Then Umar entered, and the Prophet did not stir. Then Uthman sought permission — and the Prophet ﷺ sat up and set his clothes right. When Aisha asked why, he said: "Should I not show modesty to one whom even the angels show modesty?" (Sahih Muslim 2401).

Pause on that. The angels — who never disobey Allah — are shy before Uthman. His modesty was not weakness; it was the reason the Prophet treated him with a reverence he showed to no one else in that room. The strongest man in the room that day was not the one who spoke loudest. It was the one whose character made even the Prophet adjust his seat. Modesty is not the absence of strength. It is strength that has learned manners.

5.One Mushaf for the Whole Ummah

Uthman's greatest deed came near the end of his rule, and it is the reason every Muslim on earth reads the same Quran today. Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman came to him from the campaigns in Armenia and Azerbaijan, alarmed: the Muslims of Sham and Iraq were reciting the Quran differently, and he feared they would differ over the Book as the Jews and Christians had differed before them. Uthman acted at once. He borrowed the sheets of the Quran kept with Hafsa (RA), the Prophet's widow, and ordered Zayd ibn Thabit — who had written the revelation for the Prophet himself — together with three men of Quraysh, to produce perfect copies. Where they disagreed with Zayd, he told them, write it in the dialect of Quraysh, for the Quran was revealed in their tongue. Then he sent one copy to every Muslim province and ordered all other materials burned, so that the Ummah would never split over its Book (Sahih al-Bukhari 4987).

Every mushaf you have ever held descends from that decision. The Quran you read in your home, the one your children memorise in the madrassah, the one recited in Makkah and Madinah tonight — it is the Uthmanic text, preserved by one man's refusal to let the Ummah divide. And the man who preserved the Book was also the one who carried its teaching: the Prophet's words "The best among you are those who learn the Quran and teach it" were narrated by Uthman himself (Sahih al-Bukhari 5027). He did not only compile the Quran. He taught it — and then he protected it for all of us.

6.The Siege and the Martyrdom

Uthman's rule ended the way his life had been lived — without raising a sword against a Muslim. In 35 AH, rebels from the provinces besieged his house in Madinah for weeks, cutting off his water — the man who had freed the well of Rumah was denied a drink from his own city. From his rooftop he reminded them of the well and the army, and they confirmed every word (Sahih al-Bukhari 2778). The Companions offered to fight for him. He refused: he would not be the cause of Muslim blood being shed in the city of the Prophet. He was martyred in his home on 18 Dhul-Hijjah 35 AH (656 CE), while reciting the Quran — the histories record that he died with the mushaf open before him, the Book he had preserved for the Ummah in his hands at the end.

He ruled twelve years — the longest of the four Rightly Guided Caliphs — and in those years the Muslim world reached from Persia to North Africa. But his legacy is not measured in territory. It is measured in water, in camels, in copies of a Book. He gave the Ummah the things it could not give itself, and then he gave his life rather than divide it.

7.Five Lessons from Uthman

1. Wealth is a trust, not a trophy. Uthman was one of the richest men of his time, and the richest thing about him was his giving. Spend where it outlives you — water, knowledge, the Quran — and the reward outlives you too.

2. Modesty is strength. The angels are shy before the modest. If the Prophet ﷺ sat up straight for Uthman, then shyness before Allah and His people is not smallness — it is honour.

3. Unity is worth a hard decision. Burning the variant copies was a painful order, and Uthman gave it because a divided Book would mean a divided Ummah. When unity and comfort conflict, choose unity.

4. Answer hatred with patience, not with blood. Besieged and thirsty, Uthman reminded his enemies of his good deeds and refused to fight. He lost his life and kept his Ummah whole. Most of us will never face that test — but every family dispute, every community quarrel, is a small version of it.

5. Learn the Quran — and teach it. The man who preserved the Book also narrated its greatest encouragement to teach it: "The best among you are those who learn the Quran and teach it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5027). If you have never taught anyone a single verse, start today. Uthman's lesson for our children is in Noorani Qaida Lesson 12 — the harakat drill that turns letters into reading.

"Abu Bakr is in Paradise, 'Umar is in Paradise, 'Uthman is in Paradise, 'Ali is in Paradise..." — the Prophet ﷺ (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3747, Sahih)

Three caliphs, one after another, and now the third is complete: Abu Bakr the truthful, Umar the just, Uthman the generous. The man of two lights left us two things that will never go dark — a Book that is one, and an example of giving that never stops. When your wealth, your time, or your knowledge is in your hands, ask Uthman's question: what can I buy that will still be giving after I am gone? Then give it, and let the angels be shy before you too. If this story moved you, share it with someone who gives quietly — they are the Uthmans of our time.
