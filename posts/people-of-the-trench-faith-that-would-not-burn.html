The People of the Trench: Faith That Would Not Burn

R Razia Azeem · 23 Nov, 2026

The Quran tells it in a handful of verses. A king, a boy, a monk — and a fire that could not burn faith. This is the story of the People of the Trench.

Seerah & Stories

The Quran tells it in a handful of verses. A king, a boy, a monk — and a fire that could not burn faith. This is the story of the People of the Trench, from Surah Al-Buruj, the 85th surah — one of the most powerful stories of steadfastness ever told. The Prophet ﷺ told its full version to his companions: the hadith of the boy and the king, preserved in Sahih Muslim. For another story of young people who stood firm, read The Sleepers of the Cave: Faith That Slept for 309 Years.

1."Cursed Were the People of the Trench"

Surah Al-Buruj — "The Great Stars" — is a Makki surah of twenty-two verses. It opens with an oath: by the sky containing great stars, by the promised Day, and by the witness and the witnessed. Then it moves at once to its verdict:

قُتِلَ أَصۡحَـٰبُ ٱلۡأُخۡدُودِ

Quran 85:4 — "Cursed were the companions of the trench."

ٱلنَّارِ ذَاتِ ٱلۡوَقُودِ

Quran 85:5 — "[Containing] the fire full of fuel."

The word "cursed" — qutila — is the Quran's announcement of destruction upon the people who dug the trench. Then the surah shows us the scene:

وَهُمۡ عَلَىٰ مَا یَفۡعَلُونَ بِٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِینَ شُهُودࣱ

Quran 85:7 — "And they, to what they did against the believers, were witnesses."

They sat by the fire and watched — witnesses to their own crime. And there was another Witness present, as the surah says a few verses later. But first, the surah tells us what the believers had done to deserve this:

وَمَا نَقَمُوا۟ مِنۡهُمۡ إِلَّاۤ أَن یُؤۡمِنُوا۟ بِٱللَّهِ ٱلۡعَزِیزِ ٱلۡحَمِیدِ

Quran 85:8 — "And they resented them not except that they believed in Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Praiseworthy."

That was their only crime: they believed in Allah. The Quran names no king, no place, no number of believers. The commentators identify the king as Dhu Nuwas of Himyar, and the believers as the Christians of Najran — but that comes from tafsir, not from the Quran or the hadith itself. The names are left out on purpose: this story is about every persecutor and every believer, in every age.

2.The Boy, the Magician and the Monk

The full story comes in a long hadith narrated by Suhaib (may Allah be pleased with him), preserved in Sahih Muslim (3005). The Prophet ﷺ said: there lived a king before you, and he had a court magician. When the magician grew old, he asked the king to send him a boy to teach magic to. The king sent a young boy — and on his way to the magician each day, the boy found a monk sitting by the road. He listened to the monk's words, and they impressed him. So each day he sat with the monk on his way — and arrived late to the magician, who beat him for the delay.

Then one day a huge beast blocked the road. The boy said to himself: today I will know whether the magician or the monk is superior. He picked up a stone and said: "O Allah, if the affair of the monk is dearer to You than the affair of the magician, kill this animal so the people can move." He threw the stone, the animal died, and the people passed. He told the monk, who said: "My son, today you are superior to me — and you will soon be put to trial. If you are tried, do not give my clue." The boy began to heal the blind and the lepers, always saying: "I do not cure anyone. It is Allah Who cures."

3."I Do Not Cure Anyone — Allah Cures"

A companion of the king had gone blind, and when he heard about the boy he came with many gifts: if you cure me, all of this is yours. The boy said: "I myself do not cure anyone. It is Allah Who cures — and if you affirm faith in Allah, I shall supplicate Allah to cure you." The man believed, Allah cured him, and he returned to the king's side. "Who restored your eyesight?" "My Lord." "You have a Lord besides me?" "My Lord and your Lord is Allah." The king tortured him until he gave the clue of the boy. The boy was summoned, and when he refused to turn back, he was tortured until he gave the clue of the monk. The monk was brought and told to turn back. He refused — and the king had a saw placed in the middle of his head, and he was torn into two parts. The courtier suffered the same. The boy was brought last, and he too refused.

They were a monk, a blind man, and a boy — and the king, with all his armies, could not make them take back one sentence: my Lord is Allah.

4.The Mountain, the Sea, and the Arrow

The king handed the boy to his men: take him to a mountain and throw him down if he will not renounce his faith. On the mountain the boy said: "O Allah, save me from them as You will." The mountain quaked, the men fell, and the boy came walking back. The king sent him to sea in a small boat, with the same order. The boy prayed the same prayer, the boat capsized, the men drowned — and the boy came walking back again.

Then the boy said something extraordinary: "You cannot kill me until you do what I ask you to do." "What is that?" the king asked. "Gather the people in one plain, tie me to the trunk of a tree, take an arrow from my quiver, and say: In the name of Allah, the Lord of the boy — then shoot. If you do that, you will be able to kill me." The king did as the boy said. He shot — and the arrow struck the boy's temple. The boy placed his hand on the wound, and he died. And the people cried out, three times: "We believe in the Lord of the boy!" The king had killed the boy — and lost his whole kingdom's faith in the same moment. The boy's death was his victory: his execution became the call that guided a nation to Allah.

5.The Trenches of Fire

The courtiers came to the king: what you tried to prevent has happened — the people have believed. So the king ordered trenches dug at the roadsides, fires lit in them, and it was announced: whoever does not turn back will be thrown in. And the people were thrown in, one by one, until a woman came with her baby — and hesitated at the edge of the fire.

The persecutors sat and watched, and the believers were thrown in for one crime: belief. The Quran answers with a warning that is also a promise of justice:

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِینَ فَتَنُوا۟ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِینَ وَٱلۡمُؤۡمِنَـٰتِ ثُمَّ لَمۡ یَتُوبُوا۟ فَلَهُمۡ عَذَابُ جَهَنَّمَ وَلَهُمۡ عَذَابُ ٱلۡحَرِیقِ

Quran 85:10 — "Indeed, those who persecuted the believing men and believing women and then did not repent will have the punishment of Hell, and they will have the punishment of the Burning Fire."

The persecutors were witnesses — but not the only witness:

ٱلَّذِی لَهُۥ مُلۡكُ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَ ٰ⁠تِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِۚ وَٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَیۡءࣲ شَهِیدٌ

Quran 85:9 — "To Whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth. And Allah, over all things, is Witness."

No persecution is ever unseen. The fire in the trench was lit by men — but the Fire of the Hereafter belongs to Allah, reserved for those who persecute believers and never repent. Allah saw it all.

6.The Baby Who Spoke

The hadith ends with its smallest hero. A woman came to the fire with her infant, and she held back — a mother, the fire before her, her baby in her arms. And the baby spoke: "O mother, endure this trial, for you are upon the Truth." She went in. The Quran and the hadith name no one here — not the boy, not the monk, not the mother, not the baby. The names are left out so the choices stand out: a boy chose Allah over his life, a mother chose the Truth over her child's life, and a baby who could not yet walk spoke the words that carried his mother through the fire.

What did they gain? The surah answers:

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِینَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَـٰتِ لَهُمۡ جَنَّـٰتࣱ تَجۡرِی مِن تَحۡتِهَا ٱلۡأَنۡهَـٰرُۚ ذَ ٰ⁠لِكَ ٱلۡفَوۡزُ ٱلۡكَبِیرُ

Quran 85:11 — "Indeed, those who have believed and done righteous deeds will have gardens beneath which rivers flow. That is the great attainment."

The fire took their bodies. It did not take them. The Quran says of those killed in Allah's way:

وَلَا تَقُولُوا۟ لِمَن یُقۡتَلُ فِی سَبِیلِ ٱللَّهِ أَمۡوَ ٰ⁠تُۢۚ بَلۡ أَحۡیَاۤءࣱ وَلَـٰكِن لَّا تَشۡعُرُونَ

Quran 2:154 — "And do not say about those who are killed in the way of Allah, 'They are dead.' Rather, they are alive, but you do not perceive."

The believers of the trench walked into a fire and out of this world — and into gardens beneath which rivers flow. That is the great attainment: what waited beyond the fire was greater than anything it could take.

7.What the Trench Teaches Us

1. Faith is not negotiable. The believers were asked to say one sentence and take it back — and they would not. Their only crime was belief (Quran 85:8). When your faith costs you something, remember the trench. For how to hold firm when faith is tested, read Steadfastness (Istiqamah): Staying Firm on Allah's Path.

2. Defeat can be victory. The boy died — and a whole people believed. The king won the execution and lost his kingdom. A life given for Allah is never a loss.

3. Allah records everything. The persecutors sat as witnesses to their own crime (Quran 85:7) — and Allah is witness over all things (Quran 85:9). If you have been wronged and no one saw it, the trench is your comfort: the Witness who matters saw it all.

4. The martyr is alive. The fire took bodies, not souls. "Do not say about those who are killed in the way of Allah, 'They are dead'" (Quran 2:154, in meaning). The believers of the trench are alive with their Lord (Quran 85:11). Death for Allah's sake is not an end. It is a door.

5. Steadfastness brings angels. The Quran promises those who say "Our Lord is Allah" and remain steadfast that angels will descend, saying: "Do not fear, and do not grieve, and receive good tidings of Paradise":

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِینَ قَالُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ ثُمَّ ٱسۡتَقَـٰمُوا۟ تَتَنَزَّلُ عَلَیۡهِمُ ٱلۡمَلَـٰۤىِٕكَةُ أَلَّا تَخَافُوا۟ وَلَا تَحۡزَنُوا۟ وَأَبۡشِرُوا۟ بِٱلۡجَنَّةِ ٱلَّتِی كُنتُمۡ تُوعَدُونَ

Quran 41:30 — "Indeed, those who have said, 'Our Lord is Allah' and then remained steadfast — the angels will descend upon them, [saying], 'Do not fear and do not grieve but receive good tidings of Paradise, which you were promised.'"
"Cursed were the companions of the trench — the fire full of fuel — when they sat by it, and they, to what they did against the believers, were witnesses. And they resented them not except that they believed in Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Praiseworthy." — Quran 85:4-8

Tonight, tell this story to your children. A boy their age stood before a king and would not take back one sentence — and his courage guided a whole people to Allah. Ask Allah for the same steadfastness for them, and for yourself. Share this story with someone whose faith is being tested: the fire could burn bodies, but it could not burn faith.
