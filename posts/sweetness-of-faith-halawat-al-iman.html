The Sweetness of Faith: Three Qualities That Make Iman Taste Sweet

R Razia Azeem · 28 Nov, 2026

Faith has a taste. The Prophet ﷺ said iman has a sweetness, and three qualities bring it to the heart. Here they are — what they are, why they work, and how to start tasting them today.

  4. Second: Loving Others Only for Allah's Sake

Hadith

Some people pray for years and feel nothing. Others find a sweetness in their worship that no hardship can take away. The difference is not in how much they pray — it is in what their heart loves. The Prophet ﷺ said something astonishing about this: faith itself has a taste. Iman has a sweetness, halawat al-iman, and it can be tasted in the heart the way honey is tasted on the tongue. And he did not leave the sweetness as a mystery: he named exactly three qualities that bring it.

This article is about those three qualities. They are short enough to memorise in a minute, and deep enough to spend a lifetime on. Together they are a map of the whole religion: love for Allah, love for people for Allah's sake, and guarding the gift of Islam itself. The hadith is agreed upon — narrated by Anas ibn Malik (RA) in Sahih al-Bukhari (16) and Sahih Muslim (43).

1.Faith Has a Taste

We usually think of faith as something in the mind — a set of beliefs. But the Prophet ﷺ spoke about it as something in the heart — a flavour you can taste. That is why the same worship can feel heavy to one person and light to another. The one who tastes it wakes for Fajr without a struggle, gives charity without regret, and forgives without keeping score — not because the deeds are easy, but because the heart has found something in them that the tongue cannot describe. The Quran hints at this taste when it describes the believers: "But those who believe are stronger in love for Allah" (Quran 2:165, in meaning). Love is the flavour. Where love is strong, faith is sweet; where love is weak, faith is a duty carried on the shoulders. The three qualities in the hadith are three ways of strengthening that love — and the sweetness follows the love, the way the taste of a fruit follows the fruit itself.

Notice what the hadith does not say. It does not say the sweetness comes from praying more, fasting more, or knowing more — though all of those are good. It says it comes from three states of the heart. You do not need a scholar's knowledge or a saint's worship to taste it. You need a heart that loves in the right direction.

2.The Three Qualities

Anas ibn Malik (RA) narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said:

"Whoever possesses the following three qualities will have the sweetness (delight) of faith: 1. The one to whom Allah and His Apostle become dearer than anything else. 2. Who loves a person and he loves him only for Allah's sake. 3. Who hates to revert to disbelief as he hates to be thrown into the fire." — Sahih al-Bukhari 16; Sahih Muslim 43

Three qualities, three directions of the heart: upward to Allah and His Messenger, outward to people for His sake, and protective — a hatred of returning to disbelief as strong as the fear of fire. Each one has a test by which you can tell whether you really have it.

3.First: Loving Allah and His Messenger Above All

The first quality is the foundation, and the Quran puts it as a direct challenge. Allah says:

قُلۡ إِن كَانَ ءَابَاۤؤُكُمۡ وَأَبۡنَاۤؤُكُمۡ وَإِخۡوَ ٰ⁠نُكُمۡ وَأَزۡوَ ٰ⁠جُكُمۡ وَعَشِیرَتُكُمۡ وَأَمۡوَ ٰ⁠لٌ ٱقۡتَرَفۡتُمُوهَا وَتِجَـٰرَةࣱ تَخۡشَوۡنَ كَسَادَهَا وَمَسَـٰكِنُ تَرۡضَوۡنَهَاۤ أَحَبَّ إِلَیۡكُم مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِۦ وَجِهَادࣲ فِی سَبِیلِهِۦ فَتَرَبَّصُوا۟ حَتَّىٰ یَأۡتِیَ ٱللَّهُ بِأَمۡرِهِۦۗ وَٱللَّهُ لَا یَهۡدِی ٱلۡقَوۡمَ ٱلۡفَـٰسِقِینَ

Quran 9:24

"Say, [O Muhammad], 'If your fathers, your sons, your brothers, your wives, your relatives, wealth which you have obtained, commerce wherein you fear decline, and dwellings with which you are pleased are more beloved to you than Allah and His Messenger and jihad in His cause, then wait until Allah executes His command. And Allah does not guide the defiantly disobedient people.'" (Sahih International translation)

Read the list: fathers, sons, brothers, wives, relatives, wealth, trade, homes — everything a human being loves. Then Allah asks one question: are they more beloved to you than Allah and His Messenger? The verse does not ask you to stop loving your family or your wealth. It asks what happens when the two loves collide — when Fajr calls and sleep is sweet, when a deal is profitable but dishonest, when the Messenger's ﷺ teaching and your habit disagree. That collision is the test, and it happens every single day.

Allah gives the same test in a gentler form elsewhere: "Say, [O Muhammad], 'If you should love Allah, then follow me, [so] Allah will love you and forgive you your sins'" (Quran 3:31, in meaning). Love is proven by following: the one who loves the Prophet ﷺ follows his way — in prayer, in manners, in honesty, in mercy — and the reward is the greatest love of all: Allah's own love in return. And the Prophet ﷺ put the standard plainly: "None of you will have faith till he loves me more than his father, his children and all mankind" (Sahih al-Bukhari 14). The love of Allah and His Messenger is not one love among many. It is the love that orders all the others — and when it is first, everything else falls into its right place.

4.Second: Loving Others Only for Allah's Sake

The second quality turns that love outward. "The believers are but brothers" (Quran 49:10, in meaning) — and the Prophet ﷺ said, "None of you will have faith till he wishes for his (Muslim) brother what he likes for himself" (Sahih al-Bukhari 13). But the hadith adds a condition that changes everything: the love must be only for Allah's sake. Not for wealth, beauty, status, or what the person can do for you. Love him because he believes and is trying — even when he disagrees with you, even when he is not from your family, your city, or your group.

That kind of love has a reward that matches its purity. "Whoever loves for the sake of Allah, hates for the sake of Allah, gives for the sake of Allah, and withholds for the sake of Allah has perfected the faith" (Sunan Abi Dawud 4681, graded Sahih by al-Albani). On the Day of Resurrection, Allah will say: "Where are those who loved each other for the sake of My majesty? Today, I will shelter them in My shade on a day when there is no shade but Mine" (Sahih Muslim 2566). And among the seven whom Allah will shade on that Day are "two men who love one another for the sake of Allah, meeting for that reason, and parting with this love" (Sahih al-Bukhari 660; Sahih Muslim 1031). The Quran warns us why this matters: "Close friends on that Day will be foes to one another — except for the righteous" (Quran 43:67, in meaning). Friendships built on wealth or flattery turn to blame on the Day; friendships built on Allah last past it.

A story shows what this love looks like in practice. A man set out to visit a brother in another village — no business between them, no favour owed, nothing to gain. Allah sent an angel to wait for him on the road, and when the angel asked where he was going, the man said he was visiting a brother he loved for Allah's sake. The angel told him: Allah loves you as you have loved him (reported in Sahih Muslim). That is the test of the second quality: would you still love them if there were nothing in it for you? Read more about this love in our article on Love for Your Brother: The Hadith That Measures Your Faith.

5.Third: Hating to Return to Disbelief as One Hates the Fire

The third quality sounds strange at first. Why would hating something bring sweetness? Because it measures how much you value what you have. The one who hates returning to disbelief as much as he would hate to be thrown into a fire is a person who knows what he was saved from. Before Islam, he was lost. Allah guided him, forgave him, gave him a Book, a Prophet, a community, a direction — and he would rather burn than throw all of that away.

This quality is really gratitude in its strongest form. The one who values the gift guards it: he avoids the company that mocks the Deen, he does not leave his doubts unanswered, he does not let small sins harden into a habit that pulls him away. Allah commands the same guard: "O you who have believed, fear Allah as He should be feared and do not die except as Muslims" (Quran 3:102, in meaning). The fear here is not panic — it is care. You protect what you love, and this quality protects the greatest gift you own.

And if you have slipped, the door is open, and the same quality that hates the fall is the one that runs back. Read our article on Repentance (Tawbah): The Door That Never Closes. Hating the return to disbelief does not mean despairing over a mistake. It means never making peace with it — and coming back, every time, to the One who never turns a returning servant away.

6.Why These Three?

Look at the three qualities together and a pattern appears. The first is love for Allah. The second is love for people for Allah. The third is hatred of what takes you away from Allah. Three directions, one compass: the heart points to Allah in every relationship — upward to Him, outward to His servants, and inward to guard the gift. That is why Ibn Abbas (RA) said that the firmest bond of iman is loyalty and closeness for the sake of Allah, love for the sake of Allah, and hatred for the sake of Allah (graded hasan). The sweetness is not a separate reward added on top of these qualities. It is what these qualities feel like from the inside. A heart that loves Allah above all, loves people for Him, and guards its faith — that heart is at rest. And rest, after the noise of a life pulled in a hundred directions, tastes sweet. Doing good only for Allah is the same principle; read Sincerity (Ikhlas): Doing Good Only for Allah to go deeper.

7.Tasting It Today

The sweetness is not a prize for the perfect. It is a promise for whoever has the three qualities — and you can start building them today.

First, take the 9:24 test tonight. Name the one thing you love most — your child, your business, your comfort, your phone — and ask yourself honestly: if Allah's command and this thing ever collided, which would win? Just ask, and let the answer show you where your love really stands. Then fix one small collision this week: the prayer you keep delaying, the habit you keep excusing.

Second, do one act of love for Allah's sake today. Call a brother or sister you have not spoken to in months, help someone who cannot repay you, forgive someone who never apologised — only for Allah, telling no one. The sweetness hides in the secret deed.

Third, thank Allah for Islam itself every morning. Before you ask for anything, say Alhamdulillah for the guidance you did nothing to earn. Gratitude is how a gift is kept.

The Prophet ﷺ promised that whoever has these three qualities will taste the sweetness of faith. Start with one, then the next, and the taste will come. May Allah make us among those who love Him above all, love for His sake, and hold fast to His gift until the day we meet Him. Ameen.
