The Golden Door of the Kaaba: A Story in Gold and Trust

R Razia Azeem · 22 Oct, 2026

Millions of pilgrims gaze at the Kaaba's golden door — but behind its gold and calligraphy lies a remarkable story: seven Thai craftsmen who helped build it in 1979, and a small key that became one of Islam's greatest lessons in trust.

Seerah & Stories

Every year, millions of pilgrims in tawaf raise their eyes to a golden door set high on the northeastern wall of the Kaaba. Yet few know the story behind it: of seven Muslim craftsmen who journeyed from Thailand to help build it, and of a small key that became one of Islam's greatest lessons in trust.

1.A door millions gaze at

The door sits about two metres above the ground, on the northeastern wall — roughly three metres tall and nearly two metres wide, plated with some 280 kilograms of gold and adorned with carved Quranic calligraphy. Over the centuries the door has been remade many times; the golden door standing there today was installed in October 1979.

But what connects this door to Thailand? This month, Thailand's King and Queen presided over the country's 19th National Quran Competition in Pattani — a sign of the devotion of Thailand's Muslim community. And nearly fifty years ago, seven Muslim craftsmen from that same community were entrusted with the woodwork and metal craftsmanship of the Kaaba's golden door.

2.The House that Ibrahim (AS) raised

The story of the door begins thousands of years before gold ever touched it — with the House itself. The Quran tells us that Prophet Ibrahim (AS) and his son Ismail (AS) raised the foundations of this sacred House: "And [remember] when Ibrahim and Ismail were raising the foundations of the House, [saying]: Our Lord, accept this from us." (Quran 2:127)

Allah calls it the first house of worship established for mankind: "Indeed, the first House established for mankind was that at Bakkah — blessed, and a guidance for the worlds." (Quran 3:96) And He commanded that it be kept pure: "…purify My House for those who perform tawaf, and those who stand [in prayer], and those who bow and prostrate." (Quran 22:26) The Kaaba is Baytullah — the House of Allah. Its door is honoured because it is the door of His House.

3.The key that became an eternal trust

On the day of the Conquest of Makkah, the Prophet ﷺ arrived at the Kaaba and asked for its key. As narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari (4400), he called for Uthman bin Talha, opened the door, and entered inside with Bilal (RA), Usama (RA) and Uthman (RA), praying between the two front pillars.

The key belonged to Uthman ibn Talha of Banu Shaybah, the family that had guarded the Kaaba long before Islam. As the Prophet ﷺ emerged, Allah revealed a verse that turned a physical key into an eternal principle: "Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due…" (Quran 4:58)

According to Al-Wahidi's Asbab al-Nuzul, this verse was revealed concerning Uthman ibn Talha: the Prophet ﷺ returned the key to him and said: "Take the key, O household of Talha, a trust from Allah — none but a transgressor will take it from you." From that day until today, the key of the Kaaba has remained with the descendants of Uthman ibn Talha — the Banu Shaybah family — an unbroken chain of trust spanning nearly fourteen centuries.

"Take the key, O household of Talha, a trust from Allah — none but a transgressor will take it from you."— The Prophet ﷺ, at the Conquest of Makkah (reported in Al-Wahidi's Asbab al-Nuzul on Quran 4:58)

4.The golden door of 1979

In 1979, King Khalid bin Abdulaziz commissioned a new door for the Kaaba. The intricate woodwork and metal craftsmanship were entrusted to a team of seven Thai Muslims, led by Abdullah Naknawa — who also served as the team's Arabic translator — with three master carpenters and three goldsmiths.

Working in Makkah, they shaped the door of around three metres in height and nearly two metres in width, plated with approximately 280 kilograms of gold and decorated with carved Quranic calligraphy. The completed door was officially installed in October 1979 — and it is the very door pilgrims see today, nearly fifty years later. Decades afterwards, Abdullah Naknawa returned to Saudi Arabia as an honoured royal guest: a lifetime's legacy, woven into the heart of Makkah itself.

5.Lessons cast in gold

This door carries lessons for every believer. Amanah — trust is sacred. A small key became the occasion for a verse on returning trusts to their owners, and the Banu Shaybah family keeps that trust to this day. Ihsan — give your best work. Seven craftsmen offered their finest skill to a House of worship; whatever our work, excellence for Allah's sake turns a craft into worship. Unity — one House, one ummah. A king in Makkah, artisans from Thailand, one qibla for all believers. If you would like to read more about the sacred cities themselves, see Sanctity of the Haramain: Virtues of Makkah and Madinah.

6.A reminder, not a destination

The golden door gleams, but it is a reminder, not a destination. We face the Kaaba in prayer; we do not worship it: "So let them worship the Lord of this House." (Quran 106:3) When you next see a picture of that golden door, let your heart move past the gold to the trust it guards — the trust of amanah, of excellence, and of worship reserved for Allah alone. May Allah grant every Muslim the sight of His House and accept their worship there. Ameen.
