Sunnahs of Jummah: How to Spend Friday the Blessed Way

Friday is the master of days — a weekly gift Allah gave this ummah. From ghusl to Surah Al-Kahf, from abundant darood to the hour of answered dua, here is how the Prophet ﷺ taught us to spend it.

Duas & Fazilat

Every week, Friday arrives like a guest of honour. The Prophet ﷺ called it the best day the sun has risen upon — a day on which momentous events of human history took place (reported in Sahih Muslim). While the rest of the week runs on work and routine, Jummah is Allah's weekly reset button for the believer's heart. The question is: do we spend it the way the Prophet ﷺ taught us to?

1.Why Friday Is the Best Day

The Quran itself gives Friday a special status. There is an entire surah named Al-Jumu'ah, and in it Allah commands (Quran 62:9):

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا نُودِيَ لِلصَّلَاةِ مِن يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ فَاسْعَوْا إِلَىٰ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَذَرُوا الْبَيْعَ

Quran 62:9 — "O you who believe, when the call is made for prayer on Friday, hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trade."

"Hasten" — not stroll, not wait for the khutbah to start. And after the prayer? The very next verse (Quran 62:10) releases you back into the world: "then disperse through the land and seek the bounty of Allah, and remember Allah much that you may succeed." Friday is not a day off from the deen — it is the day the deen takes centre stage, and the rest of the week orbits around it.

2.Ghusl, Clean Clothes and Arriving Early

The Prophet ﷺ did not treat Friday like an ordinary day. In Sahih al-Bukhari (877), he said that taking a bath (ghusl) on Friday is obligatory for every adult — because you are presenting yourself before Allah and meeting your brothers in congregation. Add clean, fresh clothes and perfume, and you honour the day the way it deserves.

Then comes the reward that should make every believer run — not walk — to the masjid. A famous hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari (881) teaches that whoever comes to Jummah prayer in the first hour gets a reward like sacrificing a camel in Allah's cause; the second hour like a cow; then a ram; then a hen; then an egg. The angels, the hadith says, stand at the doors of the masjid recording the arrivals until the imam sits for the khutbah. Ask yourself: what time do you arrive?

3.Surah Al-Kahf: A Light Between Two Fridays

One of the most beloved Friday sunnahs is reciting Surah Al-Kahf (Chapter 18). The Prophet ﷺ said:

Whoever recites Surah Al-Kahf on Friday, a light will shine for him between the two Fridays — from this Jummah to the next.

This narration is reported by Al-Hakim and Al-Bayhaqi, and graded sahih (authentic) by Al-Albani (Sahih al-Jami' 6470). Scholars say the "light" means guidance and protection — clarity in your decisions, and a shield against the doubts and fitan of the week ahead. The surah itself is built around four great trials: the trial of faith (the people of the cave), of wealth (the two gardens), of knowledge (Musa and Khidr), and of power (Dhul-Qarnayn). Reading it weekly keeps these lessons fresh in the heart.

4.Darood in Abundance

Friday is also the day of salawat. In Sunan Abi Dawud (1047), the Prophet ﷺ said: "Among the most excellent of your days is Friday; so invoke many blessings on me on it, for your blessings will be shown to me."

Think about that promise — your darood is presented to him ﷺ. A practical habit: set a target for the day — one hundred salawat after Fajr, another hundred before Maghrib, or a counted tasbih in your pocket. Small numbers, done consistently, are what change a heart. Allah Himself sends blessings on the Prophet ﷺ (Quran 33:56): "Indeed, Allah and His angels send blessings upon the Prophet. O you who believe, send blessings upon him and greet him with peace."

5.The Hour of Answered Dua

Perhaps the most precious secret of Friday is the hidden hour. The Prophet ﷺ said, in Sahih al-Bukhari (935): "There is an hour on Friday, and if a Muslim gets it while praying and asks Allah for something, Allah will definitely give it to him." He pointed with his hand to show how brief it is — a moment, not an hour of the clock.

Which moment? A narration in Sunan Abi Dawud (1048) guides us: seek it in the last hour after Asr. So as Friday afternoon slides toward Maghrib, pause. Sit with your hands raised. Pour out everything — your sins, your family, your hopes, this ummah. Our article on unanswered dua and tawakkul goes deeper into the etiquette of asking — but the door is widest open on Friday afternoon. Do not waste it.

6.Listen to the Khutbah with a Present Heart

Finally, the khutbah itself is worship. When the imam speaks, phones go away and tongues fall silent — even telling a companion to be quiet is counted as an idle act, as the hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari warns. And the reward of full, attentive listening is immense. In Sahih Muslim (857), the Prophet ﷺ taught that whoever performs ghusl, comes to Jummah, prays what was destined for him, listens attentively until the imam finishes, and then prays with him — his sins between that Friday and the next are forgiven, with three extra days.

Ten days of forgiveness, every single week, for doing what every Muslim should do anyway. That is Allah's generosity on Jummah.

Friday will come again in seven days. Decide now: ghusl, early arrival, Surah Al-Kahf, abundant darood, the hour after Asr, and a silent, attentive heart in the khutbah. Do this consistently, and you will feel your weeks changing — one blessed Friday at a time.
