Duha Prayer: The Two Rak'ahs That Cover 360 Charities

R Razia Azeem · 5 Nov, 2026

Every morning, charity is due on every joint of your body — 360 of them. Two rak'ahs in the forenoon cover them all. What Duha prayer is, when to pray it, and how a ten-minute habit can change your whole day.

Duas & Fazilat

After Fajr, most of us rush into the day — work, school, cooking, children, phones. Between Fajr and Dhuhr there is a wide, quiet stretch of hours, and Islam places a small, luminous prayer right in the middle of it: Salat al-Duha, the forenoon prayer. It takes about ten minutes. The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught that it covers the charity due on every joint of the body, and that Allah Himself promises to take care of the rest of the day for the one who prays it at the start of it.

This article explains what Duha prayer is, what the Quran and Hadith actually promise for it, when exactly to pray it, how many rak'ahs to pray, and a simple seven-morning plan to make it part of your life. It is a voluntary prayer — no one is blamed for leaving it — but once you understand what it carries, you may never want to leave it again.

1.What Is Duha Prayer?

Duha means the forenoon — the bright part of the morning, after the sun has risen well and its light has spread. Allah swears by this time of day at the very start of Surah Ad-Duha, a whole surah named after it:

وَٱلضُّحَىٰ وَٱلَّيْلِ إِذَا سَجَىٰ

Quran 93:1–2 — "By the morning brightness, and by the night when it covers with darkness."

The oath is a sign of honour. When Allah swears by a time, He is drawing our attention to its value. The morning brightness is the time when the night's darkness has fully lifted, the world is awake, and the day's work begins. Duha prayer is the worship that belongs to that hour: a voluntary prayer offered after the sun has risen about a spear's height — roughly fifteen to twenty minutes after sunrise — and before the sun reaches its zenith, shortly before Dhuhr.

It is also called Salat al-Awwabin, the prayer of those who turn back to Allah in repentance. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, in a narration in Sahih Muslim (748), in meaning: the prayer of those who are penitent is observed when the young camels feel the heat of the sun — that is, in the late morning, when the day has warmed. That is the best time for Duha, though any time inside its window counts. Our article on the blessed deeds after Fajr covers the start of the morning; Duha is what fills the middle of it.

2.360 Charities in Two Rak'ahs

The great promise of Duha comes in a narration reported by Abu Dharr in Sahih Muslim (720). The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, in meaning, that in the morning charity is due on every joint of the body of each one of you: every saying of SubhanAllah is a charity, every saying of Alhamdulillah is a charity, every saying of La ilaha illAllah is a charity, every saying of Allahu Akbar is a charity, enjoining good is a charity, forbidding evil is a charity — and two rak'ahs which one prays in the forenoon will suffice for all of that.

"In the morning, charity is due on every joint of your body... and two rak'ahs which one prays in the forenoon will suffice." — Sahih Muslim 720, in meaning

Think about the scale of that. Another narration, reported from Buraydah in Sunan Abi Dawud and Musnad Ahmad, tells us that the human body has 360 joints, and charity is due on each one. The companions asked, in meaning: who can manage that, O Messenger of Allah? The answer was that even removing something harmful from the road counts — and if a person cannot manage all of it, two rak'ahs of Duha will be sufficient for him.

This is how wide the door of charity is in Islam. Charity is not only money. Every glorification of Allah is charity, every kind word is charity, every harm removed from someone's path is charity — and when the day is young and the list is long, two rak'ahs stand in for the whole of it. That is Allah's mercy: He does not ask for what we cannot do. He gives us one small act that covers a mountain of dues.

3.A Promise for the Whole Day

There is a second promise, and it is even more personal. In a hadith qudsi reported in Jami' at-Tirmidhi (475), graded sahih, the Prophet (peace be upon him) narrated that Allah, Blessed and Most High, said, in meaning: "Son of Adam, perform four rak'ahs for Me in the beginning of the day, and it will suffice you for the latter part of it."

Read that slowly. Four rak'ahs at the start of the day — and Allah takes care of the rest of it. The promise is not that nothing difficult will happen. Difficult things happen to the best of people. The promise is sufficiency: what you need will be provided, what you fear will be handled, and the day will not defeat you. The one who begins the day by turning to Allah is not left to face the day alone.

This is why the scholars connect Duha with tawakkul, trust in Allah. You do your part — you get up, you pray, you work honestly — and Allah does His part, which is everything you cannot control. Our article on tawakkul, trusting Allah after you have done your part, explains the same balance from the other side: trust is not sitting idle, it is acting first and then relying on Him. Duha is that first act, placed at the very start of the day, before the emails, the errands, and the worries have had their say.

4.The Prophet's Own Practice

Duha was not a prayer the Prophet (peace be upon him) mentioned once and forgot. It was part of his own life, and part of the advice he gave to those closest to him. Abu Hurayrah reported in Sahih al-Bukhari (1178) that his close friend, the Prophet, advised him to do three things and he would not leave them until he died: to fast three days every month, to offer the Duha prayer, and to offer Witr before sleeping. Three small acts, kept for a lifetime — and Duha is one of them.

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) was asked how many rak'ahs the Prophet prayed at the forenoon prayer, and she replied, in a narration in Sahih Muslim (719): four rak'ahs, but sometimes more, as he pleased. And Umm Hani (may Allah be pleased with her) narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari (1176) that on the day of the conquest of Makkah, the Prophet entered her house, took a bath, and offered eight rak'ahs of Duha — a prayer so light she had never seen its like, yet he performed its bowing and prostration perfectly.

From these narrations, the scholars summarise the numbers simply: the minimum is two rak'ahs, the average is four or six, and the most the Prophet was seen to pray was eight. Some scholars allow up to twelve, and the chapter heading in Sahih Muslim itself records the range — least two, best eight, average four or six — so a believer should not argue over the number. Two is enough for the promise. Four is a good daily habit, matching the hadith qudsi. Eight is the fullest form the Prophet was seen to pray. Choose what you can keep every day, because a small deed done regularly is better than a large deed done once and abandoned.

5.How to Pray Duha: A Simple Guide

Duha needs nothing special — no mosque, no congregation, no long preparation. Here is the whole practice, step by step.

Check the time. Duha begins about fifteen to twenty minutes after sunrise, once the sun has clearly risen, and ends about fifteen minutes before Dhuhr, when the sun is at its highest. A prayer timetable or a phone app will give you both times. The best time is the late morning, when the sun's heat is felt — but if the morning is busy, any time in the window counts.

Make wudu, then pray two rak'ahs at a time. Duha is prayed in pairs, like any voluntary prayer: two rak'ahs, salam, then two more if you wish. After Surah Al-Fatiha you may recite any surah you know. No particular surah is prescribed for Duha in the authentic narrations, so do not worry about getting it "right" — recite what you know well and with understanding. Some people like to recite Surah Ad-Duha or Surah Ash-Shams because of their morning theme, and that is fine, but it is a personal choice, not a requirement.

Finish with a short dua. After the salam, ask Allah for what the day needs — provision, protection, health, a soft heart. The morning is a time when duas are close to acceptance, and the prayer has just put you in Allah's care for the rest of the day.

One question people often ask: is Duha the same as Ishraq? The two rak'ahs prayed just after sunrise, after sitting in remembrance since Fajr, are sometimes called Ishraq, and scholars differ on whether the two are one prayer or two. Either way, the morning is not left empty. And because Duha is voluntary, it can be prayed at home, at work, or anywhere clean — a mother between chores, a student between classes, a shopkeeper before opening. The prayer fits the life; the life does not have to stop for the prayer.

6.Starting Tomorrow: A Seven-Morning Habit

Knowing the reward is one thing; building the habit is another. Here is a simple seven-morning plan — small enough to keep, serious enough to change the month.

Days one and two: take two rak'ahs. Pray two rak'ahs of Duha after breakfast, at whatever time suits you. Two rak'ahs is the whole promise — the charity on every joint is covered. Do not aim higher yet. Just prove to yourself that you can do it two mornings in a row.

Days three and four: move to four, and move later. Four rak'ahs is the number in the hadith qudsi, and the number Aisha reported as the Prophet's usual practice. Try praying them in the late morning, closer to the best time — perhaps before lunch, or in the quiet hour before Dhuhr. Feel the difference: the morning now has a pause in it, a moment that belongs to Allah alone.

Day five: tie it to something you already do. Habits survive when they are attached to habits. Pray Duha right after the Fajr adhkar, or right after dropping the children at school, or right before opening your shop. The anchor matters more than the clock. When the anchor happens, the prayer happens.

Days six and seven: add the dua, and keep it. After the salam, sit for one minute and make a short dua for the day ahead — for your family, your work, your heart. Then decide: this is now a fixed part of my morning. Two rak'ahs a day is a strong start; four a day is the habit the Prophet's companions aimed for. For more on the daily habits that shape a believer's life, explore all our articles on the blog, or read our reflection on the power of Tahajjud, the night prayer.

Allah swore by the morning brightness. Every morning, that brightness comes back — the sun rises, the light spreads, and the day is given to you fresh. Two rak'ahs is all it takes to meet it properly: the day's charity paid before the day has even begun, and the rest of the day placed in Allah's care. Tomorrow morning, when the sun has risen and the house is awake, take ten minutes. The joints of your body will thank you, and so will your day.
