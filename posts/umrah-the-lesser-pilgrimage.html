Umrah: The Lesser Pilgrimage That Washes the Heart Clean

Hajj comes once a year, in fixed days, for those who can afford it. Umrah has no fixed season: any day of the year, any Muslim who can reach Makkah may enter ihram, circle the House of Allah, and walk between Safa and Marwah — and come home with the sins between two Umrahs wiped away.

The Prophet ﷺ called it the lesser pilgrimage. This article explains what Umrah is, what the Quran and Hadith promise for it, how its four steps unfold, and how to prepare — heart first, then hands.

1.What Umrah Is — and What It Is Not

Umrah means a visit: a visit to the Sacred House in Makkah with ihram, tawaf, prayer, sa'i, and the cutting of the hair — a complete act of worship in its own right, not a rehearsal for Hajj.

Hajj is the fifth pillar of Islam: obligatory once in a lifetime for every Muslim who has the means, and only in the fixed days of Dhul-Hijjah. Umrah is not a pillar. The scholars differ on whether it is obligatory once in a lifetime or a confirmed sunnah, but all agree on its immense virtue. And unlike Hajj, Umrah may be performed at any time of the year, in Ramadan or outside it, once or many times.

The second thing is what Umrah does not do. An Umrah in Ramadan is equal to Hajj in reward — a promise we will come to shortly — but it does not replace the obligatory Hajj. The scholars are clear on this: the equality is in reward, not in obligation. A person who has never performed Hajj still owes it, no matter how many Umrahs they have made. Umrah is a door that stays open all year; Hajj is a pillar that must still be built.

2.The Command in the Quran

The Quran joins Hajj and Umrah in a single command. Allah says:

وَأَتِمُّوا۟ ٱلۡحَجَّ وَٱلۡعُمۡرَةَ لِلَّهِ

Quran 2:196

"And complete Hajj and Umrah for Allah." The verse goes on to describe the sacrifice owed if a pilgrim is prevented from completing the journey, and forbids shaving the head before the sacrifice reaches its place. The details that follow show how seriously Allah takes completing this worship: once you have begun it, finish it, for Allah.

The two hills of the sa'i have their own verse. Allah says:

إِنَّ ٱلصَّفَا وَٱلۡمَرۡوَةَ مِن شَعَاۤىِٕرِ ٱللَّهِ

Quran 2:158

"Indeed, Safa and Marwah are among the symbols of Allah." The verse continues: whoever performs Hajj or Umrah, there is no blame on them for going between the two hills — and whoever volunteers good, Allah is Appreciative and Knowing. These two small hills, which a mother once crossed searching for water for her child, are named in the Quran as symbols of Allah Himself. Every pilgrim who walks between them follows Hajar, the mother of Ismail, whose running Allah honoured by making it a rite of His worship until the end of time.

3.Umrah to Umrah: An Expiation Between Them

The greatest promise attached to Umrah is forgiveness. The Prophet ﷺ said:

"The performance of Umrah is an expiation for the sins committed between it and the previous one. And the reward of Hajj Mabrur — the Hajj accepted by Allah — is nothing except Paradise."
— Sahih al-Bukhari 1773; also Sahih Muslim 1349

One Umrah wipes away what came between it and the one before. Not the journey, not the money spent — the act itself, performed sincerely, becomes a washing. The scholars explain that this expiation covers minor sins; major sins require sincere repentance, and the rights of other people must still be settled with them. Between two Umrahs, a believer can return to Allah with a clean record, again and again.

This is why the early Muslims performed Umrah repeatedly — not as tourism, but as a way of life: whenever the heart grew heavy, the answer was to go back to the House and come home lighter. The Prophet ﷺ himself performed Umrah several times — prevented once at Hudaybiyyah, returning the following year, and again in the year of the Farewell Hajj.

4.Umrah in Ramadan: Equal to Hajj in Reward

Of all the seasons for Umrah, one carries a promise that stops the heart. Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Prophet ﷺ asked an Ansari woman who had stayed behind from Hajj why she had not come. She explained that her family had only two water-carrying camels; her husband and son had taken one for Hajj. The Prophet ﷺ said to her: "Perform Umrah when Ramadan comes, for Umrah in Ramadan is equal to Hajj in reward" — or words to that effect. (Sahih al-Bukhari 1782; Sahih Muslim 1256)

A woman who could not afford a camel for Hajj was given a path to the same reward in the month of fasting. The scholars explain that the equality is in reward, not in obligation — the Umrah does not discharge the duty of Hajj — and that the virtue is general, open to every Muslim who performs Umrah in Ramadan. The reward of worship grows with the virtue of its time, and no month is more virtuous than Ramadan.

This is why families across the Muslim world save all year for a Ramadan Umrah. But the promise is not only for Ramadan: every Umrah carries the expiation of the previous section, and Ramadan simply multiplies what is already great. There is a practical warning too: Ramadan Umrah costs more and the crowds are at their largest. A quiet Umrah in an ordinary month, performed with calm and presence of heart, may be better for you than a crowded Ramadan Umrah performed in exhaustion. The reward is with Allah — He knows your heart better than any calendar.

5.The Four Steps of Umrah

Umrah has four rites. Knowing them before you travel turns a confusing crowd into a clear act of worship.

First, ihram. Before crossing the miqat — the boundary the Prophet ﷺ fixed around Makkah — the pilgrim makes the intention for Umrah. Men change into two unstitched white cloths; women remain in their ordinary modest dress. From that moment, perfume, cutting hair or nails, covering the head for men, and marital relations become forbidden. The white cloths are the great equaliser of Umrah: in ihram, a king and a labourer look the same, and that is the point. You arrive before Allah with nothing that distinguishes you except your heart.

Second, tawaf. On reaching the Haram, the pilgrim circles the Kaaba seven times, starting at the Black Stone, keeping the House on the left. Men walk briskly in the first three circuits, following the practice of the Prophet ﷺ, and uncover the right shoulder. Between the circuits there is no fixed prayer — any sincere dua, in any language, is accepted. After the seventh circuit, the pilgrim prays two rak'ahs, ideally behind the Station of Ibrahim, of which the Quran says: "Take the Station of Ibrahim as a place of prayer" (Quran 2:125).

Third, sa'i. The pilgrim then walks seven lengths between the hills of Safa and Marwah, remembering Hajar's search for water. Men run between the two green markers, as she ran.

Fourth, halq or taqsir. The pilgrim leaves ihram by shaving the head or trimming the hair — shaving for men, a fingertip's length for women. With that, the Umrah is complete, and the restrictions of ihram lift. The whole journey, from the miqat to the haircut, can take a few hours. Its reward, the Prophet ﷺ promised, is the wiping away of sins.

6.The Bellows That Purify: Poverty and Sins

There is one more promise, and it answers the worry that stops many people from ever going: the money. Many believers hesitate over the money — what if the journey takes what my family needs? The Prophet ﷺ answered that fear directly. Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Alternate between Hajj and Umrah, for those two remove poverty and sins just as the bellows removes filth from iron, gold, and silver — and there is no reward for an accepted Hajj except Paradise." (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 810; graded Hasan Sahih by al-Tirmidhi)

A blacksmith's bellows burns away what does not belong in the iron, and it comes out cleaner and stronger. That is the image the Prophet ﷺ chose for pilgrimage. Hajj and Umrah do not make you poorer; they purify what you have, removing the poverty and the sins together, the way fire removes rust from iron. The scholars explain it plainly: spending in Allah's way in pilgrimage is not a loss — Allah replaces what is spent, and the sins are burned away with it.

This is not a reason to borrow beyond your means or neglect your family's needs. The scholars are clear that Umrah is only for those who can afford it without debt or hardship on their dependents. But for the person who has saved honestly and can go without harming anyone, the fear of "what if I spend too much" is answered by the Prophet ﷺ himself: the journey removes poverty; it does not create it. Go with a clean intention, spend from lawful earnings, and trust the promise.

7.Before You Go: Preparing Heart and Hands

A good Umrah begins long before the flight. Here is how to prepare.

Begin with repentance. Before you ask Allah for the reward of Umrah, settle what stands between you and Him: a sincere tawbah, and the rights of people — debts paid or arranged, apologies made, forgiveness sought from parents, spouse, and family.

Earn it lawfully. The money for Umrah should come from lawful earnings. Allah accepts only what is pure, and a journey built on interest, deception, or unpaid wages carries a weight no tawaf can lift. Save honestly, even if it takes longer.

Learn the rites before you travel. Read the steps above until you could explain them to a friend. Learn what breaks ihram and what does not. The pilgrims who struggle most in Makkah are not the ones with the least money; they are the ones who arrived without learning.

Prepare your body and your patience. Umrah means long walks, crowds, heat, and waiting. And prepare the rarest provision of all: patience with other pilgrims. You will be pushed in the tawaf, delayed at the gates, and tested by strangers. Every one of those tests is part of the worship. The person who keeps their temper in a crowd has performed an Umrah of the heart as well as of the feet.

Make your list of duas. Write down the people you will pray for — parents, children, teachers, the sick, the dead, the whole Ummah. The dua of the pilgrim is answered, so carry the duas of others with you. And when you return, the scholars say the sign of an accepted Umrah is a changed life: softer in speech, quicker to forgive, steadier in prayer. That is the real souvenir of Makkah.

Umrah is the journey any believer can make, in any month, at any age. Its rites take hours; its reward is the wiping away of sins; its promise in Ramadan is the reward of Hajj itself. If you have the means, do not keep putting it off for a perfect year that never comes. Save a little, learn the rites, settle your debts, and go.
