Zakat: The Charity That Purifies Wealth

R Razia Azeem · 13 Nov, 2026

Zakat is the third pillar of Islam — 2.5% of your savings, given once a year to the eight groups Allah named in Quran 9:60. Not a tax on your wealth, but its purification.

Daily Life

Every year, millions of Muslims set aside a small part of their wealth and give it away — not as generosity, but as a duty. That duty is zakat, and it is one of the five pillars on which Islam stands. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Islam is built upon five: the testimony that none has the right to be worshipped but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah; establishing prayer; giving zakah; Hajj; and fasting Ramadan" (Sahih al-Bukhari 8; Sahih Muslim 16). Zakat sits in that list beside prayer and fasting — not beside charity. It is worship, and it has been worship since the first years of Islam. The word itself tells you what it does: zakat means purification and growth.

1.Zakat Is a Pillar, Not a Donation

The Quran commands zakat in the same breath as prayer, again and again: "And establish prayer and give zakah and bow with those who bow [in worship and obedience]" (Quran 2:43, in meaning). The two are paired so often that Abu Bakr (RA) said he would fight anyone who differentiated between prayer and zakat. Prayer is the worship of the body; zakat is the worship of wealth — and a Muslim who prays but refuses zakat has kept half the pillar and dropped the other half.

And Allah describes zakat as purification, not loss: "Take, [O Muhammad], from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase" (Quran 9:103, in meaning).

خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ صَدَقَةًۭ تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ ۖ إِنَّ صَلَوٰتَكَ سَكَنٌۭ لَّهُمْ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ

Quran 9:103 — "Take from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase."

Two words carry the whole meaning: tutahhiruhum — you purify them — and tuzakkihim — you cause them increase. The wealth is cleansed of greed, the heart of attachment, and what is given does not shrink the giver. When the Prophet ﷺ sent Mu'adh ibn Jabal (RA) to Yemen, he told him to teach the people that zakat "is to be taken from the wealthy among them and given to the poor" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1395, in meaning).

2.Who Must Pay: The Nisab and One Lunar Year

Zakat is not due on every Muslim and not due on every coin. It is due when wealth reaches a threshold and stays there for a full lunar year. The threshold is called the nisab, and the year is called the hawl. The Prophet ﷺ fixed the nisab in gold and silver: "When you possess two hundred dirhams and one year passes on them, five dirhams are payable. Nothing is incumbent on you, that is, on gold, till it reaches twenty dinars. When you possess twenty dinars and one year passes on them, half a dinar is payable. Whatever exceeds, that will be reckoned properly" (Sunan Abi Dawud 1573, graded Sahih by Al-Albani).

Half a dinar out of twenty is exactly one-fortieth — 2.5%. That is the rate, and it has never changed. In modern weights, twenty dinars is about 85 grams of pure gold, and two hundred dirhams is about 595 grams of silver. The nisab applies to wealth that can grow: cash, gold and silver, goods kept for trade, and investments. The house you live in, the car you drive and the tools of your trade are not zakatable — they are your needs. Debts you owe are subtracted first, because zakat is due on what you truly own.

Each person has their own zakat date: the day, one lunar year ago, when their wealth first reached the nisab. Zakat is due on that date every year, not only in Ramadan. Because the gold and silver nisabs give different amounts today, many scholars advise using the lower — the silver nisab — so more people give and more poor people receive. Above it for a full year, pay 2.5%; below both, nothing is due, and whatever you give is sadaqah.

3.Who Receives It: The Eight Groups of Quran 9:60

Allah did not leave the recipients to our guesswork. He named them, all eight, in a single verse: "Zakah expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to administer [zakah] and for bringing hearts together [for Islam] and for freeing captives and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the [stranded] traveller — an obligation [imposed] by Allah. And Allah is Knowing and Wise" (Quran 9:60, in meaning).

إِنَّمَا ٱلصَّدَقَٰتُ لِلْفُقَرَآءِ وَٱلْمَسَٰكِينِ وَٱلْعَٰمِلِينَ عَلَيْهَا وَٱلْمُؤَلَّفَةِ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَفِى ٱلرِّقَابِ وَٱلْغَٰرِمِينَ وَفِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱبْنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ ۖ فَرِيضَةًۭ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ

Quran 9:60 — "Zakah expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy..."

The verse begins with innama — "only". The first two are the poor (fuqara) and the needy (masakin): the person who has almost nothing, and the person who has something but not enough. Then its collectors, those whose hearts are to be won, those in bondage to be freed, those crushed by debt, those striving in Allah's cause, and the stranded traveller. And the Quran adds: "And in their wealth is a right for the one who asks and the one who is deprived" (Quran 51:19, in meaning). Zakat is not your generosity to the poor. It is their right in your wealth — a right the poor person claims, not begs for. That is why scholars explain that zakat is not given to the people you must already support — your parents, your children, your spouse — because their maintenance is your duty already. It is given outward, as ownership: the recipient must own it and spend it as they choose. For what voluntary charity looks like beside this obligation, read The Virtue of Charity (Sadaqah): Giving That Never Decreases Wealth.

4.The Promise: Wealth That Grows When You Give

Every other transaction works the same way: what you give away, you no longer have. Zakat reverses that arithmetic: "The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah is like a seed [of grain] which grows seven spikes; in each spike is a hundred grains. And Allah multiplies [His reward] for whom He wills. And Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing" (Quran 2:261, in meaning). One grain becomes seven hundred — the only investment whose return the Quran guarantees.

"The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah is like a seed which grows seven spikes; in each spike is a hundred grains." — Quran 2:261, in meaning

The promise is not only for the Hereafter. Allah says, "Whatever you spend of anything, He will replace it, and He is the best of providers" (Quran 34:39, in meaning). And He describes the givers as those who "spend their wealth by night and by day, secretly and openly" and "will have their reward with their Lord, and no fear will there be concerning them, nor will they grieve" (Quran 2:274, in meaning). A man gives 2.5% and keeps 97.5% — and the 97.5% that remains is now pure, blessed, and protected. The giver has not lost 2.5%; he has purified 100%.

5.The Warning: What Happens to Hoarded Wealth

The Quran and the Hadith are just as clear about the other side: hoarded wealth does not stay quiet. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Whoever is made wealthy by Allah and does not pay the Zakat of his wealth, then on the Day of Resurrection his wealth will be made like a bald-headed poisonous male snake with two black spots over the eyes. The snake will encircle his neck and bite his cheeks and say, 'I am your wealth, I am your treasure'" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1403, in meaning). Then he recited Quran 3:180, in meaning: "Let not those who withhold what Allah has given them of His bounty think it is better for them; rather, it is worse for them."

Muslim adds the same warning for gold and silver: "If any owner of gold or silver does not pay what is due on him, plates of fire would be beaten out for him, heated in the fire of Hell, and his sides, forehead and back cauterised with them" (Sahih Muslim 987, in meaning). And the Quran warns those who pile up gold and silver and refuse to spend it in Allah's way that it will be heated in the Fire and used to brand their foreheads, sides and backs (Quran 9:34-35, in meaning). Wealth is a trust, and a trust kept from its rightful owners turns against its keeper. The choice is between giving 2.5% willingly now, and having it taken from you unwillingly later. 6.Zakat and Sadaqah: Two Different Doors

People often mix up zakat and sadaqah, and the difference matters. Zakat is obligatory, fixed at 2.5%, due once a lunar year on wealth above the nisab, and payable only to the eight groups of Quran 9:60. Sadaqah is voluntary: any amount, at any time, to anyone in need — a smile is sadaqah, removing harm from the road is sadaqah, and a coin in a poor person's hand is sadaqah. If your wealth is below the nisab, you owe no zakat — but the door of sadaqah is still open, and even a date given sincerely is accepted. If your wealth is above the nisab, zakat is the floor, not the ceiling: you pay the 2.5% because Allah commanded it, and then you give sadaqah on top of it because your heart wants to. And neither replaces the other: sadaqah, however generous, does not discharge the zakat you owe. For the money zakat must never touch, read Sood Se Paak Banking: Pakistan's Riba-Free Future — wealth must also be earned without interest, because what is unlawful cannot be purified by giving part of it away.

7.Paying Your Zakat This Year: Five Practical Steps

Here is a short plan to pay it properly this year.

1. Fix your zakat date — the Hijri date your wealth first reached the nisab — and calculate on it every year. If you do not know it, choose one, such as the 1st of Ramadan, and keep it.

2. Count everything zakatable: cash, bank balances, gold and silver, trade goods, and money owed to you — minus what you owe to others.

3. Check the nisab: the value of 85 grams of gold or 595 grams of silver on your zakat date. Above the lower, zakat is due; below both, nothing is due.

4. Pay 2.5% to the right people: the poor and needy first, as money they own — not a loan, and not a purchase you choose for them. Give locally, and give early in the year.

5. Make the dua of the giver: O Allah, accept this from me, purify what remains, and put barakah in it for me and my family. And let your children watch you pay it, so zakat is a habit they grew up seeing, not a strange duty.

Zakat is the third pillar because wealth is the third test: faith by what you believe, prayer by what you do with your time, zakat by what you do with your money. Pay it, and your wealth is purified, your heart is lightened, and eight groups of people are lifted by your hand. That is what 2.5% buys: not less wealth, but wealth Allah has accepted.
