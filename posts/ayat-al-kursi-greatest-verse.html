Ayat al-Kursi: The Greatest Verse of the Quran

R Razia Azeem · 20 Oct, 2026

Every Muslim child grows up hearing it, and most of us have recited it a thousand times. Ayat al-Kursi — the 255th verse of Surah Al-Baqarah — is, in the words of the Prophet ﴺ, the greatest verse in the entire Quran. Here is what the verse means, the remarkable promises attached to reciting it, and how to make it a living part of your day.

Quran

For centuries, this verse has been the first long verse many children memorize, and the verse people reach for in moments of fear. Ayat al-Kursi, the 255th verse of Surah Al-Baqarah, is known as the Throne Verse — because it describes the Kursi, the majestic dominion of Allah.

اللَّهُ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ ۚ لَّهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۗ مَن ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِندَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ ۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِّنْ عِلْمِهِ إِلَّا بِمَا شَاءَ ۚ وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ ۖ وَلَا يَؤُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْعَظِيمُ

Quran 2:255 — Ayat al-Kursi

Translation of the meaning: “Allah! There is no god but Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of all existence. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission? He knows what is before them and what will be after them, and they encompass not a thing of His knowledge except for what He wills. His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth, and their preservation tires Him not. And He is the Most High, the Most Great.”

1. What Is Ayat al-Kursi?

Ayat al-Kursi is the 255th verse of Surah Al-Baqarah, the longest surah of the Quran. It is called the “Throne Verse” because it describes Allah’s Kursi — His majestic dominion that extends over the heavens and the earth. It contains no command and no story; it is pure affirmation of who Allah is.

It opens with two of Allah’s most beautiful names, Al-Hayy (the Ever-Living) and Al-Qayyum (the Self-Subsisting Sustainer who sustains all existence while needing nothing) — from the Asma’ al-Husna, which Allah commands us to call Him by (Quran 20:8).

2. The Greatest Verse in the Quran

Ubayy ibn Ka‘b (RA), a great companion and master reciter of the Quran, narrated that the Prophet ﴺ asked him: “O Abu al-Mundhir! Do you know which verse of Allah’s Book is the greatest?” Ubayy replied: “Allah and His Messenger know best.” Asked again, he said: “Allah, there is no god but He, the Ever-Living, the Self-Subsisting” — Ayat al-Kursi. The Prophet ﴺ struck him on the chest and said: “May knowledge be delightful to you, O Abu al-Mundhir!” (Sahih Muslim 810).

Of more than six thousand verses, the Prophet ﴺ singled out this one as the greatest of all — because its subject is Allah Himself, in His perfection.

3. A Shield Through the Night

Abu Hurayrah (RA) narrated that he was once guarding the zakat of Ramadan when a man came by night and stole from the food three nights running. On the third night the man offered advice: “When you go to bed, recite Ayat al-Kursi from beginning to end. Allah will appoint a guardian over you, and no devil will approach you until morning.” When Abu Hurayrah told the Prophet ﴺ, he said: “He told you the truth, although he is a habitual liar. Do you know whom you were talking to? That was Satan.” (Sahih al-Bukhari 2311).

This is why the verse has travelled from parent to child for generations: recited before sleep, it places the reciter under Allah’s guardianship through the night.

4. After Every Prayer: Nothing Between You and Paradise but Death

Nor do the rewards stop at night. The Prophet ﴺ said: “Whoever recites Ayatul Kursi after every obligatory prayer, nothing will prevent him from entering Paradise except death.” This hadith was reported by Al-Nasa’i, graded sahih by Ibn Hibban, and likewise graded sahih by Al-Albani.

“Whoever recites Ayatul Kursi after every obligatory prayer, nothing will prevent him from entering Paradise except death.”
— Reported by Al-Nasa’i; graded sahih by Ibn Hibban and Al-Albani

Think about the exchange: thirty seconds after each prayer — a verse you may already know by heart — against a promise this vast. Scholars note it is for the one who maintains this practice alongside his obligations; still, the promise’s generosity is reason enough to start today.

5. Making It Part of Your Day

With these promises, Ayat al-Kursi fits naturally into four moments of the day:

  • After every fard prayer — the direct route to the Paradise promise above.
  • Before sleep — the Bukhari promise of a guardian through the night.
  • In the morning and evening adhkar — it is a fixed part of the prophetic remembrances in Hisnul Muslim.
  • With your children — often the first long verse they are taught.

6. The Meaning That Changes You: He Never Sleeps

Consider one phrase: “Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep.” Everything in creation sleeps — you, your guardians, even kings. The One watching over you never closes His eyes. When fear comes at night, the One on guard is Al-Hayy, Al-Qayyum, the Ever-Living who sustains all existence.

And “His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth”: nothing in your life is outside His dominion. To go deeper into the names this verse opens with — Al-Hayy and Al-Qayyum — see The Beautiful Names of Allah. Start tonight: recite it before you sleep, and tomorrow after Fajr.
