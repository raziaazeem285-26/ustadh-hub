Prophet Adam (AS): The First Human and the First Repentance

R Razia Azeem · 14 Nov, 2026

Before there was any human history, there was one man, made from clay and taught by Allah. His story is our story: the fall, the repentance, and the mercy that followed.

Seerah & Stories

Before there was a single city, a single war, a single book of history, there was one man. Allah made him with His own hands from clay, breathed into him of His spirit, taught him names the angels did not know, and commanded the angels to bow before him. Then that same man made the first mistake in human history — and was taught the first repentance. His story is not ancient history. It is our story, told first so every generation would know two things: human beings can fall, and the door back to Allah is never closed.

1.Created from Clay, Honoured Above the Angels

Allah announced His plan before Adam (AS) existed: "And [mention, O Muhammad], when your Lord said to the angels, 'Indeed, I will make upon the earth a successive authority'" (Quran 2:30, in meaning). The angels questioned it, and Allah answered, "Indeed, I know that which you do not know." Humanity was not an accident. It was a decision, made before the first human being, for a purpose the angels could not yet see.

The Quran names the stages: dust (turab, 3:59), clay (tin, 6:2), altered black mud (hama' masnun), and dried clay like pottery (salsal, 55:14). "And We did certainly create man out of clay from an altered black mud" (Quran 15:26, in meaning).

وَلَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مِن صَلْصَٰلٍۢ مِّنْ حَمَإٍۢ مَّسْنُونٍۢ

Quran 15:26 — "And We did certainly create man out of clay from an altered black mud."

Then came the honour no other creature received: "So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My [created] soul, then fall down to him in prostration" (Quran 15:29, in meaning). Allah breathed into Adam of His spirit — not a part of Allah, but a spirit created and honoured by Him — and the clay became a living, knowing human being. Then Allah taught him what the angels did not know: "And He taught Adam the names — all of them" (Quran 2:31, in meaning). Knowledge was the first gift, and the reason for the honour that followed. A narration in Jami' at-Tirmidhi (2955, sahih) says Allah made Adam from a handful of dust taken from the whole earth, so his children came in the earth's colours. And the Prophet ﷺ said, "Allah created Adam, making him 60 cubits tall" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3326). The first human being was made from the lowest of materials and given the highest of honours: "And We have certainly honoured the children of Adam" (Quran 17:70, in meaning).

2.The Command to Prostrate — and the One Who Refused

When Adam was complete, the command came: "And [mention] when We said to the angels, 'Prostrate before Adam'; so they prostrated, except for Iblis. He refused and was arrogant and became of the disbelievers" (Quran 2:34, in meaning). It was a prostration of honour, not worship — worship belongs to Allah alone — and every angel obeyed at once. Only one being refused — "He was of the jinn, and he departed from the command of his Lord" (Quran 18:50). He was a jinn who had risen among the angels by his worship — and in one moment he lost everything.

His answer is the first sentence of arrogance in history: "I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay" (Quran 7:12, in meaning). He compared himself to Adam, decided he was better, and argued with Allah's command. The first sin in history was not eating from a tree. It was pride. That is why the Prophet ﷺ warned, "No one will enter Paradise who has an atom's weight of arrogance in his heart" (Sahih Muslim 91, in meaning). Expelled, he asked for time until the Day of Resurrection — and it was granted (Quran 7:14-15). Then he made his vow: "Because You have put me in error, I will surely sit in wait for them on Your straight path" (Quran 7:16, in meaning). He blamed Allah for his own choice; Adam blamed himself. For the humility that is the opposite of this disease, read Humility (Tawadu'): Strength in Lowering Yourself Before Allah.

3.A Home in Paradise — and One Forbidden Tree

After the prostration, Allah gave Adam a home and a companion: "And We said, 'O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in Paradise and eat therefrom in [ease and] abundance from wherever you will. But do not approach this tree, lest you be among the wrongdoers'" (Quran 2:35, in meaning). The Quran does not name the tree — the point was never the tree, but the test: would the human being obey one clear command, freely, in the middle of abundance?

His wife was created for companionship: "O mankind, fear your Lord, who created you from one soul and created from it its mate" (Quran 4:1), "that he might find comfort in her" (Quran 7:189). The Quran does not name Hawwa, but the hadith identifies her, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "Treat women kindly, for woman was created from a rib" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5186; Sahih Muslim 1468, in meaning). And the warning came before the test: "O Adam, indeed this is an enemy to you and to your wife" (Quran 20:117). Adam was warned who his enemy was and what it would cost. He was not caught unprepared — the enemy did not attack with force, but with a whisper.

4.The Whisper and the Fall

The Quran shows his method in detail so we can recognise it: he did not say, "Disobey Allah." He said something sweeter: "But Satan whispered to them to make apparent to them that which was concealed from them of their private parts. He said, 'Your Lord did not forbid you this tree except that you become angels or become of the immortal'" (Quran 7:20, in meaning). It promised what Adam did not have — angelhood, eternity — and made the one forbidden thing look like the door to it. Then he swore by Allah: "Indeed, I am to you from among the sincere advisors" (Quran 7:21).

"So he made them fall, through deception. And when they tasted of the tree, their private parts became apparent to them, and they began to fasten together over themselves from the leaves of Paradise" (Quran 7:22). Their covering was gone. "And Adam disobeyed his Lord and erred" (Quran 20:121, in meaning). The Quran does not soften it, but it gives the reason, gently: "And We had already taken a promise from Adam before, but he forgot; and We found not in him determination" (Quran 20:115, in meaning). He forgot — he was not stubborn or defiant like Iblis — and the moment he remembered, everything changed.

5.The First Repentance — and the Dua Every Believer Knows

They made no excuses. They said one sentence — the most beautiful dua of repentance in the Quran: "They said, 'Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers'" (Quran 7:23, in meaning).

قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ

Quran 7:23 — "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers."

Three parts in one sentence: admission, request, hope. And the answer came at once: "Then Adam received from his Lord [some] words, and He accepted his repentance" (Quran 2:37). The Prophet ﷺ said, "Every son of Adam commits sin, and the best of those who commit sin are those who repent" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2499, hasan, in meaning).

"Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers." — Quran 7:23

That is the model for every repentance after it: admit, ask, hope — and Allah accepts. For the full teaching, read Repentance (Tawbah): The Door That Never Closes. His repentance was accepted first; the descent was the plan all along (Quran 2:30).

6.The Descent: Earth as a Testing Ground

"We said, 'Go down, [all of you], as enemies to one another, and you will have upon the earth a place of settlement and provision for a time'" (Quran 2:36, in meaning). All three descended — human beings and their enemy, on the same earth until an appointed time. But the descent came with a promise: "Then when guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows My guidance — there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve" (Quran 2:38). Earth is not a prison; it is a testing ground with guidance.

Adam lived on as the first prophet to his own children. And his family saw the first tragedy: his two sons, one whose offering Allah accepted and one whose He did not, and the second killed the first out of envy (Quran 5:27-31). The first murder was born of the same disease — "why him and not me?" For that disease and its cure, read Envy (Hasad): The Hidden Disease That Eats Good Deeds. A narration in Jami' at-Tirmidhi (3076, hasan sahih) tells us Adam was shown his descendants, saw Dawud (AS) whose life was short, and gave him forty years from his own life — then forgot, and denied it. "Adam forgot, and his offspring forgot." "Indeed, the example of Jesus to Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust; then He said to him, 'Be,' and he was" (Quran 3:59, in meaning).

7.Five Lessons from Adam's Story for Your Life Today

1. Your worth comes from Allah, not from what you are made of. Dust is the lowest of materials, and Allah honoured it above the angels — your value is that He breathed into you of His spirit and taught you.

2. Watch for the words "I am better than him." It still starts most sins — arguments, envy, refusals to apologise. Hear it in your own heart, and you have found the whisper.

3. The enemy promises what you do not have. "You will become angels, you will live forever." The whisper points at what is missing and hides what will be lost. When a temptation promises everything, ask what it costs — Adam's cost was Paradise itself.

4. Repent the way Adam repented: at once, without excuses. Say the dua of Quran 7:23 tonight and mean it. The door that opened for the first human being is still open for you.

5. Earth is the test, not the home. The promise of Quran 2:38 stands: whoever follows the guidance has no fear and no grief. Live like a traveller who knows the way home.

Adam (AS) fell once, repented once, and Allah accepted him. We will fall many times. The question is whether, like our father Adam, we turn back — immediately, honestly, with the words Allah Himself taught us.
