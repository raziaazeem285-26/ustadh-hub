Surah Al-Fatiha Ki Fazilat | Quran Ki Sab Se Azeem Surah | Islamic Reminder #Shorts

R Razia Azeem · 15 Nov, 2026

Surah Al-Fatiha Ki Fazilat — the Prophet ﷺ called Surah Al-Fatiha the greatest surah in the Qur'an. Seven short verses that every Muslim recites in every rak'ah of every prayer.

In this article
  1. About this video
  2. Key reflections
  3. Watch & share

1.About this video

This short is about seven verses — the greatest surah in the Quran. The Prophet ﷺ said to Abu Sa'id ibn al-Mu'alla: "I will teach you a surah which is the greatest surah in the Qur'an." Then he said: "Alhamdu lillahi Rabbil-'Alamin — it is the Seven Oft-Repeated Verses and the Glorious Qur'an that I have been given" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4474, in meaning). Seven short verses that every Muslim recites in every rak'ah of every prayer — the Mother of the Book, the Seven Oft-Repeated Verses, and a conversation between the servant and his Lord. "And We have certainly given you seven of the often repeated [verses] and the great Qur'an" (Quran 15:87, in meaning).

The video walks through why Al-Fatiha holds this rank. Allah says in a hadith qudsi: "I have divided the prayer between Myself and My servant into two halves. When the servant says 'Alhamdu lillahi Rabbil-'Alamin', Allah says: My servant has praised Me... and My servant shall have what he asks" (Sahih Muslim 395, in meaning) — every verse of Al-Fatiha is answered, verse by verse, in the prayer itself. And there is a warning with the promise: "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book" (Sahih al-Bukhari 756, in meaning). Then the glad tidings Jibril brought: "Rejoice in two lights given to you that no prophet before you was given: the Opening of the Book and the last verses of Surah Al-Baqarah. You will not recite a letter of them without being granted it" (Sahih Muslim 806, in meaning). And Al-Fatiha is healing as well: a companion recited it over a man stung by a scorpion and he was cured, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "How did you know it is a ruqyah?" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5736, in meaning).

2.Key reflections

Think about what this means for a normal day. You recite Al-Fatiha at least seventeen times a day in the obligatory prayers — and every single time, Allah answers you verse by verse. The greatest surah in the Quran is not a surah you read once a year; it is the surah on your tongue in every prayer, and the conversation in it is live: you praise, He answers; you ask for guidance, He gives it. Most of us rush through it as a habit, and never stop to hear the answers. The lesson of this short is simple: slow down in Al-Fatiha. Say "Alhamdu lillahi Rabbil-'Alamin" and know that Allah has just said, "My servant has praised Me." Say "Guide us to the straight path" and know that the answer is already given. And when someone is ill, recite it over them with the same faith as the companion who cured the stung man — because the Prophet ﷺ called it a ruqyah, a healing. For the full article on this surah, read Surah Al-Fatiha: The Greatest Surah of the Quran, and for the two surahs of refuge that go with it at night, read Al-Mu'awwidhatayn: The Two Surahs of Refuge.

3.Watch & share

Watch the full video above, and then recite Al-Fatiha slowly in your next prayer — one verse at a time, listening for the answer Allah gives to each one. Then share this video with someone who prays five times a day and has never been told that the surah on their tongue is the greatest surah in the Quran. For the two lights that go with it — the Opening of the Book and the last verses of Surah Al-Baqarah — read Last Two Verses of Surah Al-Baqarah: Enough for the Night, and find more reminders on the blog.
