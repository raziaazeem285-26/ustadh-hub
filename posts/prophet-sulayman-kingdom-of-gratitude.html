Prophet Sulayman (AS): A Kingdom of Gratitude

R Razia Azeem · 9 Nov, 2026

A king commanded the wind, the jinn and the birds — yet when he heard a tiny ant speak, he smiled, and his first prayer was gratitude.

Seerah & Stories

Imagine a king whose army marches in three ranks at once: jinn, men, and birds. A king who understands birds, whose orders the wind obeys, for whom the jinn raise palaces. Now imagine that same king stopping his whole army because of one small voice on the ground — and smiling. This is Prophet Sulayman (peace be upon him), the son of Dawud. His story is not really about power at all — it is about what a person does with power when Allah gives it to him.

His story runs through Surah An-Naml (27), Saba (34), Al-Anbiya (21), and Sad (38) — one portrait of a prophet-king who had everything, and whose first instinct, every time, was gratitude.

1.The Son Who Inherited a Prophet's Kingdom

Sulayman was the son of Prophet Dawud (peace be upon him) — the shepherd king whose story we told yesterday. The Quran says: "And We gave knowledge to Dawud and Sulayman, and they said, 'Praise be to Allah, who has favoured us over many of His believing servants.' And Sulayman inherited Dawud" (Quran 27:15-16). Notice what is inherited first: knowledge. The kingdom came after it, and the Quran names knowledge before the throne.

The inheritance was not only a crown. "And Sulayman inherited Dawud, and he said, 'O people, we have been taught the language of birds, and we have been given from all things. Indeed, this is a clear favour'" (Quran 27:16). He called it what it was: a favour from Allah.

Before he was a king, Sulayman was a judge beside his father. When a case about a damaged field came before them both, the Quran says: "We gave Sulayman understanding of it. And to each [of them] We gave judgement and knowledge" (Quran 21:78-79, in meaning). And it frames his whole life in one line: "And We gave to Dawud, Sulayman, an excellent servant. Indeed, he was one repeatedly turning back [to Allah]" (Quran 38:30). Excellent, not because of his kingdom, but because he kept turning back to Allah.

2.The Valley of the Ants — A King's Gratitude

The most famous scene in Sulayman's story is also the smallest. "And gathered for Sulayman were his soldiers of the jinn and men and birds, and they were [marching] in rows. Until, when they came upon the valley of the ants, an ant said, 'O ants, enter your dwellings that Sulayman and his soldiers may not crush you while they perceive not'" (Quran 27:17-18). An army marching in perfect order — and one ant, warning her people to get out of the way.

Sulayman heard her — and he did not laugh, ignore her, or order the march on. "So [Sulayman] smiled, amused at her speech, and said, 'My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favour which You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents and to do righteousness of which You approve. And admit me by Your mercy into [the ranks of] Your righteous servants'" (Quran 27:19). A king who can hear an ant, and whose response is a prayer.

Look at the order: gratitude first, good deeds second, and the final hope placed in Allah's mercy, not in his own record.

رَبِّ أَوْزِعْنِي أَنْ أَشْكُرَ نِعْمَتَكَ الَّتِي أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَالِدَيَّ وَأَنْ أَعْمَلَ صَالِحًا تَرْضَاهُ وَأَدْخِلْنِي بِرَحْمَتِكَ فِي عِبَادِكَ الصَّالِحِينَ

Quran 27:19

3.The Hoopoe's News and the Queen of Sheba

One day Sulayman took attendance of the birds and found one missing: the hoopoe. "And he took attendance of the birds and said, 'Why do I not see the hoopoe — or is he among the absent?'" (Quran 27:20). A king who notices one missing bird. The hoopoe returned with news: "I have encompassed [in knowledge] that which you have not encompassed, and I have come to you from Sheba with certain news" (Quran 27:22). A woman ruled there, given of all things, with a great throne — "I found her and her people prostrating to the sun instead of Allah" (Quran 27:23-24).

Think about this: the most powerful king on earth is told something he does not know — by a bird. He does not dismiss it; he investigates, and sends a letter that begins the way every good message should: "Indeed, it is from Sulayman, and indeed, it reads: 'In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful. Be not haughty with me but come to me in submission [to Allah]'" (Quran 27:30-31). No threats, no demand for tribute — an invitation to stop worshipping the sun and submit to its Creator.

The queen — Bilqis, as she is known in the Islamic tradition — consulted her council, sent gifts first to test him, and when they were refused, she came herself. Then Sulayman asked his assembly: "O assembly [of jinn], which of you will bring me her throne before they come to me in submission?" One who had knowledge of the Scripture said, "I will bring it to you before your glance returns to you" (Quran 27:38-40, in meaning). The Quran does not name this person. The throne was there, in the blink of an eye, disguised so the queen could be tested on whether she would recognise it. She did.

Then came the palace with a floor of smooth glass. She was told to enter, thought it was water, and uncovered her shins to wade through. "He said, 'Indeed, it is a palace [whose floor is] made smooth with glass.' She said, 'My Lord, indeed I have wronged myself, and I submit with Sulayman to Allah, Lord of the worlds'" (Quran 27:44). A queen who worshipped the sun walked into a palace of glass and walked out a believer. Sulayman had not conquered her kingdom — he had invited her heart, and the invitation worked because it was carried with wisdom, not force.

4.Wind, Jinn and Copper — Power Held by Permission

What did Sulayman's kingdom look like? "And to Sulayman [We subjected] the wind — its morning [journey] was a month, and its afternoon [journey] was a month — and We made flow for him a spring of [liquid] copper. And among the jinn were those who worked for him by the permission of his Lord" (Quran 34:12). A month's journey in a morning. And the jinn: "They made for him whatever he willed of arches, images, basins as large as reservoirs, and cooking cauldrons fixed [in place]" (Quran 34:13).

"And of the devils were those who dived for him and did other work besides that. And We were watching over them" (Quran 21:82). And the wind: "We subjected to him the wind, blowing gently by his command wherever he wished" (Quran 38:36) — "proceeding by his command toward the land which We blessed" (Quran 21:81).

Notice the phrase that repeats: by the permission of his Lord. The wind obeyed Allah, for Sulayman; the jinn worked by Allah's permission. His kingdom's difference from every other empire: its king knew none of it was his — a trust, held by permission.

5.The Trial and the Dua: Forgiveness First

Even Sulayman was tried. "And We certainly tried Sulayman and placed on his throne a [mere] body; then he turned back [to Allah]" (Quran 38:34). The Quran does not describe the body further, and the commentators differ over what it was — what the verse is certain about is the response: he turned back.

Just before it, the Quran describes fine horses displayed before him one evening (Quran 38:31-33) — the commentators differ over that scene too, so we will not build a lesson on what is disputed. What is undisputed is the dua that follows the trial: "He said, 'My Lord, forgive me and grant me a kingdom that will not belong to anyone after me. Indeed, You are the Bestower'" (Quran 38:35).

"My Lord, forgive me and grant me a kingdom that will not belong to anyone after me. Indeed, You are the Bestower." — Quran 38:35

Forgiveness first — before the biggest request of his life, he asks to be forgiven, coming to Allah with his need, not his record, and ending with the Name that fits it: Al-Wahhab, the Bestower.

قَالَ رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي وَهَبْ لِي مُلْكًا لَا يَنْبَغِي لِأَحَدٍ مِنْ بَعْدِي ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْوَهَّابُ

Quran 38:35

Allah granted it — the wind, the jinn, the kingdom unlike any other. And the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) told us more: when Sulayman finished building Bayt al-Maqdis, the Sacred House in Jerusalem, he asked Allah for three things — judgement that matched Allah's judgement, a kingdom no one after him would have, and that whoever comes to that House wanting only to pray there should leave free of sin, as the day his mother bore him. The first two were given; the Prophet added his hope that the third was granted too (Sunan an-Nasa'i 693, graded sahih by Al-Albani). A king who had everything asked, at the end, for the forgiveness of strangers who would pray in a mosque he built.

6.The Death No One Noticed — and Five Lessons

Sulayman's death is the Quran's quietest rebuke to every empire. "And when We decreed for Sulayman death, nothing indicated to them his death except a creature of the earth eating his staff. But when he fell, the jinn became evident [to all] that if they had known the unseen, they would not have remained in humiliating torment" (Quran 34:14). He died leaning on his staff, and the jinn kept working, not knowing their master was gone, until a termite ate through the staff and he fell. The creatures who seemed to know the unseen did not even know the king was dead.

That is where his story ends. Five lessons to carry from the kingdom of Sulayman:

First, gratitude before asking. On hearing an ant, Sulayman's first prayer was for the ability to be grateful (Quran 27:19); his greatest dua began with forgiveness (Quran 38:35). Before asking Allah for more, thank Him for what is already in your hands.

Second, power is a trust, never a possession. The wind, the jinn, the birds — all "by the permission of his Lord" (Quran 34:12). Whatever Allah has put in your hands is held the same way.

Third, listen to small voices. A king heard an ant and took a bird's report seriously.

Fourth, invite with wisdom, not force. Sulayman sent a letter in the name of Allah (Quran 27:30-31), not an army — and the queen came, saw, and submitted. Read more in our story of Prophet Musa, from the river to the sea.

Fifth, death humbles every kingdom. The jinn worked on, not knowing their king had died (Quran 34:14). What outlasts a kingdom is only what was built for Allah.

Tell this story to your children tonight — the ant, the hoopoe, the glass palace, the queen who found the truth. See our free trial class for young learners, and the story of Sulayman's father, Prophet Dawud, whose mountains sang.

Sulayman had the greatest kingdom in history, and the Quran remembers him for two prayers: one for gratitude, one that began with forgiveness. That is the real inheritance he left — not the wind, the jinn, or the throne, but a way of asking. Ask like that, and you will find the Bestower is still giving.
