10 Tips to Get Maximum Marks from Online Classes

Online classes only work if you work them. These 10 field-tested habits turn screen time into board-exam marks — starting from your very next class.

Students who top board exams from online classes don't have better internet — they have better habits. If you're taking online tuition for Matric, FSc, or O/A Levels, these 10 tips will squeeze every mark out of every session.

1.Treat it like a real classroom

Join on time, dressed, sitting upright — not lying on the bed in pajamas. This sounds small, but your brain takes cues from your body. Students who show up to online class with the same discipline as school consistently retain more. Log in five minutes early so a slow connection never costs you the first five minutes of the lesson.

2.Fix one study space

Choose one spot in the house — a desk, a corner of the dining table — and make it your class zone. Keep your books, notebook, and charger there permanently. Tell your family your class timings so nobody walks in mid-lesson. A fixed space builds a fixed habit, and it signals to everyone at home that this class matters.

3.Keep your camera on

Camera-off students drift: they check WhatsApp, zone out, or leave the room. With the camera on, your tutor can see confusion on your face and slow down — the same way a classroom teacher does. It also keeps you honest. If your tutor allows cameras off, turn yours on anyway. Accountability is free.

4.Prepare for 10 minutes before class

Before each session, spend ten minutes skimming the topic: read the chapter headings, look at the diagrams, and write down two questions you already have. This turns you from a passive listener into an active participant. Students who pre-read understand roughly twice as much in class because their brain has "hooks" to hang new information on.

5.Ask questions live, not later

The chat box is your best friend. The moment something confuses you, type the question or unmute and ask. Shy students who never speak in school often ask more online — use that. A question asked live gets answered in thirty seconds; the same doubt left for "later" usually never gets cleared and shows up in the exam.

The students who gain the most from online classes are not the smartest ones — they are the ones who ask the most questions and revise the same day.

6.Take handwritten notes

Don't just rely on the tutor's slides or the class recording. Write key points by hand during class — formulas, definitions, diagrams, and anything the tutor repeats twice. Writing forces your brain to process, not just record. After class, spend five minutes highlighting the three most important points of the day.

7.Practice the same day

Memory of a new concept fades fast: without revision, you lose most of it within 24 hours. Do 20–30 minutes of practice the same evening — solve the examples your tutor did, attempt the exercise questions, or re-do the quiz. This single habit separates A-grade students from average ones more than any other.

8.Make past papers your routine

For Pakistani board exams and O/A Levels, the paper pattern repeats. Solve past papers topic-by-topic as you finish each chapter, then full papers under timed conditions in the last two months. Ask your tutor to mark them strictly — board-style marking, not generous marking. AI tools can generate extra practice questions on your weak topics between sessions.

9.Review recordings smartly

One of online learning's biggest advantages is the recording — but rewatching full classes is a time trap. Instead, note timestamps of the parts you didn't fully understand and rewatch only those sections at 1.25x or 1.5x speed before the next class. Ten focused minutes beats an hour of passive replay.

10.Track progress every week

Keep a simple notebook or phone note: topics completed, quiz scores, and one line on what still feels weak. Share it with your tutor monthly and with your parents too. Students who track their own progress improve faster because they stop fooling themselves about "almost understanding" a topic. Ask your tutor for a short weekly test — even ten questions — and watch the trend line.

Final thoughts

Online classes give you the tutor, the recordings, and the flexibility — but the marks come from your habits. Start with any three of these tips this week, add more as they become routine, and your next test scores will show the difference.
