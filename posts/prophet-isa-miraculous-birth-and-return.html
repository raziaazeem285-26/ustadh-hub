Prophet Isa (AS): The Miraculous Birth and the Promised Return

R Razia Azeem · 14 Nov, 2026

A baby who spoke in the cradle, a prophet who healed the blind and raised the dead by Allah's leave — and a return the world still awaits. The Quran's account of Isa, son of Maryam.

  2. The Annunciation: A Word from Allah
  3. Born Under a Palm Tree
  4. The Baby Who Spoke
  5. Miracles by Allah's Leave

Seerah & Stories

Of all the prophets, only one was born without a father, spoke in his cradle, and was raised to Allah without dying. His name is Isa, son of Maryam — Jesus, in the language of the Gospels — and the Quran tells his story across fifteen surahs, naming him twenty-five times. For a Muslim, his story is not a borrowed one. It is the Quran's own account: a prophet honoured in this world and the Hereafter, a servant of Allah, and a sign of the Hour whose return the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) promised.

1.A Mother's Vow: Maryam Before Isa

Maryam's mother vowed the child in her womb to Allah's service, and when the child was a girl she named her Maryam (Quran 3:35-36). She grew up under Zakariya's care, who found her provided with food from Allah whenever he visited her chamber (Quran 3:37).

Maryam is the only woman named in the Quran, and the Quran gives her an honour no other woman receives: "O Mary, indeed Allah has chosen you and purified you and chosen you above the women of the worlds" (Quran 3:42). The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "There is none born among the offspring of Adam, but Satan touches it. A child therefore cries loudly at the time of birth because of the touch of Satan, except Mary and her child" — then he recited, "And I seek refuge with You for her and for her offspring from the outcast Satan" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3431; Quran 3:36).

Her story matters because the Quran's account of Isa is inseparable from her. He is never named without her: always "Isa, son of Maryam" — a lineage through his mother alone.

2.The Annunciation: A Word from Allah

Maryam withdrew to a place in the east, and the angel Jibril came to her in the form of a man. "I am only a messenger of your Lord, to give you a pure boy," he said (Quran 19:16-19). "How shall I have a son," she asked, "seeing that no man has touched me?" (Quran 19:20).

The angels had already given her the answer: "[Remember] when the angels said, 'O Mary, indeed Allah gives you good tidings of a word from Him, whose name will be the Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, held in honour in this world and the Hereafter and of those nearest to Allah'" (Quran 3:45). "He will speak to the people in the cradle and in maturity and will be of the righteous" (Quran 3:46).

إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَىٰ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ ءَادَمَ ۖ خَلَقَهُۥ مِن تُرَابٍۢ ثُمَّ قَالَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ

Quran 3:59 — "Indeed, the example of Jesus to Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust; then He said to him, 'Be,' and he was."

The Quran answers the question of his birth with a parallel: Adam had neither father nor mother, and Isa had a mother but no father — both came by the same word, "Be." "When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, 'Be,' and it is" (Quran 3:47). And the Quran draws the line that protects the whole story from excess: "O People of the Scripture, do not commit excess in your religion or say about Allah except the truth. The Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, was but a messenger of Allah and His word which He directed to Mary and a soul [created at a command] from Him" (Quran 4:171). A word from Allah, a soul from Him — and a messenger, not a god. Read our article on Prophet Adam — the first human and the first repentance to see the other half of the parallel the Quran draws here.

3.Born Under a Palm Tree

Maryam withdrew to a remote place, and the pains of childbirth drove her to the trunk of a palm tree. Alone and in pain, she wished she had died before this day. Then a voice called to her: "Do not grieve; your Lord has provided beneath you a stream" — and she was told to shake the trunk so fresh, ripe dates would fall (Quran 19:22-25).

وَهُزِّىٓ إِلَيْكِ بِجِذْعِ ٱلنَّخْلَةِ تُسَٰقِطْ عَلَيْكِ رُطَبًۭا جَنِيًّۭا

Quran 19:25 — "And shake toward you the trunk of the palm tree; it will drop upon you ripe, fresh dates."

"So eat and drink and be contented," the voice said, "and if you see from among humanity anyone, say, 'Indeed, I have vowed to the Most Merciful a fast, so I will not speak today to [any] human'" (Quran 19:26). The Quran names no place for the birth — the tradition says Bethlehem — and gives no date for it. What the Quran does record is the tenderness of the scene: a frightened young mother, a stream at her feet, dates falling from a tree she shook — Allah providing for her exactly when she had nothing.

Then she carried him to her people, who met her with the accusation she had feared: "O Mary, you have certainly done a thing unprecedented. O sister of Aaron, your father was not a man of evil, nor was your mother unchaste" (Quran 19:27-28). "Sister of Aaron" is how the scholars explain it — an honourific, linking her to the righteous lineage of Aaron's family, not a claim that Aaron was her literal brother.

4.The Baby Who Spoke

Maryam did not answer. She pointed to the baby. "How can we speak to one who is in the cradle, a child?" they said — and the baby spoke.

قَالَ إِنِّى عَبْدُ ٱللَّهِ ءَاتَىٰنِىَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَجَعَلَنِى نَبِيًّۭا

Quran 19:30 — "[Jesus] said, 'Indeed, I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet.'"

"And He has made me blessed wherever I am and has enjoined upon me prayer and zakah as long as I remain alive, and [made me] dutiful to my mother, and He has not made me a wretched tyrant. And peace is on me the day I was born and the day I will die and the day I am raised alive" (Quran 19:31-33). The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: "None spoke in cradle but three: (the first was) Jesus..." (Sahih al-Bukhari 3436).

Notice what the baby's first words are: not a defence of his mother — a definition of himself. "I am the servant of Allah." Before he claims the Scripture or the prophethood, he claims servitude. And the cradle speech itself contains the whole arc of his life in three dates: the day I was born, the day I will die, and the day I am raised alive. The scholars read the "maturity" of Quran 3:46 — he will speak to people in the cradle and in maturity — as pointing to his return.

5.Miracles by Allah's Leave

Isa was sent to the Children of Israel, and the Quran lists his miracles in his own words: "Indeed I have come to you with a sign from your Lord: I design for you from clay [that which is] like the form of a bird, then I breathe into it and it becomes a bird by Allah's leave. And I heal those born blind, and the lepers, and I bring the dead to life by Allah's leave. And I inform you of what you eat and what you store in your houses" (Quran 3:49).

On the Day of Resurrection, Allah will remind him of the same list — the Holy Spirit, the cradle and maturity, the Book and wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel, the clay bird, the healing of the blind and the lepers, the raising of the dead — each of them "by My permission" (Quran 5:110).

Every miracle in the list ends the same way: by Allah's leave. The bird of clay, the blind given sight, the leper cleansed, the dead raised — none of it was Isa's own power. He was also given the Injil, the Gospel, "confirming the Law that had come before him" and containing "guidance and light" (Quran 5:46), and he announced a messenger to come after him, "whose name is Ahmad" (Quran 61:6).

6.Not Killed, Not Crucified — and the Return

The Quran's most decisive statement about Isa comes in Surah An-Nisa. His enemies claimed, "Indeed, we have killed the Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, the messenger of Allah" — and Allah answers: they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him; it was made to appear so to them, and Allah raised him to Himself (Quran 4:157-158).

وَمَا قَتَلُوهُ وَمَا صَلَبُوهُ وَلَٰكِن شُبِّهَ لَهُمْ

Quran 4:157 — "And they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him; but [another] was made to resemble him to them."

The Quran does not say who was made to resemble him — only that the killing did not happen, and that Allah raised him. And it adds a promise: "And there is none from the People of the Scripture but that he will surely believe in him before his death. And on the Day of Resurrection he will be a witness against them" (Quran 4:159).

That promise is the bridge to his return. "And indeed, Jesus will be [a sign for] knowledge of the Hour, so be not in doubt of it, and follow Me. This is a straight path" (Quran 43:61) — the verse the scholars read as the sign of his descent near the end of time. The Prophet (peace be upon him) described it: "By Him in Whose Hands my soul is, surely (Jesus,) the son of Mary will soon descend amongst you and will judge mankind justly (as a Just Ruler); he will break the Cross and kill the pigs and there will be no Jizya. Money will be in abundance so that nobody will accept it, and a single prostration to Allah (in prayer) will be better than the whole world and whatever is in it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3448). And he asked: "How will you be when the son of Mary descends amongst you and your Imam is from among you?" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3435) — he will pray behind a leader of this ummah, following the law of Muhammad (peace be upon him). The narrations describe him descending at the white minaret east of Damascus and confronting the Dajjal — the details are from those narrations — and our article on Surah Al-Kahf — a light from one Friday to the next explains the protection the Prophet taught against that trial.

"Indeed, I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet." — Quran 19:30. The first words of a baby in a cradle — and the definition of a prophet that fourteen centuries have not changed.

Five lessons stand out. First, Allah's power needs no means: "Be, and it is" made Adam from dust and Isa from a mother alone. Second, truth is best spoken by the one accused: the baby in the cradle did not argue, he simply stated who he was. Third, every miracle ends with "by Allah's leave": the moment a gift becomes a claim about yourself, it stops being a gift. Fourth, do not exceed in religion: love Isa as Allah described him — a servant and a messenger — and the love stays true. Fifth, his return is a sign of the Hour, not a date to predict: live as though it could be near, because the single prostration that will one day be worth more than the whole world is available to you tonight.

Isa, son of Maryam, was born under a palm tree, spoke in his cradle, healed the sick, raised the dead, and was taken up to Allah — and the world still waits for his return. His story begins with a mother's vow and ends with a promise: Allah's word "Be" has never failed, and it never will.
