Prophet Nuh (AS): 950 Years of Patience and the Ark

R Razia Azeem · 11 Nov, 2026

For 950 years Prophet Nuh (AS) called his people night and day — then built a ship on dry land while the crowd laughed, and the flood came. Here is his story from the Quran, and what it teaches us about patience, trust and gratitude.

  2. Nine hundred and fifty years of calling
  3. The Ark on dry land — and the mockery
  4. The flood, and the son who would not board

Seerah & Stories

Nine hundred and fifty years — that is how long one man called his people to Allah, night and day, with patience that never ran out. His name was Nuh (AS), and the Quran gives him an entire surah named after him: the longest mission of any prophet, a ship built on dry land, a flood that washed a world away — and a story about us.

Most of us know the outline: the Ark, the animals, the flood. But the Quran spends little time on the ship and much time on the man — his words, his patience, his prayer. That is where the lessons are.

1.The first messenger to a forgetful people

Allah says, "Indeed, We sent Noah to his people, [saying], 'Warn your people before there comes to them a painful punishment'" (Quran 71:1, in meaning). Nuh (AS) was sent to a people who had drifted far from the worship of Allah alone. They had taken righteous men of the past and turned them into idols — the Quran names them: Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq and Nasr (Quran 71:23, in meaning). Respect for good people had hardened, over generations, into worship of statues.

On the Day of Resurrection, people seeking intercession will come to Nuh (AS) and address him as the first messenger Allah sent to the people of the earth, and as a grateful slave of Allah (Sahih al-Bukhari 3340, in meaning). That is his standing: the first messenger, and the grateful servant. Allah describes him the same way: the Children of Israel are "descendants of those We carried [in the ship] with Noah. Indeed, he was a grateful servant" (Quran 17:3, in meaning).

And his mission was the longest of any prophet. "And We certainly sent Noah to his people, and he remained among them a thousand years minus fifty years" (Quran 29:14, in meaning). Nine hundred and fifty years of calling — not of waiting, but of calling, day after day, to people who mostly refused to listen.

2.Nine hundred and fifty years of calling

Surah Nuh opens with his own report to Allah, one of the most honest passages in the Quran about what calling people to Allah actually feels like:

قَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّي دَعَوْتُ قَوْمِي لَيْلًا وَنَهَارًا

Quran 71:5

"He said, 'My Lord, indeed I invited my people [to truth] night and day. But my invitation increased them not except in flight. And indeed, every time I invited them that You may forgive them, they put their fingers in their ears, covered themselves with their garments, persisted, and were arrogant with [great] arrogance'" (Quran 71:5-7, in meaning).

Night and day — not when it was convenient, but night and day. And the result, by every human measure, was failure: the more he called, the further they ran. When he spoke, they put their fingers in their ears; when he came close, they covered themselves with their garments so they would not even have to see him.

And yet he did not stop. He told them that if they sought forgiveness, Allah would send rain, give them wealth and children, gardens and rivers (Quran 71:10-12, in meaning). He reminded them of the seven heavens, the moon as a light and the sun as a lamp (Quran 71:14-16, in meaning) — and still, only a few believed.

That is the first great lesson of Nuh (AS): the result of your calling is not your responsibility — the calling itself is. Nine hundred and fifty years, and the Quran counts the believers as "a few" (Quran 11:40, in meaning). A prophet who calls for nine and a half centuries and never gives up is not a failure — he is the standard. For the same patience in hardship, read Prophet Ayyub (AS): Patience That Never Broke.

3.The Ark on dry land — and the mockery

Then came the command that must have sounded like madness. Allah told him, "And construct the ship under Our observation and Our inspiration and do not address Me concerning those who have wronged; indeed, they are [to be] drowned" (Quran 11:37, in meaning). Build a ship — on dry land, far from the sea — for a flood that no one could see coming.

The Quran does not tell us the size of the Ark, and we should not invent one. It tells us what it was made of: "And We carried him on a [construction of] planks and nails" (Quran 54:13, in meaning) — wood and nails, built by hand, under Allah's care. And it tells us what happened while he built it: "whenever an assembly of the eminent of his people passed by him, they ridiculed him" (Quran 11:38, in meaning).

Think about that scene: a very old man, building a huge wooden ship where there is no water, while the important people of the town walk past and laugh. Every plank he fitted was an act of faith in a promise no one else could see.

There is a lesson here for anyone mocked for doing the right thing: the young man laughed at for praying, the woman mocked for her hijab, the honest trader laughed at for refusing a crooked deal. The mockery of the crowd is not evidence that you are wrong. Nuh (AS) was mocked for building the Ark — and the Ark was the only thing that survived. For the balance of taking the means and trusting Allah, read Tawakkul: Trusting Allah After You Have Done Your Part.

4.The flood, and the son who would not board

When the command finally came, it came suddenly: "Load upon the ship of each [creature] two mates and your family... and [include] whoever has believed. But none had believed with him, except a few" (Quran 11:40, in meaning). The Quran does not fix the number of those few, and we should not present a number as certain. What is certain is that family ties alone did not save anyone — among those left behind was Nuh's own son.

The scene on the water is the most heartbreaking in the whole story. "And it sailed with them through waves like mountains, and Noah called to his son who was apart [from them], 'O my son, come aboard with us and be not with the disbelievers.' [But] he said, 'I will take refuge on a mountain to protect me from the water.' [Noah] said, 'There is no protector today from the decree of Allah, except for whom He gives mercy.' And the waves came between them, and he was among the drowned" (Quran 11:42-43, in meaning).

The Quran does not name the son — his name is not given in the Quran. What it gives us is a father calling across the waves, and a son who trusted a mountain more than his father's message. The mountain was high, the water was higher. There is no protector from Allah's decree except Allah's mercy — no mountain, no wealth, no family name, no clever plan. Only mercy saves, and mercy is found by boarding the ship: by believing, not by standing apart.

After the waves had taken his son, Nuh (AS) called out to his Lord: "My Lord, indeed my son is of my family." Allah answered, "O Noah, indeed he is not of your family; indeed, he is [one whose] work was other than righteous" (Quran 11:45-46, in meaning). Family in the sight of Allah is not blood alone — it is faith and righteous action. Nuh (AS) accepted the answer and turned back to Allah.

5.The landing on Judi — a new beginning

When the punishment had run its course, the command came to the earth and the sky: "O earth, swallow your water, and O sky, withhold [your rain]. And the water subsided, and the ship came to rest on [Mount] Judi" (Quran 11:44, in meaning). The same water that had drowned a world now obeyed. The Ark that had been mocked on dry land came to rest on a mountain — named in the Quran: Judi.

Then came the words every traveller longs to hear: "O Noah, disembark in security from Us and blessings upon you and upon nations [descending] from those with you" (Quran 11:48, in meaning). "And We made his descendants those remaining [on the earth]" (Quran 37:77, in meaning). Every one of us is, in that sense, a child of the Ark.

And Nuh (AS) was taught the words to say as he stepped off: "My Lord, let me land at a blessed landing place, and You are the best to accommodate [us]" (Quran 23:29, in meaning). Not a rich land, not a grand city — a blessed landing. After nine hundred and fifty years, his prayer is still the same: blessing, and Allah's care.

"And Noah had certainly called upon Us, and [We are] the best of responders." — Quran 37:75, in meaning

That is Allah's verdict: Nuh (AS) called, and Allah is the best of responders. The answer took nine hundred and fifty years — but it arrived, with blessings that covered the whole earth.

6.Five lessons from Nuh for us today

1. Keep calling, even when no one listens. Nuh (AS) called night and day for 950 years, and only a few believed. Do not measure your effort by their response: the calling is yours; the guidance belongs to Allah. "Indeed, you do not guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills" (Quran 28:56, in meaning).

2. Do the strange, sincere thing — even when people laugh. Building a ship on dry land looked foolish to everyone except Allah. Build your Ark anyway: the prayer no one sees, the charity no one counts, the truth no one wants to hear. The crowd's laughter is not the verdict.

3. No mountain can save you from Allah's decree. The son trusted the mountain; the mountain could not hold back the water. Our mountains today are wealth, status, connections, clever plans — all of them real, all of them limited. Only Allah's mercy protects, and mercy is found by boarding the ship: by faith and righteous action. For the door that stays open while there is still time, read Repentance (Tawbah): The Door That Never Closes.

4. Family is faith as well as blood. Nuh's own son was not saved by being Nuh's son. No one inherits salvation from a righteous parent. What we can do is what Nuh did: call them, love them, pray for them — and leave the result with Allah. The call across the waves — "O my son, come aboard with us" — is the duty of every parent, in every age.

5. Gratitude is the mark of the survivor. Allah called Nuh a grateful servant before the flood and after it. When you step off your own Ark — out of an illness, a hardship, a long trial — ask as he asked: for a blessed landing, and for Allah's care. The story did not end on Mount Judi. It continued in you.

Nuh (AS) spent 950 years calling people who mostly refused, built a ship that everyone mocked, and lost his own son to the waves — and the Quran still calls him a grateful servant, and the best of responders still answered him. If your calling feels long and your prayers feel unanswered, remember the man who waited nine and a half centuries: Allah answers.
