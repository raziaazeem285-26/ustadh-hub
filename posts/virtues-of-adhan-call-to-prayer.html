The Virtues of the Adhan: More Than a Call to Prayer

R Razia Azeem · 7 Nov, 2026

Five times a day a voice rises from the minaret — and the Prophet ﷺ taught us that answering it carries rewards most of us walk straight past.

Hadith

Five times a day, a voice rises from the minaret and reaches into homes, shops, streets and offices: Allahu Akbar — Allah is the Greatest. Most of us have heard it so often that it has become background sound — we keep working, keep talking, keep scrolling. But the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) taught that the adhan is one of the most reward-filled sounds a Muslim will ever hear — a call that makes Shaytan flee, earns forgiveness for the one who gives it, and opens a window in which dua is not rejected. This article looks at where the adhan came from, what the hadiths promise about it, and how to answer it properly.

The Quran commands us to answer the call: "O you who have believed, when the call is made for prayer on the day of Jumu'ah, proceed to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade. That is better for you, if you only knew" (Quran 62:9, in meaning). The call is not a reminder on a phone. It is Allah calling you five times a day. The question is not whether you hear it — it is what you do when you hear it.

1.How the Adhan Began: A Dream in Madinah

The adhan did not begin with a committee or a loudspeaker. It began in Madinah, in the first year after the Hijrah, with a problem: the Muslims needed a way to gather people for prayer on time. Some suggested ringing a bell, as the Christians did. Others suggested blowing a horn, as the Jews did. None of these felt right, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) did not settle the matter at once.

Abdullah ibn Zayd ibn Abd Rabbih (may Allah be pleased with him) went home troubled by the question, and that night he saw a dream. A man carrying a bell passed by him, and Abdullah asked if he would sell it. "What will you do with it?" the man asked. "We will call people to prayer with it," Abdullah replied. "Shall I not show you something better than that?" the man said — and he taught him the words of the adhan and the iqamah.

In the morning Abdullah came to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and told him what he had seen. "This is a true dream, if Allah wills," the Prophet said, and he ordered Abdullah to teach the words to Bilal ibn Rabah, "for he has a louder voice than you have." As Bilal gave the first adhan, Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) heard it from his house and came out trailing his cloak, saying he had seen the same dream. That is how the call began — taught in a dream, confirmed by the Prophet, and given first by Bilal, the first mu'adhdhin of Islam (Sunan Abi Dawud 499, sahih). Fourteen centuries later, the same words still rise from minarets all over the world, five times a day, unchanged.

2.Shaytan Flees When He Hears It

The first thing the hadiths teach about the adhan is what it does to Shaytan. Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said, in meaning: "When the adhan is pronounced, Shaytan takes to his heels and passes wind with noise, so as not to hear it. When it ends he returns — and flees again at the iqamah — then comes back to whisper into the heart until a person forgets how much he has prayed" (Sahih al-Bukhari 608).

Read that hadith slowly, because it tells you two things at once. First, the words of the adhan themselves — the declaration that Allah is the Greatest, the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is His Messenger — are so heavy with tawhid that Shaytan cannot bear to hear them. Second, he comes back. The adhan drives him away, but the real battle is inside the prayer itself, where he whispers old memories and new worries until a person forgets how many rak'ahs he has prayed. The adhan clears the air; khushu — focus and humility in prayer — is what keeps it clear.

The Quran gives the other side of the same picture. When the call to prayer is made, some people do not answer it at all — they mock it. Allah says, in meaning: "And when you call to prayer, they take it in ridicule and amusement. That is because they are a people who do not reason" (Quran 5:58). Three responses to one sound: Shaytan flees from it, the heedless mock it, and the believer answers it.

3.The Mu'adhdhin's Reward: Forgiveness as Far as His Voice Reaches

The reward of the one who gives the adhan is so great that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said people would draw lots for it if they knew. Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (may Allah be pleased with him) told a companion: "I see you liking sheep and the wilderness. So whenever you are with your sheep or in the wilderness and you want to pronounce the adhan for prayer, raise your voice in doing so, for whoever hears the adhan, whether a human being, a jinn or any other creature, will be a witness for you on the Day of Resurrection" (Sahih al-Bukhari 609). Even alone in the desert, every creature that hears his voice will testify for him.

And the forgiveness attached to it is measured by the voice itself. Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said, in meaning: "The mu'adhdhin will be forgiven as far as his voice reaches, and every moist and dry thing will bear witness for him" (Sunan Abi Dawud 515, sahih). The further the voice travels, the further the forgiveness reaches.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) put the value of the adhan in words no one can misunderstand: "If the people knew what reward there is in the adhan and the first row, and they found no other way to get it except by drawing lots, they would draw lots for it" (reported in Sahih al-Bukhari). The people who draw lots for a prize are the people who know what the prize is worth. The adhan is one of those prizes — offered to you five times a day, free. 4.What to Say When You Hear the Adhan

The reward is not only for the mu'adhdhin. The listener has a job too — and it takes less than two minutes. Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said, in meaning: "When you hear the mu'adhdhin, say what he says" (Sahih al-Bukhari 612). Repeat each phrase after him, word for word. But there are two phrases where the reply is different. When the mu'adhdhin says "Hayya 'ala as-salah" (come to prayer) and "Hayya 'ala al-falah" (come to success), you do not repeat them — you say La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah, "There is no power nor strength except through Allah." Mu'awiyah (may Allah be pleased with him) taught this from the Prophet's own practice (Sahih al-Bukhari 613). It is a confession built into the answer: I cannot even come to prayer except by Allah's help.

Then, when the adhan ends, send blessings upon the Prophet (peace be upon him). Abdullah ibn Amr (may Allah be pleased with him) heard the Prophet say, in meaning: repeat the mu'adhdhin's words, then send blessings upon me — one blessing brings ten from Allah — then ask Allah to grant me al-Wasilah, a rank in Paradise fitting only one servant, and I hope to be him. Whoever asks it for me earns my intercession (Sahih Muslim 384).

There is one more reply, for those who know it. Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said, in meaning: whoever says, on hearing the mu'adhdhin, "I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, alone, without partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and His Messenger; I am pleased with Allah as my Lord, with Muhammad as my Messenger, and with Islam as my religion" — his sins will be forgiven (reported in Sahih Muslim).

5.The Dua After the Adhan — and the Prophet's Intercession

After the adhan, there is one specific dua — and the reward attached to it is the greatest of all. Jabir ibn Abdullah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said, in meaning:

"Whoever says when he hears the call to prayer: 'O Allah, Lord of this perfect call and of the prayer to be offered, grant Muhammad the right of intercession and honour, and raise him to the praised station that You have promised him' — my intercession for him will be permitted on the Day of Resurrection." — Sahih al-Bukhari 614

اللَّهُمَّ رَبَّ هَذِهِ الدَّعْوَةِ التَّامَّةِ، وَالصَّلَاةِ الْقَائِمَةِ، آتِ مُحَمَّدًا الْوَسِيلَةَ وَالْفَضِيلَةَ، وَابْعَثْهُ مَقَامًا مَحْمُودًا الَّذِي وَعَدْتَهُ

Sahih al-Bukhari 614

Allahumma Rabba hadhihid-da'watit-tammah, was-salatil-qa'imah, ati Muhammadanil-wasilata wal-fadilah, wab'athhu maqaman mahmudanilladhi wa'adtah. Learn it — one short paragraph — and it buys the intercession of the Prophet (peace be upon him) on the hardest day there is. Al-Wasilah is the highest rank in Paradise, reserved for one servant alone. When you ask Allah to give it to him, you are asking for the best thing for the best of people — and the Prophet repays the request with his intercession for you.

One honest note: some people add the words innaka la tukhliful-mi'ad ("Indeed, You never fail in Your promise") at the end of this dua. That phrase is not part of the authentic narration — the hadith as reported in Sahih al-Bukhari ends at "alladhi wa'adtah." Say the dua as the Prophet taught it, without additions. The reward is attached to the words as he taught them, not to a longer version. For more on what makes a dua accepted, read our article on the five keys of accepted dua.

6.Between the Adhan and the Iqamah: A Dua That Is Not Rejected

There is one more gift hidden inside the adhan, and it is the easiest to miss. Anas ibn Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said, in meaning: "The supplication made between the adhan and the iqamah is not rejected" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 212, sahih). Between the call and the start of the prayer — those few minutes while people are gathering and doing wudu — there is a window in which Allah does not turn a dua away. Most of us spend that window checking a phone.

Here is how to carry all of this into your day, starting today. First, stop when you hear it. Pause the conversation, put the phone down, and repeat the words after the mu'adhdhin — with La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah at the two calls to come. Second, when it ends, send blessings on the Prophet and say the dua after the adhan, slowly, knowing what the words mean. Third, use the minutes before the iqamah for your own dua — the thing you have been carrying all day, in your own words, in any language. Fourth, teach the words to your children. A child who learns the dua after the adhan at seven will still be saying it at seventy, and every time they say it, the one who taught them shares the reward. And if you are a man who hears the adhan from the mosque near your home, go — the call says "come to prayer" and "come to success" in one breath for a reason.
