Dhul-Qarnayn: The Traveller Who Built a Wall Against Gog and Magog

R Razia Azeem · 28 Nov, 2026

Some Quran stories come with a name, a place and a date. Dhul-Qarnayn's comes with none of these — only a title, three journeys and a wall of iron. Yet in seventeen verses, Allah shows what power looks like in a believer's hands: it travels far, judges justly, builds for others, and never forgets Who gave it. This is the story of the Two-Horned One, and the wall that still stands between us and Gog and Magog.

Seerah & Stories

Open Surah Al-Kahf near its end and you meet a traveller like no other in the Quran: given power over the whole earth, he journeys to where the sun sets and where it rises, and builds a wall no army can climb and no pick can pierce. Then, the moment the wall is finished, he says it belongs to Allah and that one day it will fall. The story of Dhul-Qarnayn is not really about a wall. It is about what a person does with power — and how quickly a believer hands the credit back to the One who gave it.

1.The Question from Madinah

The story begins with a question. Allah says:

وَیَسۡـَٔلُونَكَ عَن ذِی ٱلۡقَرۡنَیۡنِۖ قُلۡ سَأَتۡلُوا۟ عَلَیۡكُم مِّنۡهُ ذِكۡرًا

Quran 18:83 — "And they ask you about Dhul-Qarnayn. Say: I will recite to you something of his story."

The books of seerah relate that the Jews of Madinah gave the Quraysh three test questions for the Prophet ﷺ — about the Sleepers of the Cave, about Dhul-Qarnayn, and about the Spirit — and that Surah Al-Kahf answered the first two, while the Spirit was answered in Surah Al-Isra (17:85). Notice what the Quran does with the question: it gives no name, no dates, no kingdom, no map. It answers with meaning — "I will recite to you something of his story" — only what we need, and only what teaches. The identity of the man is not the lesson. The pattern of his life is.

2.Who Was Dhul-Qarnayn?

"Dhul-Qarnayn" means "the Two-Horned One", or "the one with two horns". The Quran never explains the name. The commentators suggest it points to the two ends of his journeys — east and west — or to two eras his rule joined. Many classical scholars identified him with Alexander the Great, while others objected that Alexander as history knows him claimed divinity for himself, and the man of this story is a just believer who credits Allah for everything. That identification is a scholarly opinion, not an established fact, and this article takes no side on it — the Quran deliberately leaves the name out, and a believer does not need it to take the lesson.

What the Quran does establish is this: "Indeed, We established him upon the earth, and We gave him a way to everything" (Quran 18:84). Two gifts: power, and the means to use it. Then comes the phrase that opens each of his three journeys, repeated three times: "he followed a way" (18:85, 18:89, 18:92). Power is given — then it is used, in a direction. A gift from Allah is not a possession to admire; it is a trust to spend, and the spending is the test. For another king who was given power and used it in gratitude, read Prophet Sulayman (AS): A Kingdom of Gratitude.

3.The Journey West: Where the Sun Sets

He follows a way "until, when he reached the setting of the sun, he found it setting in a spring of dark, murky water, and near it a people" (Quran 18:86). The verse describes what he saw from where he stood — the sun sinking into a dark spring at the horizon — as the classical commentators explain it. Beside that spring he finds a people, and Allah gives him the choice: "O Dhul-Qarnayn, either you punish [them] or else adopt among them [a way of] goodness" (18:86).

His answer is the first portrait of his character: "As for one who wrongs, we will punish him. Then he will be returned to his Lord, and He will punish him with a terrible punishment. But as for one who believes and does righteousness, he will have a beautiful reward, and we will speak to him from our command with ease" (Quran 18:87-88). Before he judges anyone, he announces the rule: punishment for the wrongdoer, reward and gentle words for the believer. He does not decide by whim, and the final reckoning belongs to Allah. Power without justice is tyranny; his very first act of power is a law, and the law itself is mercy.

4.The Journey East: A People with No Shelter

He follows a way again, "until, when he came to the rising of the sun, he found it rising on a people for whom We had not made against it any shield" (Quran 18:90) — a people with no shelter from the sun. And then he moves on; the passage does not say he judged them, or taxed them, or built for them. It only says: "Thus it was, and We encompassed what was with him in knowledge" (Quran 18:91).

In the west he judged; in the east he simply passed — not every place needs his intervention. And the closing words quietly correct any pride the journey might have fed: whatever he knows, Allah's knowledge encompasses it all. The farthest people on earth are not beyond Allah's knowledge.

5.The People Between Two Mountains

On the third journey, "until, when he reached between two mountain barriers, he found beside them a people who could scarcely understand speech" (Quran 18:93) — a people separated by language as well as by mountains, who yet make themselves understood: "O Dhul-Qarnayn, indeed Gog and Magog are corrupting the land. So may we assign for you an expenditure that you might make between us and them a barrier?" (Quran 18:94).

Gog and Magog — Ya'juj wa Ma'juj — are a people of corruption and violence whom the Quran names here and again in Surah Al-Anbiya (21:96). The Quran says no more about who they are or where they live, and this article will not invent what the Quran leaves unsaid; the hadith tells us they are a vast people held back behind the wall until the end of time. What matters here is the request: these frightened people offer to pay him, as if protection were a service to be bought. His answer turns the whole transaction upside down:

قَالَ مَا مَكَّنِّی فِیهِ رَبِّی خَیۡرࣱ فَأَعِینُونِی بِقُوَّةٍ أَجۡعَلۡ بَیۡنَكُمۡ وَبَیۡنَهُمۡ رَدۡمًا

Quran 18:95 — "That in which my Lord has established me is better than what you offer. But assist me with strength, and I will make between you and them a dam."

He refuses the money and asks only for labour. The strong do not sell protection to the weak. Service to people in danger is not a business — and a ruler who charges the frightened for shielding them has failed his trust.

6.The Wall of Iron and Copper

Then the building begins: "Bring me sheets of iron" — until he has levelled the gap between the two mountain sides — "Blow [with bellows]" until the iron glows like fire — "Bring me molten copper to pour over it" (Quran 18:96). Iron for strength, copper for a smooth, unclimbable face. The result: "So they were unable to pass over it, nor were they able to dig through it" (Quran 18:97).

And then, in the same breath as the triumph, the builder speaks:

قَالَ هَـٰذَا رَحۡمَةࣱ مِّن رَّبِّیۖ فَإِذَا جَاۤءَ وَعۡدُ رَبِّی جَعَلَهُۥ دَكَّاۤءَۖ وَكَانَ وَعۡدُ رَبِّی حَقࣰّا

Quran 18:98 — "This is a mercy from my Lord. But when the promise of my Lord comes, He will make it level, and ever is the promise of my Lord true."

He takes no credit: the wall is Allah's mercy, not his achievement, and he announces its end in the very sentence that celebrates it — one day, by Allah's promise, it will be levelled with the ground. That is the humility of a man who knows a gift from an owner — and every human work, even one built of iron and sealed with copper, has an appointed end.

7.When the Wall Falls

The story does not end with the wall — it ends with the day the wall falls. "And We will leave them that day surging over each other, and the Horn will be blown, and We will assemble them in [one] assembly" (Quran 18:99). And in Surah Al-Anbiya:

حَتَّىٰۤ إِذَا فُتِحَتۡ یَأۡجُوجُ وَمَأۡجُوجُ وَهُم مِّن كُلِّ حَدَبࣲ یَنسِلُونَ

Quran 21:96 — "Until, when Gog and Magog have been opened and they, from every elevation, descend."

The Prophet ﷺ himself described what that opening means. Zainab bint Jahsh (RA) narrates that he came to her in a state of fear and said: "None has the right to be worshipped but Allah. Woe unto the Arabs from a danger that has come near. An opening has been made in the wall of Gog and Magog like this," making a circle with his thumb and index finger. She asked, "O Allah's Messenger! Shall we be destroyed even though there are pious persons among us?" He said, "Yes, when evil increases" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3346).

"Woe unto the Arabs from a danger that has come near. An opening has been made in the wall of Gog and Magog like this." — Sahih al-Bukhari 3346

The long hadith of the Dajjal fills in the scene of that day (Sahih Muslim 2937). After Isa (AS) kills the Dajjal at the gate of Ludd, Allah tells him of a people "against whom none would be able to fight" and sends the believers safely to Mount Tur. Then Allah sends Gog and Magog, who "they would swarm down from every slope. The first of them would pass the lake of Tiberias and drink out of it. And when the last of them would pass, he would say: There was once water there." The believers are besieged until an ox's head is dearer to them than a hundred dinars. They pray, and Allah sends insects into their necks; in the morning they perish as one person. Birds carry them away and rain washes the earth clean. Note: the same narration tells us that whoever lives to see the Dajjal should recite over him the opening verses of Surah Al-Kahf — the very surah that carries Dhul-Qarnayn's story. For the surah's own protection, read Surah Al-Kahf: A Light from One Friday to the Next.

Three lessons remain when the wall is gone. First, walls protect for a time, not forever. Dhul-Qarnayn built the strongest wall in the Quran, and he himself said it would fall. Our real protection was never a wall — it is righteousness. The Prophet's warning in Bukhari 3346 is the sharpest edge of that truth: a society is not destroyed for lack of righteous people, but when evil becomes the norm and "increases" among them. Second, the story sits in Surah Al-Kahf for a reason. The same surah holds the Sleepers who fled a tyrant, and Musa (AS) learning from Khidr what he could not see — for that journey, read Musa and Khidr: The Journey to Knowledge You Cannot See — and the surah itself is the shield the Prophet ﷺ prescribed against the Dajjal. Third, the pattern is ours to copy. Power used justly, service without a fee, credit given to Allah, and the humility to say "this is a mercy from my Lord" — that is the whole of Dhul-Qarnayn's story, and it fits in one life — in our homes, our work, our words. The wall still stands today, by Allah's leave. The question the story leaves with us is the one it began with: when power reaches our hands, what will we build with it — and whose name will we put on it?
