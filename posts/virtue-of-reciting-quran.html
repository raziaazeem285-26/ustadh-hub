The Virtue of Reciting the Quran: Reward, Light and Intercession

R Razia Azeem · 21 Oct, 2026

The first word revealed to the Prophet ﷺ was "Iqra" — Read! Fourteen centuries later, the Quran still waits on our shelves, its every letter worth ten rewards, its recitation a shield in this life and an intercessor on the Day of Judgment. Here is what the Quran and authentic Hadith teach about tilawat — and how to begin today.

Quran

Think of the most precious letter you have ever received — perhaps from a loved one far away. You read it slowly, again and again, holding every word close. The Quran is a letter from Allah Himself to you — yet for many of us it sits on the highest shelf, opened only in Ramadan. The rewards of reciting it are beyond imagination.

1.The first command was "Read"

The very first revelation was not a command to pray, fast, or fight. It was a command to read: "Read, in the name of your Lord who created" (Quran 96:1). Allah places recitation at the heart of worship: "Recite what has been revealed to you of the Book and establish prayer" (Quran 29:45). And He describes those who recite His Book as traders in a commerce that never fails: "Indeed, those who recite the Book of Allah and establish prayer and spend [in His cause] out of what We have provided them, secretly and publicly, [can] expect a profit that will never perish" (Quran 35:29). The Quran also calls itself "a healing and a mercy for the believers" (Quran 17:82) — every verse read is medicine for the heart.

2.Ten rewards for every single letter

Few promises in Islam are as precise and generous as this one. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever recites a letter from the Book of Allah, he will receive one good deed, and one good deed is multiplied ten times. I do not say that Alif-Lam-Mim is one letter, but Alif is a letter, Lam is a letter, and Mim is a letter" (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 2910, graded Hasan Sahih). Just reading Alif, Lam, Mim earns thirty good deeds in seconds — a single page holds hundreds of letters, a whole juz thousands.

3.An intercessor on the Day of Judgment

On the Day when every soul will search for someone to speak on its behalf, the Quran itself will stand up for its companions. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Read the Quran, for it will come on the Day of Resurrection as an intercessor for its companions" (Sahih Muslim 804).

"Read the Quran, for it will come on the Day of Resurrection as an intercessor for its companions." — Sahih Muslim 804

Consider what this means: the verses you recite in your living room today — half-asleep after Fajr, or on a quiet evening — will testify for you on the greatest of days. This is why the Prophet ﷺ also taught that the best among people are "those who learn the Quran and teach it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5027).

4.Ascend in Paradise with every verse

Your relationship with the Quran decides your rank in the Hereafter — literally, verse by verse. The Prophet ﷺ said: "It will be said to the companion of the Quran: Recite and rise, and recite in the manner you used to recite in the world, for your status will be at the last verse you recite" (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 2914, graded Hasan Sahih; also Sunan Abi Dawud 1464). Every ayah you memorise and recite is a step on a ladder in Paradise. How high you climb is decided by how much of the Book you carried in your heart.

5.The fragrance of the believer

The Prophet ﷺ gave a beautiful parable: "The example of a believer who recites the Quran is like a citron (utrujjah) — its fragrance is pleasant and its taste is pleasant. The believer who does not recite the Quran is like a date — it has no fragrance, but its taste is sweet" (Sahih Muslim 797). Recitation leaves a fragrance of calm, dignity and light on the believer that others can feel. Even one short surah carries immense weight — read our article on the virtues of Surah Al-Ikhlas to understand how four short verses equal one-third of the Quran.

6.Light in your home: recitation drives Shaytan away

A house where the Quran is recited is a protected house. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Do not make your houses like graves. Indeed, Satan flees from the house in which Surah al-Baqarah is recited" (Sahih Muslim 780). If the greatest surah drives Shaytan away, imagine the light that fills a home where the Quran is a daily sound. And among all verses, none carries more weight than Ayat al-Kursi (Quran 2:255) — the greatest verse of the Quran, whose reciter is promised protection by night.

7.Begin today — even stumbling earns double

"But I read slowly," some say, "I stumble over the words." The Prophet ﷺ answered this excuse himself: "The one who recites the Quran and is skilled in it will be with the noble, righteous scribes; and the one who recites it with difficulty, stammering as he recites it, will have two rewards" (Sahih Muslim 796). Struggling is not a reason to stop — it is a reason to earn more. Begin tonight: recite even ten verses after Isha, for the Prophet ﷺ promised that whoever recites ten verses in a night will not be recorded among the heedless, a hundred verses will be recorded among the devout, and a thousand among those with immense reward (Sunan Abi Dawud 1398, graded Sahih).
