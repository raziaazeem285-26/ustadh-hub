Maryam (AS): The Woman Chosen Above All Women

R Razia Azeem · 27 Nov, 2026

She is the only woman the Quran names by name. An entire surah carries her name, the angels greeted her by it, and Allah Himself announced that He had chosen her above the women of the worlds. This is the story of Maryam, mother of Isa (AS) — devotion, trust and patience that still lights the way for every believer.

Seerah & Stories

Maryam is the only woman the Quran names by name. Other righteous women appear in its pages — the wife of Pharaoh, the mother of Musa, the wife of Imran — but none of them is named. Maryam alone is called by her name, again and again, until her name becomes part of her son's name too: Isa, son of Maryam. An entire surah, Surah Maryam (19), carries her name, and Allah announced her rank in words no other woman has received: He had chosen her, purified her, and chosen her above the women of the worlds. Her story is not only about a miracle. It is about the quiet, hidden years that made it possible — a mother's dua, a childhood in prayer, and a trust in Allah that never broke, even when she stood alone.

1.A Mother's Vow Before She Was Born

The story begins before Maryam is born, with her mother's vow. The wife of Imran — known in tradition as Hannah, though the Quran does not name her — vowed the child in her womb to Allah's service: "My Lord, indeed I have pledged to You what is in my womb, consecrated [for Your service], so accept this from me" (Quran 3:35, in meaning). She expected a son for the Temple. Instead, Allah gave her a daughter. "My Lord, I have delivered a female... And I have named her Mary, and I seek refuge for her in You and [for] her descendants from Satan" (Quran 3:36, in meaning).

Look at that dua. Before her daughter had done a single deed, her mother had already placed her — and her descendants after her — under Allah's protection. And Allah accepted the vow, and the child, "with good acceptance" (Quran 3:37, in meaning). The fruit of that dua went beyond anything she could have imagined. The Prophet ﷺ said: "There is none born among the offspring of Adam but Satan touches it... except Mary and her child" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3431). A mother's dua, made before birth, was answered across a whole lifetime — and her son's too. Parents, take note: the dua you make for your child by name is a seed Allah may grow into something you never live to see.

2.Growing Up in the Prayer Chamber

Allah placed Maryam in the care of Prophet Zakariya (AS), who became her guardian. "So her Lord accepted her with good acceptance and caused her to grow in a good manner and put her in the care of Zechariah. Every time Zechariah entered upon her in the prayer chamber, he found with her provision. He said, 'O Mary, from where is this [coming] to you?' She said, 'It is from Allah. Indeed, Allah provides for whom He wills without account'" (Quran 3:37, in meaning). For the full story of the prophet who raised her, read Prophet Zakariya and Yahya: Dua Answered — it was in that prayer chamber, watching provision arrive for Maryam, that the old prophet found the courage to ask Allah for a child of his own.

The mihrab — the prayer chamber — was her world: worship, seclusion and devotion, day after day, with no audience and no applause. Provision arrived for her from where she did not expect it, and she learned young that Allah provides without account. That is the pattern of Allah's chosen people: He prepares them in hiddenness. Maryam's miracle did not begin with the angel. It began with a girl who prayed in a chamber while nobody was watching.

3."Chosen Above the Women of the Worlds"

Then the angels spoke to her directly:

وَإِذۡ قَالَتِ ٱلۡمَلَـٰۤىِٕكَةُ یَـٰمَرۡیَمُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱصۡطَفَىٰكِ وَطَهَّرَكِ وَٱصۡطَفَىٰكِ عَلَىٰ نِسَاۤءِ ٱلۡعَـٰلَمِینَ

Quran 3:42 — "And [mention] when the angels said, 'O Mary, indeed Allah has chosen you and purified you and chosen you above the women of the worlds.'"

Chosen, purified, and chosen again — the repetition is the point. And the command that came with the honour was not a crown but a prayer: "O Mary, be devoutly obedient to your Lord and prostrate and bow with those who bow [in prayer]" (Quran 3:43, in meaning). Her rank was built on her worship. She was not chosen for wealth, beauty or status, but because she prayed, guarded herself, and trusted Allah when no one was watching.

Was she a prophetess? The scholars differ, and honesty demands we say so plainly. The majority hold that she was a siddiqah — a truthful, righteous woman of the highest rank — not a prophetess. What no scholar disputes is her rank. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Many men reached perfection, but among women none reached it except Asiya the wife of Pharaoh and Maryam, the daughter of Imran" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3411). And he named her first among the four best women of all the worlds: "Sufficient for you among the women of the world are Maryam the daughter of Imran, Khadijah, Fatimah and Asiyah" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3878, graded Sahih). Maryam stands at the head of that list — with Khadija, the first believer of our Ummah, beside her.

4.The Glad Tidings of a Pure Boy

The angels brought her the news: "O Mary, indeed Allah gives you good tidings of a word from Him, whose name will be the Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary — distinguished in this world and the Hereafter" (Quran 3:45, in meaning). In Surah Maryam, the angel came in the form of a man and said, "I am only the messenger of your Lord to give you [news of] a pure boy" (Quran 19:19, in meaning).

Her reply is one of the most honest questions in the Quran: "My Lord, how will I have a child when no man has touched me?" (Quran 3:47, in meaning). She did not say "this cannot happen". She asked how — an honest question, not a doubt. And the answer came back in the words that explain every miracle: "Such is Allah; He creates what He wills. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, 'Be,' and it is" (Quran 3:47, in meaning). She accepted, and the child she carried would be known forever by her name — Isa, "the son of Maryam", again and again in the Quran. For the rest of his miraculous story, read Prophet Isa (AS): The Miraculous Birth and the Promised Return.

5.Alone by the Palm Tree

Then came the hardest part of her story. Maryam withdrew from her family "to a place toward the east" (Quran 19:16, in meaning), and when the time came, she withdrew further, "to a remote place" (Quran 19:22, in meaning). The pains of childbirth drove her to the trunk of a palm tree, and there, alone, she cried out: "Oh, I wish I had died before this and was in oblivion, forgotten" (Quran 19:23, in meaning).

Do not rush past that cry. The woman chosen above the women of the worlds wished, in her pain and fear, that she had been forgotten. That is not a complaint against Allah — it is the honest voice of a human being at the edge of what she can bear, and the Quran keeps it, word for word, so no believer ever feels ashamed of crying out in pain. And look at how Allah answered her. Not with a rebuke — with provision, and with gentleness: "Do not grieve; your Lord has provided beneath you a stream."

وَهُزِّیۤ إِلَیۡكِ بِجِذۡعِ ٱلنَّخۡلَةِ تُسَـٰقِطۡ عَلَیۡكِ رُطَبࣰا جَنِیࣰّا

Quran 19:25 — "And shake toward you the trunk of the palm tree; it will drop upon you ripe, fresh dates."

"So eat and drink and be contented" (Quran 19:26, in meaning). A stream at her feet, dates from a tree she shook herself, and a vow of silence — she would not argue her case; Allah would defend her. Two lessons live here. First, Allah's help comes through means: the dates were a miracle, but she still had to shake the trunk. Second, grief is answered with provision, not a lecture. When you are at the end of your strength, Allah gives you water, food and rest — and handles the rest.

6.The Baby Who Spoke from the Cradle

She carried her baby back to her people, and the accusation came at once: "O sister of Aaron, your father was not a man of evil, nor was your mother unchaste" (Quran 19:28, in meaning). "Sister of Aaron" was a title the commentators explain as one of honour and likeness — she was being measured against the righteous family she came from — not a claim that she was literally Aaron's sister. The accusation stung.

Maryam did not argue. She had vowed silence, and she kept it. She simply pointed to the baby. "How can we speak to one who is in the cradle, a child?" they said (Quran 19:29, in meaning). And then the baby spoke: "Indeed, I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet... and [made me] dutiful to my mother" (Quran 19:30-32, in meaning). The accusation was answered by a miracle, not an argument. She never defended herself with a single word. The truth spoke for her — from the cradle. For every believer wrongly accused, the lesson stands: sometimes the strongest defence is to point to the truth and let Allah do the talking.

7.What Maryam Teaches Us Today

Maryam's story ends, but her teaching does not. Five lessons belong in our lives. First, a mother's dua shapes a destiny. Her mother prayed for her before she was born, and that dua followed her into the cradle, the mihrab and motherhood itself (Quran 3:36; Bukhari 3431). Parents: pray for your children by name, the way her mother did. Second, chastity and devotion are the highest honour. Allah describes her as the one "who guarded her chastity" and "believed in the words of her Lord" (Quran 66:12, in meaning), and says, "We made her and her son a sign for the worlds" (Quran 21:91, in meaning). In a world that measures a woman by attention, Allah measured Maryam by her guarding of herself. Third, Allah prepares people in private. The mihrab years came before the miracle. If your life feels hidden right now — unseen, unthanked, uncelebrated — you may be in your own mihrab years. Fourth, when wrongly accused, do not argue — point to the truth. Maryam's silence was not weakness; it was trust. Fifth, help comes through means. Shake the tree. Do your part, however small, and trust Allah for the rest.

"O Mary, indeed Allah has chosen you and purified you and chosen you above the women of the worlds." — Quran 3:42

She stands at the head of the four best women of the worlds (Tirmidhi 3878) — Maryam, Khadija, Fatima and Asiya. And today, on this very channel, the story of Khadija (RA) — the first believer of Islam — has gone public as the first episode of our Sahabiyat Series: Hazrat Khadija (RA) — The First Believer of Islam. Read Maryam's story, then watch Khadija's — two women the Prophet ﷺ himself named among the best who ever lived. Her name is still spoken in every corner of the earth — because a girl raised in a prayer chamber, who asked "how" and trusted the answer, became the mother of a prophet and the standard for every woman who wants to be chosen by Allah.
