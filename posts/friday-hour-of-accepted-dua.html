The Hour of Accepted Dua on Friday

R Razia Azeem · 27 Nov, 2026

Duas & Fazilat

Every Friday carries a secret hour. Inside the best day of the week, Allah has hidden a short window in which no Muslim asks Him for anything good but He gives it to him. The Prophet ﷺ told us the hour exists, pointed out with his hands how short it is, and left us to search for it. This article is about that search: what the Prophet ﷺ actually promised, when the scholars say the hour falls, and how to catch it — this Friday, not "one day". For the sunnahs that frame the whole day, read Sunnahs of Jummah: How to Spend the Best Day, and for the surah of the day itself, Surah Al-Jumu'ah: The Friday Prayer.

1.A Hidden Hour in the Best of Days

Friday is not an ordinary day. "The best day on which the sun has risen is Friday; on it Adam was created, on it he was made to enter Paradise, on it he was expelled from it" (Sahih Muslim 854a). The whole human story — our beginning, our home, our fall — is tied to this one day. And inside it, Allah placed something extra. The Prophet ﷺ "talked about Friday and said, 'There is an hour (opportune time) on Friday and if a Muslim gets it while praying and asks something from Allah, then Allah will definitely meet his demand.' And he (the Prophet) pointed out the shortness of that time with his hands" (Sahih al-Bukhari 935). Muslim's version carries the same promise: "There is a time on Friday at which no Muslim servant would pray and would ask Allah for a thing (that is good) but He would give it to him" (Sahih Muslim 852a).

Notice two things. First, the certainty: "definitely". Not "maybe", not "if you are lucky". The Prophet ﷺ used the language of a guarantee. Second, the shortness: he gestured with his hand that the hour is brief. A short window with a guaranteed answer — that combination is the whole point. Allah could have named the minute. He hid it instead, the way Laylat al-Qadr is hidden in the last ten nights of Ramadan. The hiding is itself a mercy: it turns one hour into a whole day of dua. If we knew the minute, we would pray for sixty seconds and forget the rest of the day. Because we do not know it, the wise believer fills the entire Friday with asking.

2.The Promise: "Allah Will Definitely Meet His Demand"

Read the words of Bukhari 935 slowly. "If a Muslim gets it while praying" — the hour is found in prayer and dua, not in idleness. You catch it on your prayer mat, in sujood, with your hands raised, not while scrolling through the day. "And asks something from Allah" — anything good: forgiveness, guidance, health, lawful provision, righteous children, a good end. "Then Allah will definitely meet his demand." The demand is met — but remember how Allah answers. Sometimes the answer is the thing itself, delivered exactly as you asked. Sometimes it is something better than what you asked. Sometimes it is a harm turned away from you that you never saw coming. And sometimes it is reward stored for the Day when you will need it most. Our post on 5 Keys to Accepted Dua explains these forms of answering in detail — the point here is simpler: the promise is real, and the only way to lose it is to stop asking. "The invocation of anyone of you is granted (by Allah) if he does not show impatience (by saying, 'I invoked Allah but my request has not been granted')" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6340). Impatience is the one thing that cancels the guarantee. Ask, then trust the timing to the One who set the hour.

3.When Is the Hour? What the Scholars Say

The scholars differ on when the hour falls, and honesty demands we say so plainly — no one can name the exact minute, and anyone who claims certainty about it is guessing. Two views carry the strongest evidence. The first: the hour is during the Friday prayer itself — "between the time when the Imam sits down and the end of the prayer" (Sahih Muslim 853, reported by Abu Musa al-Ash'ari from his father). That is why the khutbah matters so much: sit, listen in silence, and make dua in your heart while the imam sits between the two parts of the khutbah and in the prayer itself. The second view: the hour is the last hour of the day, after Asr until sunset. "Friday is divided into twelve hours. Amongst them there is an hour in which a Muslim does not ask Allah for anything but He gives it to him. So seek it in the last hour after the afternoon prayer" (Sunan Abi Dawud 1048, graded Sahih by al-Albani). The Prophet ﷺ also said: "Seek out the hour that is hoped for on Friday after Asr until the sun has set" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 489). Two cautions belong with that last narration: Imam Tirmidhi himself called this particular chain gharib, and he recorded that Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Ishaq held the after-Asr view — indeed Ahmad said most of the narrations about the hour point to after the Asr prayer. So the after-Asr view rests on the weight of many narrations together, not on one chain alone.

What should you do with two strong views? Cover both. Be in the mosque, attentive and asking, during the khutbah and the prayer — that is the first window. Then free the hour after Asr until Maghrib for dua — that is the second. You cannot lose by covering both, and the disagreement itself is a teaching: the hour is searched for across the whole day, not gambled on in a single minute.

4.Friday Itself: The Best Day Allah Made

Before you chase the hour, understand the day that holds it. Allah commands the Friday prayer by name in the Quran:

یَـٰۤأَیُّهَا ٱلَّذِینَ ءَامَنُوۤا۟ إِذَا نُودِیَ لِلصَّلَوٰةِ مِن یَوۡمِ ٱلۡجُمُعَةِ فَٱسۡعَوۡا۟ إِلَىٰ ذِكۡرِ ٱللَّهِ وَذَرُوا۟ ٱلۡبَیۡعَۚ ذَ ٰ⁠لِكُمۡ خَیۡرࣱ لَّكُمۡ إِن كُنتُمۡ تَعۡلَمُونَ

Quran 62:9 — "O you who believe! When the call is made for prayer on Friday, hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trade. That is better for you, if you only knew."

"Hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trade." The day is built around the prayer: work before it and after it, but the prayer itself is the centre, and trade — the thing that feeds your family — is set aside for it. Then the next verse completes the picture: "And when the prayer has ended, disperse in the land and seek the bounty of Allah, and remember Allah much, that you may succeed" (Quran 62:10, in meaning). Friday is not a day off from the world. It is a day ordered around Allah: gather for His remembrance, then go back to your work carrying that remembrance with you. The hidden hour sits inside a day like that — a day whose whole shape is already a dua.

5.How to Catch the Hour: A Practical Friday Plan

Here is a plan you can follow this Friday, built on the two windows above. Before Jumu'ah: take the Friday ghusl, wear your best clothes, use perfume, and come to the mosque early — the sunnahs of the day, which our Jummah post lists in full. During the khutbah: listen in silence. Do not talk, do not fiddle with your phone, do not nudge the person next to you. The first window runs through the prayer itself (Muslim 853), so make dua in your heart while the imam sits between the two khutbahs, and in your sujood. After Asr — the golden hour: this is the window most scholars point to (Abu Dawud 1048), so protect it like you would protect an appointment with a king. Sit facing the qiblah, in wudu, with a written list of your duas in your hand. Begin with praise of Allah and blessings on the Prophet ﷺ — Allah says, "Indeed, Allah and His angels send blessings upon the Prophet. O you who believe, send blessings upon him and greet him with peace" (Quran 33:56, in meaning) — and the scholars list beginning and ending a dua with salawat among its etiquette. Then ask. In winter the time between Asr and Maghrib is short — sometimes barely an hour — so guard it: no errands, no scrolling, no "just one call". Tell your family in advance: this hour is mine with Allah.

6.What to Ask For — and How to Ask

Ask for good in both worlds, and ask big — Allah's treasures never run out. Ask for guidance before you ask for things, because guidance makes everything else fall into place. Ask for forgiveness first, because a heart that is forgiven asks more freely. Ask for health, for lawful provision, for righteous children, for your parents living and dead, for a good end. Ask for the Ummah: the sick, the oppressed, the ones in debt, the ones who have no one to ask for them. Include others in your dua — a heart that asks for others is a heart Allah loves to answer. And ask with certainty, not with a shrug. The Prophet ﷺ taught us not to be hasty (Bukhari 6340): never say "I asked and nothing happened", because the answer may already be on its way in a form you have not recognised — a door closing that would have harmed you, a delay that protected you, a reward waiting for the Day you need it most. Write your list before Asr today. Put the impossible things on it. The hour you are about to enter is the one hour in the week when Allah has promised, in the Prophet's own words, that He will definitely meet the demand.

7.This Friday, Don't Miss It

Today is Friday. Two windows stand open before you: the khutbah and the prayer, and the hour after Asr until the sun sets. The hour is short — the Prophet ﷺ showed its shortness with his hand — but the promise inside it is absolute. Before Asr, write your list. After Asr, put the phone away, make wudu, face the qiblah, and ask. Ask for what you need, ask for what your family needs, ask for what the Ummah needs, and then ask for one thing you have stopped believing is possible. Then tell someone else about the hour — a friend, a child, a parent — because guiding another person to a guaranteed answer is itself a dua that keeps paying you back.

"There is an hour (opportune time) on Friday and if a Muslim gets it while praying and asks something from Allah, then Allah will definitely meet his demand." — the Prophet ﷺ (Sahih al-Bukhari 935)

The hour is hidden, but the day is here, and the promise is waiting. Do not let this Friday pass like every other day. Catch the hour — and when Allah answers you, remember who told you about it, and make a dua for them too. For the surah of this day, read Surah Al-Jumu'ah: The Friday Prayer.
