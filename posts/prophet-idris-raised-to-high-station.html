Prophet Idris (AS): The Prophet Raised to a High Station

R Razia Azeem · 17 Nov, 2026

Seerah & Stories

Some prophets fill whole surahs of the Quran. Prophet Idris (AS) fills only four verses — yet those four verses carry some of the highest praise Allah gives to any human being: a man of truth, a prophet, counted among the patient, admitted into His mercy, and raised to a high station. His story is short in words and enormous in meaning. It is the story of a quiet life that Allah remembered forever.

The Quran mentions Idris (AS) by name in only two places. That brevity is itself a lesson: Allah does not measure a life by how much is said about it, but by how truthfully it was lived. What the Quran does say about him is enough to build a whole way of life upon — truthfulness, patience, knowledge, and a station with Allah that no crowd can give and no crowd can take away.

1.The Prophet the Quran Mentions in Four Verses

In Surah Maryam he is introduced right after the story of Zakariya and Yahya, in a passage that gathers the honoured prophets together. Allah says:

وَٱذۡكُرۡ فِی ٱلۡكِتَـٰبِ إِدۡرِیسَۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ صِدِّیقࣰا نَّبِیًّا ۝ وَرَفَعۡنَـٰهُ مَكَانًا عَلِیًّا

Quran 19:56–57

"And mention in the Book, Idris. Indeed, he was a man of truth and a prophet. And We raised him to a high station."

The very next verse gathers him with the other prophets Allah favoured, "of the descendants of Adam and of those We carried [in the ship] with Noah, and of the descendants of Abraham and Israel" — people who, "when the verses of the Most Merciful were recited to them, fell in prostration and weeping." (Quran 19:58)

His second mention comes in Surah Al-Anbiya, where he is grouped with Ismail and Dhul-Kifl:

وَإِسۡمَـٰعِیلَ وَإِدۡرِیسَ وَذَا ٱلۡكِفۡلِۖ كُلٌّ مِّنَ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِینَ ۝ وَأَدۡخَلۡنَـٰهُمۡ فِی رَحۡمَتِنَاۤۖ إِنَّهُم مِّنَ ٱلصَّـٰلِحِینَ

Quran 21:85–86

"And [mention] Ishmael and Idris and Dhul-Kifl. All were of the patient. And We admitted them into Our mercy. Indeed, they were of the righteous."

Four verses, five qualities: truthfulness, prophethood, patience, mercy, righteousness — and one promise, a high station. The classical scholars placed Idris among the earliest prophets after Adam (AS), in the generations before Nuh (AS); Ibn Kathir, in his history Al-Bidayah wan-Nihayah, places him as the third prophet sent to humanity, after Adam and his son Shith — a detail from the classical histories, not from the Quran itself. The Quran gives no dates for his life, and it is silent on the details of his death. Where the sources are silent, this article stays silent too.

2."A Man of Truth and a Prophet"

The first word Allah uses for Idris is siddiq — a man of truth. It is not the ordinary word for "truthful"; it is the intensive form, the one who lives truth so completely that truth becomes his character. The same word describes Maryam (AS) in Surah Al-Ma'idah (5:75) and Abu Bakr (RA), whom the Prophet ﷺ himself called As-Siddiq.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise. And a man keeps on telling the truth until he becomes a truthful person." (Sahih al-Bukhari 6094; Sahih Muslim 2607) Idris is the living proof of that hadith. A lifetime of small, unobserved truthful choices added up to a name Allah Himself recorded in His Book. Notice the order of the verse: siddiqan nabiyya — a man of truth, then a prophet. Character came before the office, and before we ask Allah for a bigger role we should ask whether our character can already carry it.

3.Among the Patient

In Surah Al-Anbiya, Idris is not praised alone. He is listed with Ismail and Dhul-Kifl as one of "the patient" — and then all three are described as admitted into Allah's mercy, among the righteous. Patience here is not the patience of enduring a single disaster. It is the long patience of a whole life: calling people to Allah in an age when, the classical histories tell us, human beings were drifting away from the guidance Adam (AS) had left behind, and doing it day after day without applause.

There is a kind of patience we rarely talk about: the patience of the unnoticed. Idris did not face a flood like Nuh (AS), or a fire like Ibrahim (AS), or a Pharaoh like Musa (AS) — at least, the Quran tells us of no such trial for him. His patience was quieter: teaching, reminding, worshipping, and persisting in a world that was slowly forgetting. That is the patience most of us actually need — the mother raising her children on Deen without praise, the shopkeeper refusing to cheat when every competitor does, the student keeping Fajr while his friends sleep. Allah counts that patience too.

The verse ends with a promise that belongs to every patient believer: "We admitted them into Our mercy. Indeed, they were of the righteous." Mercy is not earned by dramatic suffering. It is earned by steady righteousness, and patience is the thread that holds it together. Read more about patience in our article on the virtue of patience (sabr).

4."We Raised Him to a High Station"

"And We raised him to a high station." — Quran 19:57

This single phrase — wa rafa'nahu makanan 'aliyya — is the most discussed line in Idris's story, and the scholars of tafsir explain it in two main ways. Some, including Al-Tabari, understood it as a raising in rank: Allah lifted Idris to a lofty spiritual station because of his truthfulness, patience, and devotion. Others, including Ibn Kathir, connected it to a literal raising: the narration of the Night Journey, in which the Prophet ﷺ met Idris in the fourth heaven, suggests that Allah raised him bodily to the heavens. Ibn Kathir presents both views without forcing a final answer, and this article follows the same honesty: the Quran states the raising; the exact manner of it is a matter the scholars discussed.

What is certain is the principle underneath the verse: Allah raises whom He wills, and He raises them for the qualities He loves. The Quran says elsewhere: "Allah will raise those who have believed among you and those who were given knowledge, in degrees." (Quran 58:11) Idris had both — faith and knowledge — and Allah raised him in degrees. The world raises people for wealth, fame, and followers. Allah raises people for truth, patience, and knowledge. The two ladders lead in opposite directions, and every one of us is climbing one of them.

5.The Meeting in the Fourth Heaven

Centuries after Idris, on the night of the Isra wal-Mi'raj, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ ascended through the heavens with Jibril (AS). In the first heaven he met Adam (AS). In the second, Isa and Yahya. In the third, Yusuf. And in the fourth heaven, he met Idris. The narration is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari (349): Jibril said, "This is Idris; pay him your greetings." The Prophet greeted him, and Idris returned the greeting and said, "Welcome! O pious Prophet and pious brother."

The meeting confirms Idris's station in a way no argument could. He is not a forgotten figure from the distant past. He is alive in the honour Allah gave him, greeted by the greatest of all prophets in the fourth heaven, and remembered in the Quran that Muslims recite every single day. A quiet life, faithfully lived, is never wasted. Allah does not forget it — and on the Night Journey, He showed His Prophet that He had not forgotten Idris. 6.The First Teacher: Writing, Knowledge and Honest Work

The classical histories credit Idris with gifts that shaped human civilisation itself. Ibn Kathir and Al-Tabari report that he was the first human being to write with a pen, the first to learn and teach the sciences of the stars and arithmetic, and the first to cut, sew and wear stitched clothes. These details come from the historical tradition, not from the Quran, and should be read as reports of the scholars, not as revelation — but they fit the Quran's portrait perfectly: a man of truth who teaches, a man of knowledge who works with his hands.

The pen matters. Before Idris, the histories say, knowledge lived only in memory and speech, and it died with the people who carried it. Writing made knowledge survive its teacher. Every book you have ever read, every Quran you have ever held in print, every lesson a teacher ever wrote on a board — the tradition traces that blessing back to the pen of Idris. The prophet remembered in only four verses is also remembered as the one who taught humanity to write things down.

And the needle matters too. Idris did not only pray and preach; he worked. The tradition that he sewed clothes reminds us that honest work is itself worship when it is done with sincerity. The Prophet ﷺ said: "No one has ever eaten any food better than that which he earns with his own hands." (Sahih al-Bukhari 2072) Idris earned with his own hands, taught with his own pen, and called people to Allah with his own tongue. Knowledge, work, and worship were not three separate compartments of his life. They were one life.

That is why the Quran pairs his raising with his knowledge. Allah raises "those who were given knowledge, in degrees" (Quran 58:11). Idris sought knowledge, taught knowledge, and lived by knowledge — and Allah raised him. It is not how much you know that raises you, but how truthfully you live what you know.

7.What Idris Teaches Us Today

Idris (AS) never led an army, never built a city, and never performed a miracle the Quran records. His whole story is four verses. And yet Allah honoured him with a title, a station, and a meeting with the final Prophet in the fourth heaven. If that does not change how we think about an "important" life, nothing will. Here is what his story leaves us with.

First, be a siddiq in the small things. Truthfulness is a thousand small choices: the honest word when a lie would be easier, the full measure when no one checks, the promise kept when breaking it costs nothing. Idris became a man of truth one truthful choice at a time.

Second, practise the patience of the unnoticed. Most of our good deeds will never be seen, praised, or rewarded in this world. Do them anyway. Idris's patience had no audience and no applause, and Allah grouped him with the patient and admitted him into His mercy. The audience of One is enough.

Third, learn and teach. Write things down. Pass on what you know. The pen Idris is credited with is still the most powerful tool a believer owns — more lasting than wealth, more far-reaching than any speech. Teach your child one verse. Write down one lesson. The knowledge you share outlives you.

And finally, remember that Allah does not forget a quiet life. Idris lived long before any of us, in a time the Quran does not even date for us — and yet we are reading about him today, in a language he never spoke, on a screen he never imagined. That is what a high station looks like: not fame in your own time, but remembrance in Allah's Book, forever. May Allah make us among the truthful, the patient, and the righteous — and raise us, as He raised Idris, to a high station. Ameen.
