Controlling Anger in Islam

R Razia Azeem · 21 Oct, 2026

A rude word in traffic, a child's spilled glass, a colleague's unfair criticism — anger visits every home and every heart. Islam does not ask us to become emotionless; it asks us to master the emotion before the emotion masters us. Here is what the Quran and the Prophet ﷺ teach about turning rage into calm.

Daily Life

Anger visits every home and every heart. Uncontrolled anger has broken marriages, ended friendships, and left people with regrets they carry for years. That is exactly why Islam treats anger not as a small bad habit, but as a serious spiritual battlefield — and gives us a complete, practical strategy to win it.

1.What the Quran says about anger

The Quran does something remarkable: it does not describe the righteous only by their prayers and fasting, but by how they behave when they are furious. Allah says of the God-conscious:

وَالْكَاظِمِينَ الْغَيْظَ وَالْعَافِينَ عَنِ النَّاسِ ۗ وَاللَّهُ يُحِبُّ الْمُحْسِنِينَ

Quran 3:134

"…who restrain anger and who pardon the people — and Allah loves the doers of good." This verse sits inside a passage describing Paradise, "prepared for the righteous" (Quran 3:133). Restraining anger is presented as one of the tickets to Jannah itself. And in Surah Ash-Shura, Allah praises those "who avoid major sins and indecencies, and when they are angry, they forgive" (Quran 42:37). Note the honesty: it does not say the righteous never feel anger — it says that when they are angry, they forgive.

2.Real strength is self-control, not muscle

We admire the strong wrestler, the loud voice, the person nobody dares to cross. The Prophet ﷺ completely reversed that definition:

"The strong is not the one who overcomes the people by his strength, but the strong is the one who controls himself while in anger." — Sahih al-Bukhari 6114

The same teaching appears in Sahih Muslim (2609). In another narration, the Companions were asked whom they counted as a wrestler; when they pointed to the man who throws others down, the Prophet ﷺ answered that the true strong one is "he who controls himself when he is in a fit of rage" (Sahih Muslim 2608).

3."Do not get angry" — the three-word prescription

A man once came to the Prophet ﷺ and asked for a piece of advice. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Do not become angry and furious." The man asked again and again, hoping for something more detailed — and each time the answer was the same: "Do not become angry and furious" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6116). The lesson applies to all of us: whoever controls their anger closes the door to most sins of the tongue and the hand.

4.Four remedies in the heat of the moment

Advice is easy in calm moments and forgotten in furious ones. So the Sunnah gives us physical, doable steps for the exact moment rage hits:

1. Seek refuge with Allah. Two men quarrelled before the Prophet ﷺ, and one man's face turned red with fury. The Prophet ﷺ said, "I know a word which, if he were to say it, his anger would depart: A'udhu billahi min ash-shaytan ar-rajim — I seek refuge with Allah from Satan, the accursed" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6115; Sahih Muslim 2610).

2. Change your posture. The Prophet ﷺ advised: "If any of you becomes angry and he is standing, let him sit down, so that the anger will go away from him; if it does not, then let him lie down" (Sunan Abi Dawud 4782, graded sahih by Al-Albani). A changed body posture breaks the physical storm.

3. Step away and stay silent. Words spoken in anger cannot be unsaid. Leave the room and give the anger thirty minutes to cool. Silence in the moment of rage is not weakness; it is protection.

4. Remember the reward. It is reported that the Prophet ﷺ said, "Do not get angry, and Paradise will be yours" (Al-Mu'jam al-Kabir 7033, graded sahih by Al-Albani). Holding back when you could take revenge is one of the heaviest deeds on the scale.

5.Forgiveness: the crown of anger control

Controlling anger is only half the teaching; the Quran adds the crown — pardon. "Whoever is patient and forgives — indeed, that is of the matters requiring resolve" (Quran 42:43). And the Prophet ﷺ lived it. 'A'isha (RA) said he never took revenge for his own sake unless the limits of Allah were violated (Sahih al-Bukhari 6126). When a rough Bedouin yanked his cloak hard enough to leave marks on his neck and demanded wealth, the Prophet ﷺ turned, smiled, and ordered that he be given something (agreed upon — Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim). That is the standard: strength that smiles instead of strikes. If you want to grow a forgiving heart, the reflections in The Virtue of Patience: How Sabr Turns Hardship into Reward continue this theme.

6.Making calm a daily habit

Anger control is a muscle, built in ordinary days for the hard ones. Pause three seconds before replying when provoked. Make the morning and evening adhkar your shield — you will find them with references in our Dua Library. After an outburst, apologize quickly instead of defending yourself. Do not let anger write your decisions. The next time anger knocks, answer with A'udhu billah, and watch who is really in control. Explore more practical Islamic guidance on the blog.
