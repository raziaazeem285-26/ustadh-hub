Hazrat Bilal (RA): The Voice of Faith That Torture Could Not Silence

R Razia Azeem · 17 Oct, 2026

Seerah & Stories

Some stories are remembered for centuries because they carry a truth too large for one lifetime. The story of Hazrat Bilal ibn Rabah (RA) is one of them. He was born a slave in Abyssinia, yet his name became one of the most honoured in Islam. His body was crushed under a boulder on the scorching sand of Makkah — but all they heard from him was one word: "Ahad… Ahad" — One… One.

This is the story of how a man the world called worthless became the voice that still echoes from every minaret.

1.Who Was Bilal?

Bilal (RA) was born into slavery in Abyssinia (modern-day Ethiopia). As the seerah accounts record, he became the slave of Umayyah ibn Khalaf, one of the fiercest opponents of Islam in Makkah — a foreign slave with no tribe to protect him and no voice anyone was obliged to hear.

And yet this was the man Allah chose to honour. When the message of Islam reached Makkah, Bilal was among the very first to accept it. A slave opened his heart to the truth — and that single decision changed the meaning of his entire existence.

2."Ahad! Ahad!" — Faith Under Torture

The early seerah accounts describe what happened when Umayyah discovered that his slave had become a Muslim. At the hottest hour of the day, Bilal was dragged to the burning sands outside Makkah and laid bare on the scorching ground. A massive, heated boulder was placed on his chest, pressing the life out of him.

His masters demanded one thing: renounce Islam, return to the idols. But through the pain, Bilal kept repeating a single word — "Ahad! Ahad!" — "One! One!" — his testimony to the Oneness of Allah.

They crushed his body with a boulder, but all they heard was one word: "Ahad — One."

This was not stubbornness; it was certainty. Bilal had tasted something in his heart that pain could not reach. Every rock they laid on his chest only pressed his faith deeper into his soul. 3.The Price of Freedom

The seerah accounts record that Abu Bakr (RA), seeing Bilal's suffering, could not bear it. He approached Umayyah and purchased Bilal's freedom — paying a price some accounts say was far above what a slave was worth — and set him free for the sake of Allah alone.

Think about this. In a society where freedom was the most precious thing a slave could own, Bilal was given his freedom — and he used it as he had used his chains: in the service of Allah. He never looked back. His first loyalty was to the One who had freed his heart long before his body was freed.

4.The First Voice of the Adhan

After the Hijrah to Madinah, the Muslims needed a way to call the community to prayer. Allah showed the adhan in a dream to some of the companions, and the Prophet ﷺ instructed Bilal to give the call — because, as he told him, Bilal had the most beautiful and far-reaching voice.

Imagine it: the man once dragged through Makkah's streets in chains now stood above the Kaaba itself on the day of the Conquest, his voice rolling over the city — "Ash-hadu an la ilaha ill-Allah…"

That was not revenge. That was Islam's answer: the slave became the herald of the Oneness of Allah, and the idols of the Kaaba lay shattered at his feet.

5."I Heard Your Footsteps in Paradise"

Bilal's reward was not only in this world. In a hadith recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ said to him one morning at the time of Fajr:

"O Bilal, tell me about the best deed you have done since you embraced Islam, for I heard the sound of your sandals in front of me in Paradise." Bilal replied that there was nothing he hoped more from than this: whenever he performed ablution at any hour of the day or night, he would pray as much as was decreed for him.

A slave of Makkah — walking in Paradise, ahead of even the Prophet ﷺ in rank of that moment. Not because of wealth, or lineage, or power. Because of a quiet habit done sincerely: wudu, and prayer.

6.The Lesson: Taqwa, Not Lineage

Bilal's story is Allah's answer to every measure the world uses to judge a person. Race, wealth, status, family name — all of it falls away before the one measure that matters. Allah says in the Quran:

إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِندَ اللَّهِ أَتْقَاكُمْ

Quran 49:13

"Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you." (Quran 49:13)

This verse is the whole of Bilal's story in one sentence. He was black in a society that mocked the dark-skinned, a slave in a society that worshipped power, foreign in a society obsessed with lineage — and he became more honoured than all of them, because his taqwa was greater.

So the next time you hear the adhan, remember the voice that carried it first. Remember that the man who taught the world the meaning of "Ahad" never owned a thing — yet owned what the richest of Makkah never found: unshakeable certainty in the Oneness of Allah.

May Allah be pleased with Bilal ibn Rabah, and may He grant us a fraction of his certainty. Ameen.
