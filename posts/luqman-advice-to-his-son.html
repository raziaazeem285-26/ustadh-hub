Luqman's Advice to His Son (Surah Luqman)

R Razia Azeem · 12 Nov, 2026

A wise father once sat his son down and gave him advice — and Allah preserved that advice, word for word, in the Quran. Eight verses of Surah Luqman (31:12-19) hold a father's whole curriculum: what to believe, how to treat your parents, how to pray, how to walk and how to speak.

Quran

The surah is named after the father: Luqman, a man Allah gave wisdom. The Quran never calls him a prophet — most scholars hold he was a righteous servant — and yet Allah gave a whole surah his name, because the advice of a wise father is worth preserving for every father and every child until the end of time.

1.Who Was Luqman? The Wise Man Who Was Not a Prophet

"And We had certainly given Luqman wisdom [and said], 'Be grateful to Allah.' And whoever is grateful is grateful for [the benefit of] himself. And whoever denies [His favor] — then indeed, Allah is Free of need and Praiseworthy" (Quran 31:12, in meaning).

Notice how the surah introduces him: not by his lineage, not by his wealth, not by his trade — only by the gift Allah gave him. The classical commentators report he was a righteous servant; some say a slave from Abyssinia, a carpenter by trade. The Quran says nothing about any of that. It introduces him by the one thing that mattered: Allah gave him wisdom.

And the first fruit of his wisdom was gratitude. "Be grateful to Allah" — that is the first thing wisdom taught him, before any advice to his son. His son's name is not given in the Quran, and neither is the time or the place. What matters is the advice itself — and Allah placed His own command about parents right in the middle of it, as we will see.

That is the first lesson of Luqman before we even reach his advice: wisdom is not cleverness, it is seeing things as they truly are — Allah as the Giver, yourself as the receiver — and acting on what you see. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Each of you is a shepherd and each of you is responsible for his flock" (Sahih al-Bukhari 893; Sahih Muslim 1829). A father is a shepherd of his children, and the first thing a shepherd owes his flock is guidance.

2."O My Son, Do Not Associate Anything With Allah" (31:13)

Luqman's first advice is about Allah alone — before prayer, before manners, before anything else. The verse opens with a word of affection: he calls his son ya bunayya, "O my little son." Instruction begins with love, not with a lecture. A child who feels loved listens.

وَإِذْ قَالَ لُقْمَانُ لِابْنِهِ وَهُوَ يَعِظُهُ يَا بُنَيَّ لَا تُشْرِكْ بِاللَّهِ ۖ إِنَّ الشِّرْكَ لَظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌ

Quran 31:13 — "O my son, do not associate [anything] with Allah. Indeed, association [with him] is great injustice."

"And [mention, O Muhammad], when Luqman said to his son while he was instructing him, 'O my son, do not associate [anything] with Allah. Indeed, association [with him] is great injustice'" (Quran 31:13, in meaning).

Why is this the first lesson? Because everything else in the advice is built on it. A child who knows Allah — who He is, and that He alone deserves worship — needs nothing else explained first. Prayer makes sense only after you know to whom you pray, and gratitude only after you know who gave. And shirk is called "great injustice" because it is the greatest wrong a person can commit: giving Allah's right to another, after Allah gave you everything.

3."Be Grateful to Me and to Your Parents" (31:14–15)

Then something remarkable happens: right in the middle of Luqman's advice to his son, Allah interrupts with His own words — His own command about parents. The father's advice is paused so that Allah Himself can speak about the parents' right:

"And We have enjoined upon man [care] for his parents. His mother carried him, [increasing her] in weakness upon weakness, and his weaning is in two years. Be grateful to Me and to your parents; to Me is the [final] destination" (Quran 31:14, in meaning).

Gratitude to Allah and gratitude to parents stand in the same verse, side by side. The mother carried the child in "weakness upon weakness" — nine months of growing heavier, then two years of weaning. No one can repay a mother; the verse does not ask for repayment. It asks for gratitude — and gratitude begins with remembering what was done for you.

Then comes the hardest case of all: "But if they endeavor to make you associate with Me that of which you have no knowledge, do not obey them but accompany them in [this] world with appropriate kindness and follow the way of those who turn back to Me [in repentance]" (Quran 31:15, in meaning).

Even if parents push you toward shirk — the very thing the first verse warned against — obedience stops, but kindness does not. "Do not obey them, but accompany them in this world with appropriate kindness." There is no verse that releases a child from kindness to parents. The limit is on obedience; there is no limit on kindness. For more, read Serving Parents: Your Door to Paradise in Islam.

4.The Mustard Seed Allah Never Misses (31:16)

Luqman turns back to his son with the advice that makes a child honest when no one is watching:

يَا بُنَيَّ إِنَّهَا إِنْ تَكُ مِثْقَالَ حَبَّةٍ مِنْ خَرْدَلٍ فَتَكُنْ فِي صَخْرَةٍ أَوْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ أَوْ فِي الْأَرْضِ يَأْتِ بِهَا اللَّهُ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَطِيفٌ خَبِيرٌ

Quran 31:16 — "O my son, indeed if wrong should be the weight of a mustard seed and should be within a rock or [anywhere] in the heavens or in the earth, Allah will bring it forth. Indeed, Allah is Subtle and Acquainted."
"O my son, indeed if wrong should be the weight of a mustard seed and should be within a rock or [anywhere] in the heavens or in the earth, Allah will bring it forth." — Quran 31:16, in meaning

A mustard seed is the smallest thing the people of that time knew — a speck you could lose between your fingers. Hide it inside a rock, hide it in the heavens, hide it in the earth: Allah will bring it forth. The verse speaks about every deed: the small good no one saw, and the small wrong no one saw. Both will be brought forth, because Allah is Latif, the Subtle — He reaches what no one else can reach — and Khabir, the Acquainted — He knows what no one else knows.

This is the root of taqwa, and the root of honesty. The child who believes this verse does not need to be watched to be honest, because he knows he is watched by the One who sees inside the rock. And the adult who believes it keeps doing good even when no one claps, because the One who counts a mustard seed will not miss a sincere deed.

5.Prayer, Goodness and Patience (31:17)

"O my son, establish prayer, enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong, and be patient over what befalls you. Indeed, [all] that is of the matters [requiring] determination" (Quran 31:17, in meaning).

Four commands in one verse, and the order is a curriculum in itself. First, prayer — the connection with Allah, the anchor of the day. Then the duty to others: enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong. Then patience — because doing good and stopping wrong will cost you, and the verse says so in advance. And it closes with the reason: "Indeed, [all] that is of the matters [requiring] determination" — these are the things that take resolve. Anyone can do them once; doing them for a lifetime takes determination.

Notice that prayer comes first. The one who stands before Allah five times a day has the strength to stand for the truth the rest of the day. Prayer is not separate from character; it is the source of it. That is why Allah commands every parent the same thing Luqman commanded his son: "And enjoin prayer upon your family [and children] and be steadfast therein" (Quran 20:132, in meaning). For the patience this verse demands, read The Virtue of Patience: How Sabr Turns Hardship into Reward.

6.Walk Gently, Speak Softly (31:18–19)

"And do not turn your cheek [in contempt] toward people and do not walk through the earth exultantly. Indeed, Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful. And be moderate in your pace and lower your voice; indeed, the most disagreeable of sounds is the voice of donkeys" (Quran 31:18–19, in meaning).

Two manners close the advice: how you carry your body, and how you carry your voice. Do not turn your cheek away from people in contempt — the turned face is the body language of pride. Do not walk through the earth exultantly — the strutting walk is pride made visible. Pride shows in the face and the walk before it ever shows in words, and the cure is physical too: walk moderately, and lower your voice.

The comparison at the end is blunt on purpose. The donkey's bray is the most disagreeable of sounds — not because the donkey is a bad animal, but because a loud, harsh voice is ugly, not strong. The person who shouts to be heard is not powerful; he is unpleasant. True strength speaks quietly and is still heard. Luqman's last advice is about humility, and humility is the thread that runs through the whole passage: the first verse warned against the greatest arrogance, and the last verses warn against the smallest — turning your cheek from a fellow human being. For more, read Humility (Tawadu'): Strength in Lowering Yourself Before Allah.

7.Living Luqman's Advice This Week

Eight verses, and a lifetime of work. Here are five small ways to begin living them this week — one for each working day.

1. Teach your child one verse. Sit with your child and read Quran 31:13 and 31:16 together, in Arabic and in meaning. Ask them what a mustard seed is, and where Allah could find one.

2. Thank your mother — out loud, today. Call her, or sit beside her, and thank her for one specific thing: the nights she stayed awake, the years she carried you.

3. Pray one prayer together as a family. Allah commands, "enjoin prayer upon your family and be steadfast therein" (Quran 20:132). One prayer a day, together, is how a family becomes a family of prayer.

4. Do one hidden good deed. Give a charity no one sees, forgive someone who will never know, help someone who cannot repay you. The hidden deed is not lost — Allah will bring it forth.

5. Lower your voice once — on purpose. The next time you want to raise your voice, at home or at work, lower it instead, and notice what happens.

And make the dua of the righteous for their children: "Our Lord, grant us from among our wives and offspring comfort to our eyes and make us an example for the righteous" (Quran 25:74, in meaning). Luqman shepherded his son with eight verses, and Allah kept them for us. A father's advice, preserved forever — that is the gift of this surah, and the duty it leaves with every parent who reads it.
