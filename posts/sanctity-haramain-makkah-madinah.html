Sanctity of the Haramain: Virtues of Makkah and Madinah

Two cities, loved by over a billion hearts, declared sacred not by any king — but by Allah and His Messenger ﷺ. Here is what the Quran and Hadith say about the sanctity of Makkah and Madinah.

In this article
  1. Two Sanctuaries, Declared by Allah Himself
  2. Makkah: The First House and the Secure City
  3. Madinah: The City the Prophet ﷺ Loved
  4. Sanctity Means Adab: Honouring What Allah Honoured
  5. From Far Away: Loving the Haramain Today

Duas & Fazilat

Five times a day, every Muslim turns toward Makkah. And every Muslim who has heard the stories of the Hijrah carries Madinah in the heart too. Together they are the Haramain Sharifain — the two honoured sanctuaries. But why do these two cities hold a rank no other place on earth holds? The answer is not history or politics. It is the Quran and the Hadith — Allah and His Messenger ﷺ declaring their sanctity in clear words.

1.Two Sanctuaries, Declared by Allah Himself

A haram is a place made sacred — set apart, protected, honoured. Makkah's sanctity was declared by Allah from the beginning. In Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ taught that Allah made Makkah a sanctuary on the day He created the heavens and the earth — its plants are not to be cut, its game is not to be chased. This was not a decision of a tribe or a treaty of men; it is a decree woven into creation itself.

And Madinah? The Prophet ﷺ himself declared it a sanctuary. A hadith in Sahih Muslim reports that he said Madinah is a sanctuary between its two lava plains (A'ir and Thawr) — its trees are not to be cut, and no harm is to be caused within it. Two cities, two divine declarations of sanctity. No other places on earth carry this title.

2.Makkah: The First House and the Secure City

Allah says in the Quran (3:96):

"Indeed, the first House of worship established for mankind was that at Bakkah [Makkah] — blessed, and a guidance for the worlds."

The first house — the oldest place of Allah's worship on earth. And Allah says of its city (3:97): "And whoever enters it shall be safe." Safety is built into its name: Allah calls it "this secure city" (Quran 95:3).

وَهَٰذَا الْبَلَدِ الْأَمِينِ

Quran 95:3 — "And this secure city"

Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him) raised his hands for it, and his dua is recorded forever (Quran 2:126): "My Lord, make this a secure city, and provide its people with fruits." Look at Makkah today — a desert valley flowing with provision from every land — and you see that dua answered before your eyes. Allah Himself confirmed it (Quran 28:57): "Have We not established for them a safe sanctuary, to which fruits of all kinds are brought as provision from Us?"

And the reward of worship there is beyond imagination: a narration reported in Musnad Ahmad and Sunan Ibn Majah tells us that one prayer in al-Masjid al-Haram equals one hundred thousand prayers anywhere else — because no other building is the First House.

3.Madinah: The City the Prophet ﷺ Loved

Madinah's honour comes from the man who chose it as his home. When the Prophet ﷺ settled there, he made a dua for it that the ummah has treasured ever since — reported in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim:

اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ بِالْمَدِينَةِ ضِعْفَيْ مَا جَعَلْتَ بِمَكَّةَ مِنَ الْبَرَكَةِ

"O Allah, place in Madinah twice the blessing You placed in Makkah." — Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim

He ﷺ said: "One prayer in this mosque of mine is better than one thousand prayers anywhere else — except al-Masjid al-Haram." — Sahih al-Bukhari

Think about that dua: twice the blessing of Makkah. He also counted Madinah's mosque among the only three mosques worth a journey — as we explored in our article on the virtues of Masjid al-Aqsa.

4.Sanctity Means Adab: Honouring What Allah Honoured

Sanctity is not just a title — it is a way of behaving. The Quran gives the principle (22:32):

"And whoever honours the symbols of Allah — indeed, it is from the piety of the hearts."

The Haramain taught the ummah what that honour looks like in practice: no hunting within their boundaries, no cutting their trees, no raising of voices in dispute near the Prophet's Mosque, and hearts filled with awe at the Kaaba. This adab is the proof that sanctity has entered the heart, not just the words.

And it begins at home. Every local mosque is a small reflection of the great ones: a house of Allah we enter with clean clothes, calm voices, and focused hearts. Honouring the Haramain from afar starts with honouring the mosque next door.

5.From Far Away: Loving the Haramain Today

Most of us cannot see the Kaaba today — but we can love what Allah loves. Here is how the Haramain stay close to a faraway heart:

Teach their stories. Tell your children why Makkah is the First House and why Madinah holds the Prophet's dua. A love planted in childhood, rooted in Quran and Hadith, lasts a lifetime.

Make dua to visit. Allah promised the believers (Quran 48:27): "You will surely enter al-Masjid al-Haram, if Allah wills, in safety." Put the same hope into your duas — ask big, as our article on unanswered dua and tawakkul explains.

Prepare your heart like a traveller. Read about Hajj and Umrah. Save a little for the journey. Learn the adab of the Haramain now, so that if Allah ever calls you there, you arrive ready — not as a tourist, but as a guest of Allah.

The Haramain are not just places on a map — they are the First House and the Prophet's city. May Allah grant us all a visit, and the adab their sanctity deserves. Ameen.
