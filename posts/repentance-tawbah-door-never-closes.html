Repentance (Tawbah): The Door That Never Closes

R Razia Azeem · 26 Oct, 2026

You have done the thing you swore you would never do again. Or you have done it so many times that asking for forgiveness feels embarrassing now. Islam has one word for the way back, and one promise attached to it: tawbah — turning back to Allah — and a door that stays open as long as you are alive to walk through it.

There is a moment every believer knows: you sit alone after a mistake, and a voice says, “You have asked too many times.” That voice is despair — and despair is exactly what the Quran forbids. Repentance is not a favour you beg for once you are good enough. It is the road back, open to anyone willing to turn.

1. What Tawbah Really Means

Tawbah means turning back. Not feeling bad for an evening, and not a sentence whispered while planning to repeat the sin tomorrow. A turn: you were walking away from Allah, and you turn around and walk toward Him. Scholars describe sincere repentance with three marks — stop, regret, and resolve not to return — plus restoring others' rights where involved.

That last point matters most. Tawbah is not a one-time event for a saint. It is the daily maintenance of an ordinary believer.

2. The Verse That Forbids Despair

Allah addresses the worst case directly: “Say, ‘O My servants who have transgressed against themselves [by sinning], do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful’” (Quran 39:53). If you think your sins are too many, this verse was revealed for you.

“Do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins.” — Quran 39:53

The Quran repeats the promise from every angle. “Indeed, Allah loves those who are constantly repentant” (Quran 2:222). “And whoever does evil or wrongs himself but afterwards seeks Allah's forgiveness, he will find Allah Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful” (Quran 4:110). And for those who repent, believe and do righteous deeds, Allah promises He “will replace their evil deeds with good” (Quran 25:70-71). Your worst chapter can be rewritten as good.

3. Allah Rejoices at Your Return

We usually imagine repentance as a miserable affair — a servant dragging himself back, expecting anger. The hadith reverses the picture. Anas ibn Malik (RA) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said Allah is more pleased with the repentance of His servant than one of you who loses his camel in a desert, with his food and drink on it, and then finds it standing before him (Sahih Muslim 2749).

The Prophet ﷺ himself modelled constant return: he said he sought Allah's forgiveness a hundred times a day (Sahih Muslim 2702) — not because he needed it as we do, but to show that repentance is not a crisis measure. It is a daily habit, not a crisis measure.

4. The Man Who Killed a Hundred

No story makes the point more sharply than the man who killed ninety-nine people. Reported in Sahih al-Bukhari (3470) and Sahih Muslim (2766), it tells how he asked a worshipper whether repentance was possible, was told no, and killed him too — making a hundred. Then he asked a scholar, who answered: “Yes — who can stand between you and repentance?” The scholar told him to leave his land of sin for a land of righteous people. He set out, died on the way, and the angels of mercy took him, because he was nearer to the good land than to the one he had left.

His repentance was not a feeling; it was a journey — a decision, a direction, a step. He never reached the destination, yet he was counted among those who had turned. If a hundred murders did not close the door, what makes you think your sins have?

5. What Sincere Tawbah Looks Like

Allah calls it tawbatan nasuha — sincere repentance: “O you who have believed, repent to Allah with sincere repentance” (Quran 66:8). Sincerity shows in four places. You stop — today, not “from Monday.” You feel genuine regret, not annoyance at being caught. You resolve not to go back, and you change the road that leads you there: the company, the app, the late-night hour. And if your sin took someone else's right, you restore it or ask their pardon.

Two warnings belong here. Do not delay: the door closes only when the soul reaches its final moment, and “later” is the one word Shaytan loves most. And do not use mercy as a licence: planning a sin because “Allah will forgive me” is not repentance.

6. Five Steps to Turn Back Today

1. Name it plainly. “O Allah, I did this, and it was wrong.” Honesty is the first step of every return.

2. Pray two rak'ahs and ask. Make wudu, pray, and say Astaghfirullah with your heart present. Our article on istighfar explains why this short word opens doors of relief.

3. Replace, don't just remove. Every sin fills a need badly. Put a good deed in its place: a page of Quran, a walk, a call to a righteous friend.

4. Change one trigger today. Delete the app, avoid the gathering, tell one trusted person. A changed environment usually wins where willpower alone fails.

5. Do a good deed immediately after. Charity, a prayer, help to someone — proof to your own heart that the turn is real.

Then get up and live. The man in the hadith was counted among the righteous while still on the road — because he had turned. Turn today. Ameen.
