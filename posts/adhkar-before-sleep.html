Adhkar Before Sleep: The Words That Guard Your Night

R Razia Azeem · 22 Nov, 2026

Duas & Fazilat

Every night, you hand your soul back to Allah and trust Him to return it in the morning. The Prophet ﷺ did not leave that moment to chance. He had a short, beautiful routine for the minutes before sleep — a few words and a few small acts that turn an ordinary night into a guarded one. It takes about five minutes. Here it is, exactly as the Quran and the authentic Hadith teach it.

1.Sleep Is a Small Death — Every Night

The Quran describes sleep in a way that changes how you feel about bedtime. Allah says: Quran 39:42 — "Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that do not die [He takes] during their sleep." Every night, your soul is taken; every morning, it is returned — unless your appointed time has come. Sleep is a small rehearsal of death, and waking is a small resurrection.

ٱللَّهُ یَتَوَفَّى ٱلۡأَنفُسَ حِینَ مَوۡتِهَا

Quran 39:42 — "Allah takes the souls at the time of their death…"

The same truth returns in Quran 6:60: "It is He who takes your souls by night and knows what you have committed by day, then He raises you up therein that a specified term may be fulfilled." Your day is recorded, your night is a trust, and your waking is a gift you did not earn — it was returned to you.

وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِی یَتَوَفَّىٰكُم بِٱلَّیۡلِ

Quran 6:60 — "It is He who takes your souls by night…"

That is why the Prophet's ﷺ bedtime words are so honest. He did not say "good night" to his body; he named what was happening. When he lay down he said: Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya — "In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6324). And when he woke, his first words were praise: "All praise is for Allah who gave us life after He caused us to die, and to Him is the resurrection." The day opens and closes on the same sentence, said from both sides.

Once you see sleep this way, the bedtime adhkar stop being a chore. They are the last words of your day — the words you would want to be your last if the night turned out to be your last. That is the whole spirit of this routine.

2.Start With Wudu, Lie on Your Right Side

The routine begins before you lie down. Al-Bara ibn Azib (RA) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said to him: "When you go to bed, perform ablution as you do for prayer, then lie down on your right side" (Sahih al-Bukhari 247). Wudu before sleep is not obligatory, but it is the Sunnah — you go to your bed in the same state of purity in which you stand before Allah in prayer.

Then dust off your bed. Abu Hurayrah (RA) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "When one of you goes to his bed, let him dust it off with the inside of his lower garment, for he does not know what came onto it after he left it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6320; Sahih Muslim 2714). A small, practical act — and a reminder that care in small things is part of faith.

Then lie on your right side, as the Prophet ﷺ did, and say the dua of surrender that he taught Al-Bara to make his last words: "O Allah, I surrender myself to You, I turn my face to You, I entrust my affairs to You, and I place my back under Your protection, out of hope in You and fear of You. There is no refuge and no escape from You except to You. O Allah, I believe in Your Book which You revealed and in Your Prophet whom You sent." The Prophet ﷺ said: if you die that night, you die upon the fitrah — the natural state of faith — so make these the last words you say (Sahih al-Bukhari 6311; Sahih Muslim 2710).

Notice what the dua does: it hands everything over. Your face, your affairs, your back, your hope, your fear. You do not fall asleep holding your worries; you place them in Allah's hands first.

3.The Three Surahs in Your Cupped Hands

Aishah (RA) reported that every night, when the Prophet ﷺ went to bed, he would put his cupped hands together, blow into them, and recite Surah Al-Ikhlas (112), Surah Al-Falaq (113) and Surah An-Nas (114). Then he would wipe his hands over as much of his body as he could — starting with his head and face and the front of his body — and he would do that three times (Sahih al-Bukhari 5017).

Think about what is happening in this act. The three shortest chapters that speak of Allah's oneness and His protection are spoken into your own hands, and your own hands carry them over your body — head, face, front — three times, like a shield being fastened. It is ruqyah you do for yourself, every night, in under a minute.

The two surahs of refuge, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, are covered in depth in our article on Al-Mu'awwidhatayn: The Two Surahs of Refuge — what each evil named in them means, and why nothing else protects like them. At bedtime, they are not a lesson; they are a practice. Recite them slowly, blow gently into your palms, wipe, and repeat. Children can learn this before they can read: it is the first ruqyah most Muslim children ever learn, and many carry it for life.

4.Ayat al-Kursi: A Guardian Until Morning

Then recite Ayat al-Kursi, the greatest verse of the Quran (Quran 2:255). Abu Hurayrah (RA) reported the famous story: a stranger who came three nights to steal from the charity of Ramadan taught him, "When you go to bed, recite Ayat al-Kursi, for there will continue to be a guardian over you from Allah, and no devil will come near you until morning." When Abu Hurayrah told the Prophet ﷺ, he said: "He told you the truth, though he is a liar. That was Satan" (Sahih al-Bukhari 2311).

The verse itself explains why it protects: it describes Allah as the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of all, whom neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes. The One who never sleeps guards the one who must. Read more about the verse itself in Ayat al-Kursi: The Greatest Verse of the Quran.

One honest note: a popular saying claims that reciting Ayat al-Kursi before sleep sends an angel to protect you. The authentic narration says a guardian from Allah will be appointed over you — the wording in Sahih al-Bukhari 2311 is "a guardian from Allah," and the Prophet ﷺ confirmed it. We keep to what is authentically reported and do not add details the narration does not give.

5.The Last Two Verses of Al-Baqarah

Then recite the last two verses of Surah Al-Baqarah (Quran 2:285-286). Abu Mas'ud (RA) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever recites the last two verses of Surah Al-Baqarah in a night, they will suffice him" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5009; Sahih Muslim 808).

What does "they will suffice him" mean? The scholars of Hadith explained it in several ways, and Imam al-Nawawi and Ibn Hajar both recorded that it may mean all of them together: they suffice him as protection from evil and from Satan that night, and they suffice him in place of standing in night prayer. Two verses — about belief, obedience, and a dua for forgiveness and mercy — carry the weight of a whole night's worship for the one who cannot stand.

These two verses were given to the Prophet ﷺ on the Night Journey, and their meaning is covered verse by verse in Last Two Verses of Surah Al-Baqarah: Enough for the Night. At bedtime, read them slowly. The second verse ends with a dua that fits the night perfectly: "Our Lord, do not burden us with what we have no strength to bear. Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy upon us."

6.Thirty-Three, Thirty-Three, Thirty-Four

Then the tasbih the Prophet ﷺ taught his own daughter. Fatimah (RA) came to him exhausted from grinding grain at the hand-mill and asked for a servant to help her. The Prophet ﷺ came to her home that night, sat between her and Ali (RA), and said: "Shall I not teach you a thing which is better than what you have asked me? When you go to bed, say: Allahu Akbar thirty-four times, SubhanAllah thirty-three times, and Alhamdulillah thirty-three times — for that is better for you both than a servant" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3705).

One hundred words, counted on your fingers, and the Prophet ﷺ weighed them heavier than a servant. Ali (RA) later said he never left this tasbih off — not even on the night of Siffin, the hardest night of his life. That is the point: this dhikr is not for easy nights. It is the strength you carry into hard ones. The same three phrases are the dhikr after every prayer too — but at night, said slowly in the dark, they become a lullaby of praise: glory to Allah, all praise to Allah, Allah is the Greatest.

One hundred words on your fingers before sleep — and the Prophet ﷺ weighed them heavier than a servant. The night's last words should be praise, not worry.

Some narrations of this hadith count the three phrases slightly differently (thirty-three of each), and the wording above follows Sahih al-Bukhari 3705. Either way, the count is one hundred in total, and the meaning is the same: go to sleep praising Allah, and let praise be the last thing your tongue does.

7.The Last Words, and If a Bad Dream Comes

Now you are ready to sleep. Say the shortest dua of the night — the one the Prophet ﷺ said every night when he lay down: Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya, "In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6324). Seven words. Then close your eyes.

And if a bad dream wakes you, the Sunnah has an answer for that too. Jabir (RA) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: if one of you sees a dream he dislikes, let him spit lightly to his left three times, seek refuge in Allah from Satan three times, and turn over to his other side — and it will not harm him (Sahih Muslim 2261). Do not tell the dream to anyone. Change your side, seek refuge, and go back to sleep. The dream has no power over you unless you give it one.

That is the whole routine: wudu and your right side, the three surahs in your hands, Ayat al-Kursi, the last two verses of Al-Baqarah, one hundred tasbihs, and seven last words. Start with one part tonight — wudu and the three surahs is a good first pair — and add the rest over the week. In a month it will be as automatic as locking your door. And on the nights when sleep will not come, you will have something better than counting hours: words that carry you, and a Guardian who never sleeps.

One more thing for parents: teach this routine to your children the way you teach them to brush their teeth — a little at a time, every night.
