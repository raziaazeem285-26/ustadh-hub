As-Salam: The Greeting That Builds Love

R Razia Azeem · 10 Nov, 2026

Two words — as-salamu alaykum — carry ten rewards, open the door to love, and were the very first greeting Allah taught Adam.

  1. The First Greeting Ever Taught
  2. A Command in the Quran: Answer Better, or at Least Equal
  3. Spread Salam and You Will Love One Another

Daily Life

It takes less than two seconds. Three words, spoken as you pass someone in the street, enter a room, or answer the phone: as-salamu alaykum — peace be upon you. Most of us say it a dozen times a day without thinking. Yet those three words are not small talk. They are a dua you make for another person, a command from the Quran, and — in the words of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) — the key to loving one another.

If you take one idea from this page, let it be this: salam is one of the cheapest, easiest good deeds in Islam — and one of the most powerful. A greeting costs nothing, takes two seconds, and can soften a heart you have never met.

1.The First Greeting Ever Taught

Salam is older than humanity's life on earth. When Allah created Adam (peace be upon him), the very first lesson He taught him was about greeting. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that Allah told Adam: "Go and greet that group of angels sitting there, and listen to what they will say in reply to you, for that will be your greeting and the greeting of your offspring." Adam went and said, "As-salamu alaykum — peace be upon you." The angels replied, "As-salamu alayka wa rahmatullah," adding the extra words (Sahih al-Bukhari 6227).

Think about what that means. Before Adam had a home, a family, or a single day of life on earth, he already had a greeting — and it was a greeting of peace. Every time you say salam, you are repeating the first conversation in human history.

The word itself carries the weight. Salam is one of the Names of Allah — "He is Allah, other than whom there is no deity, the Sovereign, the Pure, the Peace (As-Salam)" (Quran 59:23). When you greet someone with salam, you are placing them under that Name. It is also the greeting of the people of Paradise, where, the Quran tells us, "their greeting therein will be peace" (Quran 14:23).

السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ وَبَرَكَاتُهُ

The greeting of Islam — peace, mercy, and blessings be upon you

So salam is three things at once: a dua (you are asking peace, mercy, and blessings for the person in front of you), a gift (you give it freely, expecting nothing back), and a sign (it marks you as a believer the moment it leaves your lips).

2.A Command in the Quran: Answer Better, or at Least Equal

Allah did not leave the greeting to custom. He commanded it. "And when you are greeted with a greeting, greet in return with one better than it or [at least] return it [in a like manner]. Indeed, Allah is ever, over all things, an Accountant" (Quran 4:86). Read that verse slowly: it gives you two options, and both are commands — answer with something better, or at least answer with the same. Ignoring a greeting is not one of the options.

وَإِذَا حُيِّيتُم بِتَحِيَّةٍ فَحَيُّوا بِأَحْسَنَ مِنْهَا أَوْ رُدُّوهَا ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ حَسِيبًا

Quran 4:86

This is why the greeting has three grades, and why the reward grows with it. A man came to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and said, "As-salamu alaykum." The Prophet said, "Ten." Another came and said, "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah." The Prophet said, "Twenty." A third came and said, "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuh." The Prophet said, "Thirty" (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2689, graded hasan sahih). The reward grows with every word you add, because Allah multiplies every good deed tenfold: "Whoever comes with a good deed will have ten times the like thereof" (Quran 6:160).

The scholars explain the verse and the hadith together like this: starting the greeting is a sunnah — a blessed practice the Prophet (peace be upon him) never left — while answering it is a duty. When someone greets you, the Quran has already told you what to do: give them back something better. The greeting the Prophet taught has three grades — peace, then mercy, then blessings — and that is the complete form.

3.Spread Salam and You Will Love One Another

The most famous hadith about salam ties it to the biggest things in faith. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "You will not enter Paradise until you believe, and you will not believe until you love one another. Shall I not tell you of something which, if you do it, you will love one another? Spread salam among yourselves" (Sahih Muslim 54).

"You will not enter Paradise until you believe, and you will not believe until you love one another. Shall I not tell you of something which, if you do it, you will love one another? Spread salam among yourselves." — Sahih Muslim 54

Follow the chain the Prophet draws: Paradise needs faith, faith needs love, and love grows from a greeting. It sounds too simple — until you test it. A street where people greet each other feels different from a street where they do not.

That is why the Prophet (peace be upon him) told us to greet people we do not know, not only our friends. A man asked him which part of Islam is best, and he replied: "That you feed people and greet those whom you know and those whom you do not know" (Sahih al-Bukhari 12; Sahih Muslim 39). Greeting a stranger is the harder half of the teaching — and the more rewarding half. It is easy to greet your brother; it takes faith to greet the shopkeeper, the driver, or the neighbour you have never spoken to, and mean it.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) gathered the whole path into one short sermon: "O people, spread salam, feed the hungry, uphold the ties of kinship, and pray at night while people are asleep — you will enter Paradise in peace" (Jami at-Tirmidhi 1855, graded hasan sahih). Look at the company salam keeps in that list: feeding the hungry, keeping family ties — read more in our article on keeping the ties of kinship — and the night prayer.

Salam is even counted among the rights one Muslim owes another. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The rights of a Muslim over another Muslim are five: returning the greeting of salam, visiting the sick, following the funeral procession, accepting an invitation, and saying 'yarhamuk Allah' to one who sneezes" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1240; Sahih Muslim 2162). Your greeting is not a favour you do for someone. It is their right over you — and yours over them. When you visit the sick — see our article on visiting the sick — you are paying the same kind of debt.

4.Who Greets First? The Etiquette the Prophet Taught

Salam has manners, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) taught them in detail. The basic rule is simple: the one who is moving greets the one who is still. "The rider should greet the pedestrian, and the pedestrian the one who is seated, and a small group should greet a larger group" (Sahih Muslim 2160). In another wording: "The young should greet the old, the one passing by the one sitting, and the small group the large group" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6231).

Behind the rule is a beautiful logic. The person who arrives is the one who disturbs the scene, so he is the one who brings the peace. The young greet the old out of respect; the small group greets the large out of humility. And if the person who should greet first does not, the other should greet anyway. When two people meet, the better of the two is the one who greets first.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) greeted everyone — including children. Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Prophet passed by some children and greeted them (Sahih Muslim 2168). The Messenger of Allah, the leader of a community, stopping to greet a group of children playing in the street. No one is too small for salam, and no one is too great to give it.

Salam also guards the home. The Quran says: "O you who believe, do not enter houses other than your own until you have asked permission and greeted their inhabitants" (Quran 24:27). Permission first, greeting second. And when you enter your own home, the greeting belongs there too: "When you enter houses, greet one another with a greeting from Allah, blessed and good" (Quran 24:61). The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The best of you is the best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family" (Jami at-Tirmidhi 3895). Being best to your family starts with the smallest thing — greeting them like you mean it, every single time you come home.

5.Salam and the Handshake: Sins Forgiven Before You Part

The greeting does not end with words. When two Muslims meet, greet each other, and shake hands, the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Two Muslims will not meet and shake hands having their sins forgiven them before they separate" (Sunan Abu Dawud 5212, graded sahih by Al-Albani). Two people, a greeting, a handshake — and they part cleaner than they met.

The handshake matters because it makes the greeting real. Words can be spoken while looking at a phone, while walking past, while thinking of something else. A handshake stops you: it asks you to face the person, take their hand, and mean what you just said. Add a smile, and the reward grows again: "Your smiling in the face of your brother is charity" (Jami at-Tirmidhi 1956, graded hasan). A greeting with a cold face is a body without a soul.

6.Seven Small Habits for This Week

Knowledge without practice is a seed that never gets watered. So here are seven small habits — one for each day of the week — that turn this article into something you will still be doing next month.

First, greet first. Do not wait for the other person — the one who greets first is the better of the two. Second, give the full greeting: say all three parts — peace, mercy, and blessings — at least once a day, and collect the thirty rewards instead of settling for ten. Third, answer better. When someone greets you, add a word to your reply.

Fourth, greet a stranger every day — the shopkeeper, the guard at the gate, the person in the lift. Fifth, greet your children by name, with a smile. Sixth, greet your family when you come home, and teach your children to do the same. Seventh, make it a family habit: for one week, count your greetings together at dinner.

Start tomorrow morning. Greet three people before you reach your first cup of tea — one at home, one on the street, one you do not know. Three greetings, six seconds, up to ninety rewards, and three hearts reminded that a believer wishes them peace. That is the whole teaching in one morning: a greeting so small you can miss it, and so great that Allah made it the first lesson He taught Adam, the greeting of Paradise, and the key to loving one another. Say it like you mean it. It was never just a hello.
