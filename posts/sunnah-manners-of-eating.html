Eating the Sunnah Way: Manners at Every Meal

R Razia Azeem · 6 Nov, 2026

Every meal is a chance to earn reward. The Prophet ﷺ taught us how to eat — Bismillah first, the right hand, from what is near, a third for food — and this article brings those manners to your table.

  2. Before the First Bite: Bismillah
  3. The Right Hand, and What Is in Front of You

Daily Life

We eat three times a day, every day of our lives — and yet most of us never think of a meal as an act of worship. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) taught otherwise. He showed his followers that the words you say before the first bite, the hand you eat with, the amount you take, and the thanks you give after the last bite can turn an ordinary meal into reward. This article gathers the manners of eating from the Quran and the Hadith — simple, practical, and ready to use at your very next meal.

The Quran begins with the food itself. Allah says, in meaning: "O you who have believed, eat from the good things which We have provided for you and be grateful to Allah, if it is [indeed] Him that you worship" (Quran 2:172). And again: "So eat of the lawful and good things which Allah has provided for you, and be grateful for the favour of Allah" (Quran 16:114). In both verses two things are paired: eat what is lawful and good, and be grateful. Everything that follows is built on those two pillars — halal food, and a grateful heart.

1.Every Meal Is a Chance to Earn Reward

Before any rule or manner, consider what is at stake. Anas ibn Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Indeed, Allah is pleased with His servant when he eats a morsel and praises Him for it, or drinks a sip and praises Him for it" (Sahih Muslim 2734). Read that again: a single morsel, followed by a sincere "Alhamdulillah", and Allah is pleased with you. The reward is not locked behind long prayers or great expense. It is sitting on your plate, three times a day.

That is the secret behind all the manners of eating. A meal is not a break from the religion; with the right intention, it is part of it. The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught that actions are judged by intentions (Sahih al-Bukhari 1) — so eating to gain strength for prayer, for work, for looking after your family, is itself an act of worship. The manners below are the way a believer turns something he has to do anyway into something Allah loves.

2.Before the First Bite: Bismillah

The first manner is also the most important, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) taught it to a child. Umar ibn Abi Salamah (may Allah be pleased with him) was a young boy in the Prophet's care, and his hand used to wander all around the dish. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said to him: "Boy, mention the name of Allah, eat with your right hand, and eat from what is near you" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5376; Sahih Muslim 2022). Three manners in one short sentence — and the first is Bismillah. Say it out loud, and teach your children to say it, so that the meal begins with Allah's name and not with habit.

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ

If you forget at the start, the teaching does not leave you stranded. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "When any one of you eats, let him mention the name of Allah. If he forgets to mention the name of Allah at the beginning, then let him say: Bismillahi awwalahu wa akhirahu — in the name of Allah at its beginning and at its end" (Sunan Abi Dawud 3767; Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1858). There is a reason for this, beyond blessing the food. A narration in Sahih Muslim (2018) explains that when a person mentions Allah's name upon entering his house and upon eating, Satan says to his followers: "You will find no place to spend the night and no dinner." One word at the start of the meal closes the door on him.

Before you sit down, wash your hands. It is simple hygiene, and it fits the same spirit of cleanliness Islam calls half of faith — read our article on cleanliness (taharah), half of faith.

3.The Right Hand, and What Is in Front of You

The second instruction from the boy's hadith is to eat with the right hand. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "When one of you eats, let him eat with his right hand, and when he drinks, let him drink with his right hand, for Shaytan eats with his left and drinks with his left" (Sahih Muslim 2020). The right hand is the hand of honour in Islam — used for eating, giving, greeting — while the left is kept for cleaning. Teaching a child this one habit early saves a lifetime of correction later.

The third instruction is just as practical: eat from what is in front of you. Do not reach across the dish, do not pick through the food for the best piece, do not take from someone else's side. It is a manner of consideration — the people eating with you matter as much as the food. The Prophet (peace be upon him) himself used to eat with three fingers — the thumb, the index finger and the middle finger (Sahih Muslim 2032) — and he taught that when one of you eats, he should lick his fingers, "for he does not know in which part of the food the blessing lies" (Sahih Muslim 2031). Even the last grain on the fingers is worth honouring, because the blessing may be in exactly that part.

Two more manners complete the picture of how the Prophet (peace be upon him) sat at a meal. A narration in Sahih al-Bukhari reports him saying: "I do not eat while reclining" — he ate sitting upright, in the posture of a servant, not lounging like a king. And he taught that when one of you drinks, he should not breathe into the vessel (reported in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim) — a small courtesy that protects both the drink and the people sharing it.

4.A Third for Food, a Third for Drink

The manners of eating in Islam also include how much to eat — and here the guidance is famous. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "No human being has ever filled a vessel worse than his stomach. It is enough for the son of Adam to have a few bites to keep his back straight. If he must, then one third for his food, one third for his drink and one third for his breath" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2380; Sunan Ibn Majah 3349). The one-third rule is not a strict measure — nobody is expected to weigh their stomach — it is a direction of travel: stop before you are full.

The Quran gives the same command in one line, in meaning: "Eat and drink, but do not be excessive. Indeed, He does not like those who are excessive" (Quran 7:31). Overeating dulls the body and the heart: the stomach is heavy, the prayer is heavy, the energy for good deeds is gone. A narration reported in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim puts it sharply: "The believer eats in one stomach, while the disbeliever eats in seven" — the believer is satisfied with little, because his aim is not the food itself.

In practice, this is the easiest manner to start with, and the hardest to keep. Take a smaller portion than usual. Eat slowly, and put the spoon down between bites. Stop when you are no longer hungry, not when you cannot eat another bite. Eat a little less for a week and notice the difference — in prayer, sleep and mood alike.

5.Never Criticise the Food; Eat Together

Here is a manner that protects the cook more than the eater. A narration in Sahih al-Bukhari reports that the Prophet (peace be upon him) never criticised any food: if he liked it, he ate it, and if he did not like it, he left it without a word. Think about who cooks in most homes — a mother, a wife, a host who spent hours in the kitchen. One careless comment about the salt or the spice can undo all of that effort and hurt a heart that only wanted to feed you. The Sunnah is simple: eat what you like, leave what you do not, and say nothing unkind. If you can, say something kind — a word of thanks to the cook is itself a form of gratitude.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) also taught his followers to eat together. His companions once told him: "We eat, but we are not satisfied." He asked: "Perhaps you eat separately?" They said yes. He said: "Eat together, and mention the name of Allah over it, and it will be blessed for you" (Sunan Abi Dawud 3764). The blessing is in the gathering: a family eating together, a guest eating with the household, a plate shared rather than eaten alone. That is why the same teaching runs through our article on honouring the guest — food shared is food blessed.

If your family has drifted into eating at different times, in different rooms, in front of different screens, this is the manner to revive first. One meal a day together, phones away, Bismillah said out loud, and the food tastes different — not because the recipe changed, but because the blessing returned.

6.After the Meal: Thank Allah

The meal does not end when the plate is empty; it ends with thanks. The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught a short dua for the end of a meal, and attached to it a promise that should make every believer learn it by heart:

"Whoever eats food and then says: 'All praise is due to Allah who fed me this and granted it as provision to me, without any effort from me nor power,' his past sins shall be forgiven." — Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3458 (hasan)

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَطْعَمَنِي هَذَا وَرَزَقَنِيهِ مِنْ غَيْرِ حَوْلٍ مِنِّي وَلَا قُوَّةٍ

Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3458

Al-hamdu lillahi-lladhi at'amani hadha wa razaqanihi min ghayri hawlin minni wa la quwwah — "All praise is due to Allah who fed me this and provided it for me, without any power or strength on my part." The scholars explain that promises like this cover minor sins, and they never replace sincere repentance — but the generosity of the promise is the point: a few seconds of thanks after a meal, and Allah forgives what came before it.

There is another praise the Prophet (peace be upon him) used when the table was lifted from before him: "All praise is due to Allah, abundant, good, blessed praise, without being left off, nor being without need of it, O our Lord" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5458). Even the short form — a heartfelt "Alhamdulillah" — is enough to close the meal the way it began: with Allah's name. And the Quran promises what gratitude brings, in meaning: "If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favour]" (Quran 14:7). For more on that promise, read our article on the virtue of gratitude — how shukr brings more blessings.

Start tonight. At your next meal, say Bismillah out loud, eat with your right hand, take from what is in front of you, stop at a third, and finish with the dua above. Teach it to your children the same way — these small duas are among the first things young students memorise, and they carry them for life.
