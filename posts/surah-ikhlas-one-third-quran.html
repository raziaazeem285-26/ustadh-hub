Surah Al-Ikhlas: The Surah Equal to One-Third of the Quran

R Razia Azeem · 15 Oct, 2026

Just four verses — shorter than most paragraphs — yet the Prophet ﷺ said this surah equals one-third of the Quran. Here is what Surah Al-Ikhlas says, why it carries such weight, and how to make it part of your daily life.

  2. What "One-Third of the Quran" Really Means
  3. The Hadith That Made the Companions Marvel

Duas & Fazilat

Surah Al-Ikhlas — the 112th chapter, only four verses — is the surah the Prophet ﷺ recited constantly, swearing an oath of its reward. Here is what it says, why it carries such weight, and how to make it part of your daily life.

1.Four Verses That Describe Allah

According to a narration reported in Sunan al-Tirmidhi, the polytheists of Makkah once asked the Prophet ﷺ to describe his Lord — and Allah revealed these four verses as the answer. They are the purest statement of tawhid (the Oneness of Allah) in the entire Quran:

"Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent." (Quran 112:1–4)

"The One" (Al-Ahad) — unique, without partner, as Allah declares: "And your God is One God; there is no god but He, the Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful" (Quran 2:163). "The Eternal Refuge" (As-Samad) — the One to Whom all creation turns for every need, Who needs nothing. "He neither begets nor is born" — free of the human needs people invented for Him. And the closing verse, echoed in "There is nothing like Him, and He is the Hearing, the Seeing" (Quran 42:11): nothing in creation resembles Allah at all.

2.What "One-Third of the Quran" Really Means

The scholars explain: the Quran's themes gather into three subjects — the knowledge of Allah and His Oneness (tawhid), the laws of worship and life, and the stories and lessons of the past. Surah Al-Ikhlas contains the first theme in its purest form. That is why it equals one-third of the Quran in meaning and reward.

One clarification: "equal to one-third" means equal in reward, not that reciting it three times replaces reading the whole Quran. The reward is immense, but the Quran as a whole — its rulings, stories, guidance — remains irreplaceable. For more on how Allah answers and rewards our worship, see When Your Dua Feels Unanswered.

Four verses, and yet "equal to one-third of the Quran" — because knowing who Allah is, is the foundation of everything else in Islam.

3.The Hadith That Made the Companions Marvel

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that a man heard another reciting Surah Al-Ikhlas repeatedly through the night. In the morning he mentioned it to the Prophet ﷺ — as if such a short surah carried little reward. The Prophet ﷺ replied: "By Him in Whose Hand my life is, this surah is equal to one-third of the Quran!" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5013)

In another narration, the Prophet ﷺ asked his Companions: "Is any of you unable to recite one-third of the Quran in one night?" They said, "How could anyone recite one-third of the Quran?" He answered: "Qul Huwa Allahu Ahad is equivalent to one-third of the Quran." (Sahih Muslim 811; a similar narration is in Sahih Muslim 812)

The reward of a third of the Quran, available in less than a minute of recitation — this is the generosity of Allah, multiplying the reward of sincere words beyond what our minds can measure.

4.Love for This Surah Opens Paradise

The virtue is not only in the reward but in what loving it means. In Sahih al-Bukhari (7375), Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that a man from the Ansar led his companions in prayer and always concluded his recitation with Surah Al-Ikhlas. Asked why, he said: because it describes the Most Merciful, and I love to recite it. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Tell him that Allah loves him."

And Abu Huraira (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that he once walked with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and heard a man reciting Qul Huwa Allahu Ahad. The Prophet ﷺ said: "It is obligatory." Abu Huraira asked, "What is obligatory?" He said: "Paradise." (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 2897; Sunan an-Nasa'i 995)

To love this surah is to love its subject — the Oneness and perfection of Allah; and when Allah loves a servant for that, Paradise becomes his home.

5.Making Surah Al-Ikhlas Part of Your Day

Here are simple, authentic ways the Prophet ﷺ himself used it:

Morning and evening. Recite Qul Huwa Allahu Ahad with Surah Al-Falaq and Surah An-Nas thrice in the morning and thrice in the evening — "they will suffice you against everything" (a narration reported in Sunan al-Tirmidhi and Sunan Abi Dawud). Three short surahs, three times — a shield for the whole day.

In your salah. The Prophet ﷺ frequently recited Surah Al-Ikhlas in his prayers, including witr. Recite it in your sunnah prayers with presence of heart: Allah, the One, the Eternal Refuge.

Before sleep. The Prophet ﷺ would recite the three Quls before sleeping and pass his hands over his body. Under two minutes — protection and reward for the whole night.

Start today: recite Surah Al-Ikhlas three times right now, slowly, thinking of its meaning — and earn the reward of a third of the Quran before your next meal.
