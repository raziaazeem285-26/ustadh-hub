Riba-Free Pakistan: The Road to Interest-Free Banking by 2028

Daily Life

Few topics touch a Muslim's everyday life more directly than money — and few Quranic prohibitions are stated more forcefully than that of riba (interest/usury). In Pakistan, the issue has moved from pulpits to policy: the constitution now sets January 1, 2028 as the deadline to eliminate riba from the country. Just this week, nearly 1,000 Shariah scholars gathered in Bahrain to discuss the very standards shaping that transition. What does it all mean for you? Let us start with the foundations.

1.What Exactly Is Riba?

Riba is any guaranteed extra charged over a loan — interest. The Quran permits trade but forbids riba: "Allah has permitted trade and has forbidden riba" (Quran 2:275). Trade carries risk and effort; riba multiplies the lender's money off the borrower's need, without any risk or work. That is the essence of the prohibition: profit must come from real exchange, not from exploiting debt.

Islamic finance replaces interest with real, asset-backed contracts — profit-sharing (mudarabah), partnership (musharakah), cost-plus sale (murabaha), and leasing (ijarah). The difference is not just a name change: in a valid contract, the financier shares the risk of the venture rather than guaranteeing a return regardless of the outcome.

2.Why the Prohibition Is So Severe

The Quran treats riba unlike almost any other financial sin. Allah commands the believers: "O you who have believed, fear Allah and give up what remains of interest, if you should be believers. And if you do not, then be informed of a war against you from Allah and His Messenger" (Quran 2:278–279). A declaration of war from Allah — this is a warning attached to no other economic act. And Allah promises the opposite for charity: "Allah destroys riba and gives increase for charities" (Quran 2:276).

The Prophet ﷺ was equally emphatic. Jabir (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ "cursed the one who consumes riba, the one who pays it, the one who records it, and the two witnesses — and he said: they are all equal (in sin)" (Sahih Muslim 1598). Not only the borrower or the lender — everyone knowingly involved carries the same weight. This is why the question of interest touches every salary account, savings scheme, and loan we sign.

"O you who have believed, fear Allah and give up what remains of interest, if you should be believers." — Quran 2:278

3.Pakistan's Journey Toward Riba-Free Banking

Pakistan's riba-free journey is not a new idea. In April 2022, the Federal Shariat Court gave the government five years to build an interest-free financial system, ruling that riba — including interest on the government's own domestic and foreign loans — is completely forbidden in Islam.

Then came the constitutional lock: the 26th Constitutional Amendment (2024) changed Article 38(f) of the Constitution from "eliminate riba as early as possible" to a firm deadline — "eliminate riba completely before the first day of January, two thousand twenty-eight."

The machinery is already moving. The State Bank of Pakistan has adopted 20 Shariah standards of the Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions (AAOIFI); Islamic banks now hold about 28% of deposits and 38% of financing in the banking system; and the government has published a strategy paper ("Post-2027 Financial System in Pakistan") outlining how conventional financing will be replaced by Shariah-compliant instruments as it matures after December 2027. This week, around 1,000 Shariah scholars met in Bahrain for AAOIFI's 24th annual conference (October 11–12), working through the practical questions of the transition — reconciling Islamic standards with national law, estimating the true cost of loans, and handling contracts fairly.

4.What This Means for Ordinary Muslims

You do not need to be an economist to act on the Quranic command. The riba question lives in ordinary decisions: a savings account that earns interest, a loan taken to buy a phone, a credit card bill paid late. Every riba-based arrangement we sign today is something the Quran tells us to leave (Quran 2:278).

Note this clearly: no blog article can give you a fatwa. Scholars differ on the details of individual products, and only a qualified scholar can judge your specific situation. But two principles are universally agreed: (1) interest on loans and deposits is riba and is forbidden; (2) a Muslim should never sign a financial contract he has not read and understood. Ask your bank exactly what charges apply and whether the product is certified as Shariah-compliant — and verify claims with a scholar you trust.

5.Three Things You Can Do Today

Learn the basics. Spend one evening understanding the difference between interest-based and Islamic contracts. Knowing the words — mudarabah, murabaha, ijarah — turns bank brochures from confusion into clarity, and Allah rewards seeking beneficial knowledge.

Audit your own dealings. List every account, loan, and card you hold. For each one that involves interest, plan — practically and without panic — how to close it or switch to a Shariah-compliant alternative. Small steps, taken sincerely, carry great reward.

Make dua and spread awareness. Dua is a weapon of the believer — ask Allah for halal rizq free of riba (for more on rizq and relief, see Istighfar: The Forgotten Door to Rizq and Relief). Then share this article: many Muslims have simply never been told that Pakistan is on a real, dated path to a riba-free system.
