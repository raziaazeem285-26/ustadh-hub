Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (RA): The Closest Companion of the Prophet ﷺ

Seerah & Stories

When the Prophet ﷺ passed away, the community stood at the edge of collapse — and the man who held it together had stood beside him from the very first day. Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (RA): the first adult man to believe, the companion in the cave, the one who gave everything and asked for nothing back. A merchant, a friend, a believer — the closest of all men to the Prophet ﷺ.

1.The Merchant of Makkah

His name was Abdullah ibn Abi Quhafa, of the Quraysh tribe of Taym, born in Makkah about two years after the Prophet ﷺ — a contemporary, a neighbour, and a friend long before prophethood began. A successful cloth merchant, wealthy and respected, he was called al-Ateeq by the people of Makkah — the noble one, the freed one. The scholars differ on why he carried the name; what is agreed is that it marked a man of standing, and that he spent his standing well.

The biographers record that he never bowed to an idol and never drank wine, even in the days of ignorance. While Makkah's wealthy men spent their fortunes on idols and drink, Abu Bakr kept his wealth and his dignity clean — and when Islam came, both were ready to be spent in its service. He was the Prophet's friend before the revelation, and the two men were known in Makkah for the same thing: truthfulness.

2.The First to Believe

When the Prophet ﷺ came down from the Cave of Hira with the first revelation, the first to believe was his wife Khadijah (RA). And then, the historians record, the first free adult man to accept Islam was Abu Bakr — at once, without a moment's hesitation, without asking for a sign or a delay. Ali (RA) was first among children, Zayd ibn Harithah (RA) first among freed slaves — but the first man of Makkah's free society to say "I believe" was Abu Bakr.

Then came the night that gave him his name. When the Prophet ﷺ told the people of the Night Journey — to Jerusalem and the heavens in a single night — the Quraysh mocked him, and ran to Abu Bakr hoping he would doubt too. His answer, as the early biographers record it, was simple: "If he said it, then it is true." From that day he was as-Siddiq — the Truthful Confirmer, the one who confirms the truth the moment he hears it. A title no one else in the Ummah has ever carried.

3.The Cave: "Do Not Grieve; Allah Is with Us"

Thirteen years later, when the Prophet ﷺ left Makkah for Madinah, he chose one companion — Abu Bakr. For three nights they hid in the Cave of Thawr while the Quraysh searched the hills with a price on their heads. Abu Bakr whispered, in fear for the Prophet's life, "If any of them should look under his feet, he would see us." And the Prophet ﷺ answered with words that have steadied believers ever since: "O Abu Bakr! What do you think of two, the third of whom is Allah?" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3653). Two men in a cave, an army outside — and the third with them was Allah.

The Quran itself honours this night. It does not name Abu Bakr — but it names his companionship:

إِذۡ هُمَا فِی ٱلۡغَارِ إِذۡ یَقُولُ لِصَـٰحِبِهِۦ لَا تَحۡزَنۡ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ مَعَنَا

Quran 9:40 — "when they were in the cave and he said to his companion, 'Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us.'"

"The second of the two" — that is Abu Bakr, counted by Allah alongside His Messenger. "His companion" — sahibihi — the word the Quran chose for him. No other Companion is so honoured in the Quran. Allah sent His tranquillity down upon them, and the searchers turned away from the mouth of the cave. For the full story of that journey, read The Hijrah: A Journey That Changed History.

4.Giving Everything Away

Abu Bakr's wealth had one purpose after Islam: to be spent. The seerah accounts record that he bought and freed Bilal (RA) — tortured on the hot stones of Makkah for saying "Ahad, Ahad" (One, One) — paying far above a slave's worth, and setting him free for Allah alone. He freed several others the same way, and his fortune, built over a lifetime of trade, melted away in the service of the oppressed. For Bilal's full story, read Hazrat Bilal (RA): The Voice of Faith That Torture Could Not Silence.

The most famous gift came at Tabuk, when the Prophet ﷺ called the Companions to give for the expedition. Umar (RA) brought half of everything he owned, thinking this might finally be the day he surpassed Abu Bakr. Then Abu Bakr arrived — with everything he had. The Prophet ﷺ asked him, "O Abu Bakr! What did you leave for your family?" And he replied, "I left Allah and His Messenger for them." Umar said, "By Allah, I will never be able to beat him to something" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3675, Hasan Sahih). Half a fortune is a remarkable gift; everything is something else entirely. It was not recklessness — it was certainty: Allah and His Messenger were enough.

لَن تَنَالُوا۟ ٱلۡبِرَّ حَتَّىٰ تُنفِقُوا۟ مِمَّا تُحِبُّونَۚ

Quran 3:92 — "Never will you attain righteousness until you spend from that which you love."

That verse is the measure of his giving: charity is counted by what it costs you, not by the amount — and Abu Bakr gave what he loved most, until nothing was left but himself. For the full teaching on charity, read The Virtue of Charity: How Sadaqah Lifts Lives and Multiplies Blessings.

5.The Final Days: The Prayer and the Door

In his last illness, the Prophet ﷺ could no longer lead the prayer. He said, "Tell Abu Bakr to lead the prayer." Aisha (RA) objected — her father was a softhearted man who wept when he recited the Quran, and the people would not hear him. The Prophet ﷺ said it again, and a third time: "Tell Abu Bakr to lead the people in prayer" (Sahih al-Bukhari 678). So Abu Bakr stood in the Prophet's place, in the Prophet's lifetime, and led the Ummah in prayer — the first man to do so.

Then, in his final sermon, the Prophet ﷺ gave the sign Abu Bakr alone understood at once. He said: "Allah gave a choice to one of His slaves, either to choose this world or what is with Him in the Hereafter — and he chose the latter." Abu Bakr wept; he knew the slave was the Prophet himself. Then the Prophet said: "Abu Bakr has favoured me much with his property and company. If I were to take a Khalil from mankind, I would certainly have taken Abu Bakr — but the Islamic brotherhood and friendship is sufficient. Close all the gates in the mosque except that of Abu Bakr" (Sahih al-Bukhari 466). One door left open in the mosque of the Prophet — Abu Bakr's door. It is open to this day.

Ali (RA) himself settled the question. When his son asked, "Who are the best people after Allah's Messenger?" he said, "Abu Bakr." Asked "Who then?" he said, "Then Umar" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3671) — and then, with the humility that defined his own life, added, "I am only an ordinary person."

6.The First Caliph

When the Prophet ﷺ died, the Ummah faced its first crisis: who would lead? The Companions chose Abu Bakr, and he stood before them with words that no ruler before or since has bettered — as the histories record his first address: "I have been given authority over you, and I am not the best of you. If I do well, help me; and if I do wrong, set me right." He ruled a little over two years — the hardest the young Ummah would ever face. Tribes apostatised, false prophets rose, and some refused zakat. Umar (RA) urged caution — how could they fight people who said the shahadah? Abu Bakr answered, "By Allah! I will fight those who differentiate between the prayer and the zakat... By Allah! If they refuse to pay me even a she-kid which they used to pay at the time of Allah's Messenger, I would fight with them for withholding it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1399). Umar later admitted Allah had opened Abu Bakr's chest to the decision, and that it was right. The softhearted man who wept in prayer held the Ummah together with iron.

His greatest gift came after the Battle of Yamama, when many who had memorised the Quran were killed. Umar urged him to gather the Quran into one book; Abu Bakr hesitated — the Prophet ﷺ had not done it — then agreed, charging Zayd ibn Thabit (RA) with the task. Zayd collected it from palm stalks, thin stones, and the memories of men, until the whole Quran stood in one manuscript (Sahih al-Bukhari 4986). The copy you hold today traces back to that decision.

His mercy matched his firmness. When Mistah, a relative Abu Bakr had long supported, joined those who slandered Aisha (RA), Abu Bakr swore he would never support him again. Then the verse came down: "Let them pardon and overlook... do you not love that Allah should forgive you?" (Quran 24:22, in meaning) — and the commentators report that Abu Bakr said, "Yes, by Allah, I love that Allah should forgive me," and restored Mistah's support at once. The man who would fight for zakat would not let anger stop his charity. He died in 13 AH, at sixty-three — the same age as the Prophet ﷺ — and was buried beside him in Madinah, under the dome that is green today.

7.Five Lessons for Us Today

1. Believe without delay. Abu Bakr did not wait for proof, for a second opinion, or for the crowd to move first. When the truth is clear, the believer acts. How many of us are still waiting — to pray, to learn the Quran, to change a habit — for a sign that has already come?

2. Loyalty is proven in hardship, not in comfort. Anyone can stand beside a friend in victory. Abu Bakr stood in a cave, an army outside and no way out, and heard: "Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us." Be the friend who stays when the cave is dark.

3. Spend what you love. "Never will you attain righteousness until you spend from that which you love" (Quran 3:92). Charity is counted by the sacrifice, not the amount. Give the thing you would rather keep — that is the gift Allah counts.

4. Gentleness is strength. The man who wept while reciting the Quran held an empire together and refused to compromise on zakat. Softness in worship and firmness in principle are not opposites; in Abu Bakr they were the same man.

5. Leave something that outlives you. Abu Bakr's caliphate lasted barely two years, but the Quran he gathered still guides the world fourteen centuries later. Your legacy is not your wealth or your title — it is what keeps working after you are gone. Build that, and build it now.

Abu Bakr (RA) asked for nothing, gave everything, and is remembered as the best of this Ummah after the Prophets. The door of his house in the Prophet's mosque is still open — and so is the path he walked: believe at once, stand firm in the dark, give what you love, leave something that outlives you. If this story moved you, share it — and say a prayer for the man who never left the Prophet's side.
