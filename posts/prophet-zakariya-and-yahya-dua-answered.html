Prophet Zakariya and Yahya: A Dua Answered in Old Age

R Razia Azeem · 16 Nov, 2026

Seerah & Stories

An old man with white hair and a barren wife prayed in secret — and Allah answered him with a son whose name had never been given to anyone before. This is the story of Prophet Zakariya (AS) and his son Yahya (AS): a dua made in the quiet of the prayer chamber.

1.The Man Who Prayed in Secret

Surah Maryam opens with a single line that sets the whole tone: "A mention of the mercy of your Lord to His servant Zakariya" (Quran 19:2, in meaning). His story is introduced as mercy. And the first thing we learn about his prayer is how quiet it was: "when he called upon his Lord, a call in secret" (Quran 19:3, in meaning). An old man, alone with his Lord.

His dua is one of the most honest prayers in the Quran. "My Lord, indeed my bones have weakened, and my head has filled with white, and never have I been in my supplication to You, my Lord, unhappy" (Quran 19:4, in meaning). He names his condition and adds: I have never once prayed to You and been left unhappy.

قَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّی وَهَنَ ٱلۡعَظۡمُ مِنِّی وَٱشۡتَعَلَ ٱلرَّأۡسُ شَیۡبࣰا وَلَمۡ أَكُنۢ بِدُعَاۤىِٕكَ رَبِّ شَقِیࣰّا

Quran 19:4 — "My Lord, indeed my bones have weakened, and my head has filled with white, and never have I been in my supplication to You, my Lord, unhappy."

What did he ask for? He feared for those who would come after him, and his wife was barren: "And indeed, I fear the successors after me, and my wife has been barren, so give me from Yourself an heir who will inherit me and inherit from the family of Ya'qub. And make him, my Lord, pleasing [to You]" (Quran 19:5-6, in meaning). He asked for a successor — someone to carry the trust of prophethood after him, the inheritance of prophethood and knowledge the commentators describe — and he added the condition that matters most: make him pleasing to You.

2.The Guardian of Maryam

To understand why Zakariya's dua was answered, the Quran takes us first to the prayer chamber where he served. Years before his own son was born, Zakariya was given charge of Maryam. Her mother had vowed the child in her womb to Allah's service and sought His protection for her and her offspring from Satan (Quran 3:35-36, in meaning). Allah accepted Maryam with a beautiful acceptance — "and Zakariya took charge of her" (Quran 3:37, in meaning). The commentators say his wife was Maryam's maternal aunt.

And there, in the mihrab, he saw something that changed his prayer. "Every time Zakariya entered upon her in the prayer chamber, he found with her provision. He said, 'O Maryam, from where is this for you?' She said, 'It is from Allah. Indeed, Allah provides for whom He wills without account'" (Quran 3:37, in meaning). The commentators say it was fruit out of season. Watching this, something shifted in his heart: if Allah provides for Maryam without means, then Allah can give him a child without means. For the full story of Maryam's son, read Prophet Isa (AS): The Miraculous Birth and the Promised Return.

3.The Prayer in the Mihrab

The Quran ties the two moments together with a single word: hunālika — right there. "At that, Zakariya called upon his Lord, saying, 'My Lord, grant me from Yourself a good offspring. Indeed, You are the Hearer of supplication'" (Quran 3:38, in meaning). Right there, in the prayer chamber, he prayed. He asked for a good offspring — dhurriyyatan tayyibah — a pure, righteous child.

هُنَالِكَ دَعَا زَكَرِیَّا رَبَّهُۥۖ قَالَ رَبِّ هَبۡ لِی مِن لَّدُنكَ ذُرِّیَّةࣰ طَیِّبَةًۖ إِنَّكَ سَمِیعُ ٱلدُّعَاۤءِ

Quran 3:38 — "At that, Zakariya called upon his Lord, saying, 'My Lord, grant me from Yourself a good offspring. Indeed, You are the Hearer of supplication.'"

The answer came while he was still standing in prayer. "So the angels called him while he was standing in prayer in the chamber, 'Indeed, Allah gives you good tidings of Yahya, confirming a Word from Allah and [who will be] honourable, abstaining [from women], and a prophet from among the righteous'" (Quran 3:39, in meaning). Four qualities: he would confirm the Word from Allah — Isa (AS) — be a chief among his people, chaste, and a prophet among the righteous. The glad tidings arrived where the prayer was made, while he still stood in the mihrab.

4.A Name Never Given Before

In Surah Maryam the announcement is given with a detail found nowhere else in the Quran: "O Zakariya, indeed We give you good tidings of a boy whose name will be Yahya. We have not assigned to any before [this] name" (Quran 19:7, in meaning). No one had ever been called Yahya. The name comes from the root for life — hayy, "he lives" — given to a child born to parents whom life had passed by.

یَـٰزَكَرِیَّاۤ إِنَّا نُبَشِّرُكَ بِغُلَـٰمٍ ٱسۡمُهُۥ یَحۡیَىٰ لَمۡ نَجۡعَل لَّهُۥ مِن قَبۡلُ سَمِیࣰّا

Quran 19:7 — "O Zakariya, indeed We give you good tidings of a boy whose name will be Yahya. We have not assigned to any before [this] name."

Zakariya's reply is the natural one: "My Lord, how will I have a boy when my wife has been barren and I have reached extreme old age?" (Quran 19:8, in meaning). The answer: "Thus [it will be]; your Lord says, 'It is easy for Me, for I created you before, while you were nothing'" (Quran 19:9, in meaning). The One who made you from nothing can make a child from an old man and a barren wife.

Zakariya asked for a sign, and the sign was silence: "Your sign is that you will not speak to the people for three nights, [being] sound" (Quran 19:10, in meaning) — three days, in the other telling, except by gesture, "and remember your Lord much and exalt [Him] in the evening and the morning" (Quran 3:41, in meaning). He was sound, able to speak — simply told to hold his tongue from people and fill those three days with remembrance instead. The sign of an answered dua was not a celebration. It was three days of dhikr. Surah al-Anbiya tells the same story in two verses: Zakariya called, "My Lord, do not leave me alone, while You are the best of inheritors" — and Allah responded, giving him Yahya and amending his wife for him (Quran 21:89-90, in meaning). Do not leave me alone — and Allah answered by giving him a family. For another dua answered from the depths, read Yunus's Dua: Answered in the Darkest Place.

5.The Boy Who Took the Book with Strength

The Quran tells us one thing about him as a boy, and it is enough: "O Yahya, take the Scripture with determination. And We gave him judgement while [yet] a child" (Quran 19:12, in meaning). He was given wisdom while still a boy — sabiyy — and he took the Book with strength.

Then the Quran draws his character in four strokes: "And affection from Us and purity, and he was fearing of Allah and dutiful to his parents, and he was not a disobedient tyrant" (Quran 19:13-14, in meaning). Compassion, purity, God-consciousness, kindness to parents — and neither arrogance nor disobedience. The child whose father had prayed for a pleasing son (19:6) grew into exactly that.

And Allah Himself greets him for his whole life: "And peace be upon him the day he was born and the day he dies and the day he is raised alive" (Quran 19:15, in meaning). The same three days of peace are spoken by Isa (AS) about himself: "And peace is on me the day I was born and the day I will die and the day I am raised alive" (Quran 19:33, in meaning). The Quran places them together among the righteous: "And Zakariya and Yahya and Isa and Ilyas — all of them were of the righteous" (Quran 6:85, in meaning).

"And peace be upon him the day he was born and the day he dies and the day he is raised alive." — Quran 19:15

The Quran does not tell us how Yahya died. Reports in the books of tafsir say he was killed by the ruler of his time, but the Quran is silent on the manner — it only promises him peace on the day he dies. We say what the Quran says, and mark the rest as reports, not revelation.

6.Yahya's Five Commands

One hadith shows us Yahya as a grown prophet, teaching his people. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said: "Indeed, Allah commanded Yahya, the son of Zakariya, with five commandments to abide by, and to command the Children of Israel to abide by them. But he delayed, so Isa said to him, 'Either you command them, or I shall.' Yahya said he feared the earth might swallow him if Isa went first, so he gathered the people in Jerusalem, and they filled the mosque" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2863, graded Sahih, in meaning).

The five commands are a complete way of life, each carried by a parable. First, worship Allah and associate nothing with Him — like a man whose servant hands the profits to someone else. Second, prayer — and when you pray, do not turn away, for Allah faces His servant in prayer as long as he does not turn away. Third, fasting — like a man carrying a sachet of musk: the breath of the fasting person is more pleasant to Allah than its scent. Fourth, charity — like a man captured by his enemies who ransoms himself with a little or a lot. Fifth, remembrance of Allah — like a man fleeing an enemy until he reaches a protected fortress; nothing protects a servant from Satan like the remembrance of Allah. The boy who took the Book with strength became the man who taught his people to live by it.

7.What Their Story Teaches Us

1. It is never too late. Old age and barrenness are real barriers, but they are not barriers for Allah. "It is easy for Me" (19:9) is the answer to every dua that looks impossible on paper.

2. Pray in secret. The dua that changed this family was a call in secret (19:3). The quietest duas are often the most sincere — and sincerity is what Allah answers. For the conditions that open the door of accepted dua, read 5 Keys That Open the Door of Accepted Dua.

3. Provision follows trust. Zakariya's dua was born watching Maryam receive provision without means. When you see Allah provide for someone else without means, let it enlarge your own dua.

4. Ask for righteous children, not just children. He asked for a good offspring (3:38) and a successor who would be pleasing to Allah (19:6). Allah gave him both.

5. Children are a trust, raised on the Book. Yahya was given wisdom while still a boy. A child's character is built on what the parents put in their hands.

6. The sign of an answered dua is more remembrance, not less. Zakariya's sign was three days of silence from people, filled with remembering Allah much. When Allah answers you, remember Him more.

An old man prayed in secret for a successor, and Allah gave him a son with a name no one had ever carried — a boy who took the Book with strength and received Allah's own greeting of peace at birth, at death, and at the resurrection. If you have a dua you have carried for years, carry it the way Zakariya did: quietly, honestly, and with a lifetime of answered prayers behind it.
