Surah Al-Waqi'ah: Three Groups on the Day

R Razia Azeem · 9 Nov, 2026

When the Event befalls, mankind will stand in three groups — the foremost, the people of the right, and the people of the left. Which group are you preparing for?

In this article

  2. Three Groups on One Day
  3. The Foremost: Nearest to Allah
  4. The People of the Right
  5. The People of the Left — and Four Proofs
  6. The Soul at the Throat — and Tonight's Step

Quran

There is a surah that begins with a word you cannot argue with: "When the Inevitable Event befalls" (Quran 56:1, in meaning). No "if". No "perhaps". Al-Waqi'ah — the Event — is coming, and when it comes there will be no one to deny it, no one to delay it, and no one to hide from it. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) once said that this surah, along with a few others about the Day, had turned his hair grey: "Hud, Al-Waqi'ah, Al-Mursalat, and 'Amma have made me gray" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3297).

Surah Al-Waqi'ah is the 56th surah of the Quran — Makki, 96 verses, in Juz 27. It does not argue about the Day; it describes it, in pictures so clear you can almost see them. And what makes it different from most surahs about the Day is its structure: it does not divide people into two groups — believers and disbelievers — but into three. The foremost, the people of the right, and the people of the left. This article walks through the surah as it walks through that Day — and ends with an honest word about the famous poverty hadith.

1.When the Event Befalls

The surah opens with a scene of the earth itself coming apart: "When the Inevitable Event befalls, there is, at its occurrence, no denial. It will bring low [some] and exalt [others]. When the earth is shaken with convulsion, and the mountains are broken down, crumbling, and become dust dispersing" (Quran 56:1-6, in meaning). The mountains — the very picture of permanence — become scattered dust. Nothing that looks permanent is permanent. That is the first lesson, aimed at us: the job, the house, the body, the name — all of it is dust waiting for its hour.

And the Event "will bring low [some] and exalt [others]" (Quran 56:3). The people who looked low in this world will be raised high, and the people who looked high will be brought low. The Day reverses the world's rankings completely. That is why the Prophet (peace be upon him) carried these surahs so heavily — not because he feared for himself, but because he knew what they meant for his Ummah. A narration reports that he would recite Al-Waqi'ah and surahs like it in Fajr — he began his days with the Day.

2.Three Groups on One Day

Then the surah draws its map of mankind: "And you will be [divided into] three groups: the companions of the right — what are the companions of the right? And the companions of the left — what are the companions of the left? And the foremost are the foremost. Those are the ones brought near [to Allah]" (Quran 56:7-11, in meaning).

وَكُنْتُمْ أَزْوَاجًا ثَلَاثَةً ۝ فَأَصْحَابُ الْمَيْمَنَةِ مَا أَصْحَابُ الْمَيْمَنَةِ ۝ وَأَصْحَابُ الْمَشْأَمَةِ مَا أَصْحَابُ الْمَشْأَمَةِ ۝ وَالسَّابِقُونَ السَّابِقُونَ ۝ أُولَٰئِكَ الْمُقَرَّبُونَ

Quran 56:7-11

Notice how the surah asks its questions. "What are the companions of the right?" — asked to glorify them. "What are the companions of the left?" — asked to warn you. And the third group is named twice: "the foremost are the foremost" — those who raced ahead in this world will be foremost on that Day, "the ones brought near to Allah". Most surahs speak of two groups; this one speaks of three, because among the saved there are ranks — and the surah wants you to aim for the highest.

3.The Foremost: Nearest to Allah

The surah describes the foremost first, and in the most detail: "Those are the ones brought near [to Allah], in the Gardens of Pleasure. A multitude from the former peoples, and a few from the later peoples. On thrones woven [with ornament], reclining on them, facing each other. There will circulate among them young boys made eternal, with cups, pitchers, and a cup [of wine] from a flowing spring — no headache will they have from it, nor will they be intoxicated — and fruit of what they select, and the meat of fowl, of whatever they desire. And [for them are] fair women with large, [beautiful] eyes, the likenesses of pearls well-protected — a reward for what they used to do. They will not hear therein ill speech or commission of sin — only a saying: 'Peace, peace'" (Quran 56:11-26, in meaning).

The foremost are not described by wealth or lineage, but by nearness: "the ones brought near to Allah". Their garden is a place where even the speech is clean — no idle talk, no sinful speech, only peace. And note the numbers: "a multitude from the former peoples, and a few from the later peoples" (Quran 56:13-14). The foremost were many among the early generations and few among the later ones. That is not a reason to despair; it is a reason to race. Who are the foremost? Those first to faith, first to prayer, first to charity, first to repentance — the ones who did not wait to be told twice. The companion surah before this one, Surah Ar-Rahman, asks thirty-one times which of your Lord's favours you will deny; Al-Waqi'ah shows what those favours look like for the ones who raced for them.

4.The People of the Right

Then the surah turns to the second group, the people of the right: "And the companions of the right — what are the companions of the right? [They will be] among lote trees with thorns removed, and banana trees layered [with fruit], and shade extended, and water poured out, and fruit, abundant [and varied], neither limited [to season] nor forbidden, and [upon] beds raised high" (Quran 56:27-34, in meaning).

And here is the beautiful contrast with the foremost: "A multitude from the former peoples, and a multitude from the later peoples" (Quran 56:39-40). The foremost were few from the later generations — but the people of the right are many, from every age. The right is the broad road. It is the ordinary believer: the one who prayed, gave, repented, kept going, and did not give up. If the foremost are the racers, the people of the right are the ones who finished the race — and the surah makes room for multitudes of them, from the later generations too. That includes you, if you keep walking. The fruit is "neither limited to season nor forbidden" — in this world every blessing ends; in the Garden, nothing is held back.

5.The People of the Left — and Four Proofs

The third group is described last, and briefly: "And the companions of the left — what are the companions of the left? [They will be] in scorching fire and scalding water, and a shade of black smoke, neither cool nor refreshing. Indeed they were, before that, indulging in affluence, and they used to persist in the great violation, and they used to say, 'When we die and become dust and bones, are we indeed to be resurrected? And our forefathers [as well]?'" (Quran 56:41-48, in meaning).

Their crime is named plainly: they lived in luxury and persisted in denial. And the surah answers their question — "are we indeed to be resurrected?" — with four proofs, each one drawn from things they could see every day. First, creation itself: "We have created you, so why do you not believe?" (Quran 56:57, in meaning). Second, the seed: you sow it in the earth — who makes it grow? "Is it you who makes it grow, or are We the Grower?" (Quran 56:63-64). Third, the water you drink: "Is it you who brought it down from the clouds, or are We the Bringer? If We willed, We could make it bitter" (Quran 56:68-70). Fourth, the fire you kindle: "Is it you who produced the tree that fuels it, or are We the Producer? We have made it a reminder and a provision for the travellers" (Quran 56:71-73). If Allah can create you once from nothing, grow a plant from a dead seed, send sweet water from the sky, and kindle fire from green wood — raising you again is no harder for Him. Between the proofs, the surah swears by the stars: "Then I swear by the setting of the stars — and indeed, it is a great oath, if you only knew — that indeed, it is a noble Quran" (Quran 56:75-77). The night sky above you is the surah's own witness. The virtue of reciting the Quran — ten rewards for every letter — is the reason this surah is worth reading even before you understand all of it.

6.The Soul at the Throat — and Tonight's Step

The surah ends where every life ends: "Then why, when the soul reaches the throat, and you are at that time looking on — and Our angels are nearer to him than you, but you do not see — then why do you not, if you are not to be recompensed, return it, if you should be truthful?" (Quran 56:83-87, in meaning). We all stand around the dying person and watch, helpless; the soul leaves, and no one in the room can call it back. That is the proof no one can argue with.

Then the three endings, one for each group: "And if the deceased was of those brought near to Allah, then [for him is] rest and bounty and a garden of pleasure. And if he was of the companions of the right, then [the angels will say], 'Peace for you; [you are] from the companions of the right.' But if he was of the deniers [who were] astray, then [for him is] accommodation of scalding water and burning in Hellfire" (Quran 56:88-94, in meaning). And the surah closes with certainty: "Indeed, this is the true certainty. So exalt the name of your Lord, the Most Great" (Quran 56:95-96, in meaning).

"So exalt the name of your Lord, the Most Great." — Quran 56:96

فَسَبِّحْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الْعَظِيمِ

Quran 56:96

One honest word is needed here, about a hadith many people quote with this surah. A widely shared report, attributed to Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (may Allah be pleased with him) and recorded by al-Bayhaqi in Shu'ab al-Iman, says: "Whoever recites Surah Al-Waqi'ah every night will never be afflicted by poverty." The scholars differ over this report: Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Abu Hatim, ad-Daraqutni and al-Albani graded its chain weak, while other scholars graded one of its chains acceptable. So it should not be presented as a guaranteed promise from the Prophet (peace be upon him) — and no fixed count, such as seven times, is established by any authentic hadith at all. What is established is the general reward of reciting the Quran — and the surah's own content, which is itself the protection: a person who remembers the Day does not chase the world the way the people of the left did. Read it tonight after Isha — not as a spell for wealth, but as a reminder of where you are going. One surah a night: that is how the foremost were made. If you want your children to learn to read the Quran themselves, with a teacher who knows these surahs by heart, you can begin with a free trial class of one-to-one online Quran teaching. And if you recite a surah every night before sleep, Surah Al-Mulk is the one the Prophet (peace be upon him) would not sleep without.

Al-Waqi'ah ends where it began — with the greatness of Allah. The Event is inevitable; the groups are real; and the choice of which group you stand in is made today, in the small races: first to pray, first to give, first to say sorry, first to turn back. Tonight, read it once — and at the last verse, say it slowly: SubhanAllah, exalted is the name of your Lord, the Most Great.
