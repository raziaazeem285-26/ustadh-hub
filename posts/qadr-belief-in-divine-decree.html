Qadr: The Decree That Frees Your Heart

R Razia Azeem · 12 Nov, 2026

Allah wrote every decree before the heavens existed — and still told you to act. How belief in qadr turns anxiety into peace.

In this article
  1. Qadr: The Sixth Pillar of Faith
  2. Written Before the Heavens and the Earth
  3. Nothing Reaches You Except What Was Written
  4. Adam and Musa: A Debate About Decree
  5. Then Why Should I Act?
  6. Living With Qadr This Week

Daily Life

You did everything right. You studied, you applied, you prayed — and the door still closed. Or the opposite: something wonderful arrived that you never planned or asked for. In both moments the same question rises: was this written for me, or did I make it happen?

Islam answers with a single word that holds both truths at once: qadr — the divine decree. It is the sixth pillar of faith, and the most misunderstood: some hear it as fate and give up trying; others treat it as a puzzle and argue until their faith is shaken. This article stays with what the Quran and the authentic hadith actually say — and ends with five small ways to live with qadr this week, so it becomes what it was meant to be: not a prison, but a freedom.

1.Qadr: The Sixth Pillar of Faith

When the angel Jibril came to the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) and asked, "What is faith?" he answered: "That you believe in Allah, His angels, His Books, His Messengers, the Last Day, and that you believe in the divine decree — its good and its evil" (Sahih Muslim 8). It is not an optional extra; it is part of what faith is.

What does it mean to believe in qadr? The scholars explain it in four levels. First, Allah's knowledge: He knows everything that was, is, and will be — "not a leaf falls but that He knows it" (Quran 6:59). Second, the writing: Allah recorded it all in the Preserved Tablet — "in a Preserved Tablet" (Quran 85:22). Third, Allah's will: nothing happens except by His will — "And you do not will except that Allah wills" (Quran 81:28-29). Fourth, Allah's creation: He is the Creator of all things, while the servant still chooses and is still accountable.

Notice what is not in that definition: it does not mean Allah forces you to sin and then punishes you for it, and it does not mean your choices are an illusion. The Quran holds both sides together: your will is real — "for whoever wills among you to take a straight path" (Quran 81:28) — and Allah's will is above it. How the two fit together is a mystery the scholars warn us not to chase into argument. What matters in daily life is not solving the paradox — it is trusting the One who wrote it.

2.Written Before the Heavens and the Earth

The scale of the decree is what first takes the breath away. Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As reported that the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: "Allah wrote down the decrees of creation fifty thousand years before He created the heavens and the earth, as His Throne was upon water" (Sahih Muslim 2653). Fifty thousand years before the sky or the earth existed, before a single human being worried about any of it — the provision, the lifespan, the meetings, the losses, the whole story was already written.

إِنَّا كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقْنَاهُ بِقَدَرٍ

Quran 54:49 — "Indeed, all things We created with predestination."

"Indeed, all things We created with predestination" (Quran 54:49). The verse does not say that some things are decreed and the rest are left to chance. It says all things — every leaf, every job offer, every illness, every child, every delay. The word qadar itself means measure: everything has its exact measure, its exact time, its exact place. Nothing in your life is a rounding error.

The Pen wrote everything that will occur until the Day of Resurrection — and then, the Prophet said, the pens were lifted and the pages dried. The writing is finished. Your job is not to rewrite it; your job is to live inside it well.

3.Nothing Reaches You Except What Was Written

The most famous verse of qadr is also the shortest answer to fear: "Say: Never will we be struck except by what Allah has decreed for us; He is our protector. And upon Allah let the believers rely" (Quran 9:51). Say it slowly: nothing will happen to us except what Allah has written for us. Not what our enemies planned — what Allah wrote, as our Protector, our Mawla.

قُل لَّن يُصِيبَنَا إِلَّا مَا كَتَبَ اللَّهُ لَنَا هُوَ مَوْلَانَا ۚ وَعَلَى اللَّهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ

Quran 9:51 — "Say, 'Never will we be struck except by what Allah has decreed for us; He is our protector.' And upon Allah let the believers rely."

The Quran gives the same truth a second form, for the days when something is taken from you: "No disaster strikes upon the earth or among yourselves except that it is in a register before We bring it into being — indeed that, for Allah, is easy — so that you may not grieve over what has escaped you, nor rejoice over what He has given you" (Quran 57:22-23). Allah gives the reason plainly: so that you may not grieve. The decree was written before the disaster existed — so do not break over what was never yours to keep, nor swell with pride over what was never yours to claim.

"Know that if the whole nation gathered together to benefit you with something, they would not benefit you except with what Allah had already written for you. And if they gathered together to harm you with something, they could not harm you except with what Allah had already written against you. The pens have been lifted and the pages have dried." — Jami at-Tirmidhi 2516 (Hasan Sahih)

That is the hadith the Prophet spoke to a young Ibn Abbas (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2516, graded Hasan Sahih). The whole nation gathered to help you cannot give you one thing Allah did not write for you; gathered to harm you, they cannot take one thing Allah did not write against you. The pens are lifted. The pages are dry. No enemy can edit your decree — and that is not a threat. It is the greatest protection a believer owns.

4.Adam and Musa: A Debate About Decree

Even the Prophets asked about qadr. Musa (peace be upon him) said to Adam (peace be upon him): "You are our father; you disappointed us and caused us to be expelled from Paradise." Adam replied: "You are Musa, whom Allah chose with His speech and wrote the Torah for you with His own hand. Do you blame me for something which Allah had decreed for me forty years before He created me?" Adam won the argument, the Prophet said (Sahih al-Bukhari 6614; Sahih Muslim 2652).

The point is not that Adam's mistake was excused — he repented, and Allah accepted his repentance. The point is the time-scale: forty years before Adam was even created, the decree was written. And yet Adam still repented, still strove, still carried the weight of his choice. The decree and the deed live side by side in the same man, without cancelling each other out.

The same balance runs through the hadith of the womb. Ibn Mas'ud reported that the Prophet said: while a child is in its mother's womb, Allah sends an angel who writes four things — its provision, its lifespan, its deeds, and whether it will be fortunate or unfortunate (Sahih al-Bukhari 3208; Sahih Muslim 2643). Your rizq was written before you took your first breath — and the Prophet still told you to go out and seek it. As Abu Huraira reported the Prophet saying: "The pen has dried with what you are destined to receive" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6596). What is written for you will reach you; what is not will never reach you — so chase it the right way, and rest your heart.

5.Then Why Should I Act?

This is the question every honest reader asks, and the Companions asked it first. Sitting with the Prophet at the graveyard of al-Baqi', they heard that Allah has written the place of every person — in Paradise or in the Fire — and asked: "Shall we then rely on what is written and give up doing deeds?" The Prophet answered: "Act, for everyone will find it easy to do what he was created for" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4949; Sahih Muslim 2647). One word — act — and the whole question is answered. You do not know what is written for you, so you act as if the best is written for you, and Allah makes the path easy for the one who walks it.

Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) showed the same balance in the most dramatic way. Marching toward Syria, he learned a plague had broken out there and turned the army back. Abu Ubaydah asked: "Are you fleeing from the decree of Allah?" Umar answered: "Yes, we are fleeing from the decree of Allah to the decree of Allah" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5729; Sahih Muslim 2219). Taking precautions is not a lack of faith in qadr — it is qadr.

That is why the Quran pairs qadr with effort: "But perhaps you hate a thing and it is good for you; and perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you. And Allah Knows, while you know not" (Quran 2:216). The door that closed may be the decree protecting you from something you cannot see; the delay that frustrated you may be the decree keeping you for something better. You cannot see the whole writing — only your line of it. So act, plan, strive, and leave the outcome to the One who wrote it. To go deeper, read our article on tawakkul — trusting Allah after you have done your part; qadr is the belief, and tawakkul is the way you live it.

6.Living With Qadr This Week

Here are five small ways to live with qadr this week.

First, change one sentence. When something goes wrong, do not say "if only I had done such-and-such" — the Prophet warned that "if only" opens the working of Shaytan, and taught us to say instead: "Allah decreed, and what He willed, He did" (Sahih Muslim 2664). Second, make dua — boldly. "Nothing repels the divine decree except supplication," the Prophet said (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2139, graded hasan). Third, stop negotiating with the past. The pens have dried. Regret has its place — it teaches — but replaying a decision a hundred times is not repentance; it is arguing with a decree already written. Learn the lesson, make tawbah if you need to, and walk on.

Fourth, act as if the best is written for you — the Prophet told you to act, and the one who acts with hope finds the path made easy. Fifth, when a blessing arrives, remember the second half of Quran 57:23: do not rejoice as if you earned it alone. Say alhamdulillah and share it — gratitude is the proof that you know the gift came from the Writer, not from your own cleverness. Our article on the virtue of patience pairs well with this one for hard days, and contentment (qana'ah) for days when what you have feels too small.

One honest note before we close: you will meet people who argue about qadr as if it were a puzzle to be solved. The Prophet himself disliked that argument among his Companions. What is certain is what the Quran and the authentic hadith state plainly: everything is written, the pens have dried — and you must still act, still pray, still choose. Hold both truths in the same heart, and the decree stops being a fear and becomes what it was always meant to be: the knowledge that nothing in your life is an accident, nothing is wasted, and the One who wrote it is your Protector.
