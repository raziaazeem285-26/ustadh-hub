Noorani Qaida Lesson 9: Huroof-e-Muqatta'at — Alif Laam Meem

R Razia Azeem · 20 Nov, 2026

Noorani Qaida Lesson 9: Alif Laam Meem — the disjointed letters that open Surah Al-Baqarah, and how to read them letter by letter.

In this article
  1. About this video
  2. Key reflections
  3. Watch & share

1.About this video

This is Lesson 9 of the Noorani Qaida series, and it teaches something every Quran reader meets on the very first page after Surah Al-Fatiha: the Huroof-e-Muqatta'at, the disjointed letters. Alif Laam Meem — three letters, standing alone at the start of Surah Al-Baqarah. The same three letters open Surah Aal-e-Imran, Surah Al-Ankabut, Surah Ar-Rum, Surah Luqman and Surah As-Sajdah. In total, twenty-nine surahs of the Quran begin with letters like these — Alif Laam Meem, Alif Laam Ra, Ha Meem, Ta Ha, Ya Seen, Qaf, Noon — and every one of them is read the same way: each letter separately, by its own name.

The lesson explains what these letters are and how to read them. They are not words, so you do not join them the way you join the letters of a word. You say each letter by its name: Alif, then Laam, then Meem. And the stretch matters: Alif is read short, with no stretch, while Laam and Meem are each stretched for six counts — the long madd that the Qaida teaches at this stage. That is the whole lesson in one line: say the letters by name, stretch the ones that carry a madd, and do not rush them into a word. For the previous lesson in this series, watch Noorani Qaida Lesson 8: The Non-joiners.

2.Key reflections

Think about why Allah opened so many surahs with single letters. The scholars have said, in the position held by the majority, that the exact meaning of these letters is known only to Allah — and that is itself the lesson. The Quran begins its longest surah with three letters whose full meaning Allah kept to Himself, and then immediately says: "This is the Book about which there is no doubt" (Quran 2:2, in meaning). The letters are a reminder that this Book is from Allah, and that its miracle is in its very letters — the same twenty-eight letters every Arab child knows, arranged in a way no human being has ever matched.

Allah challenged the doubters with those same letters: "And if you are in doubt about what We have sent down upon Our servant, then produce a surah the like thereof and call upon your witnesses other than Allah, if you should be truthful" (Quran 2:23, in meaning). No one could — and no one ever has. So when you read Alif Laam Meem, read it slowly, with the six-count stretch on Laam and Meem, and remember what it means: the Book you are holding begins with letters only Allah fully understands, and continues with guidance no one can doubt. For the surah that opens with these very letters, read Surah Al-Baqarah: The Surah That Shields Your Home.

3.Watch & share

Watch the full lesson above and practise with it: read Alif Laam Meem out loud, slowly — Alif short, Laam stretched six counts, Meem stretched six counts — until it feels natural. Then open Surah Al-Baqarah and read its first verses the same way, and notice how the letters you have just learned are the first thing Allah says in the longest surah of the Quran. Share this lesson with someone who is learning to read the Quran — a child, a new Muslim, or anyone starting from the Qaida — because the disjointed letters are the first thing that confuses a beginner, and this lesson makes them simple.
