How to Choose an Online Tutor: 7 Tips That Actually Work

Degrees and glowing profiles don't guarantee results. Use these 7 practical checks — from demo lessons to fee clarity — before you commit to any online tutor.

Choosing an online tutor feels risky because you can't see them teach before you pay. But parents who follow a simple checklist almost always land on a good teacher. Here are the 7 tips that actually separate great tutors from average ones.

1.Book a demo lesson first — never skip it

A serious tutor offers a demo or trial class, usually free or heavily discounted. Treat it like an audition: watch how the tutor explains one concept your child struggles with, and watch your child's face. Do they ask questions? Do they look less confused afterward? That 45-minute session tells you more than any profile. On UstadhHub, verified tutors clearly mark whether they offer a demo — shortlist only those who do.

2.Ask the right trial questions

During or after the demo, ask three specific questions: "What is my child's weakest topic, and what is your plan to fix it?", "How will you prepare them for the board exam pattern specifically?", and "How will I as a parent track progress?" Vague answers ("don't worry, we will cover everything") are a warning sign. Good tutors give concrete answers: weekly tests, a topic-wise plan, and monthly parent updates.

3.Check reviews — carefully

Reviews matter, but read them like a detective. Look for specific details ("my daughter's Physics went from 68 to 89 in three months") rather than generic praise ("best teacher"). Check that reviews span several months, not a single week — a burst of five-star reviews on the same day is often fake. Also look for how the tutor responds to critical reviews; professionalism in reply tells you a lot.

4.Get fee clarity in writing

Before the first paid class, confirm in writing: the monthly fee, how many classes per week, class duration, and what happens if a class is missed (whose side?). Typical online fees in Pakistan range from Rs 3,000–12,000 per subject per month. Beware of tutors who quote one fee and later add "extra test session" or "notes charges." Clear terms protect both sides.

A great tutor is not the one with the most degrees — it is the one who can explain a concept three different ways until your child gets it.

5.Do a tech check

Great teaching fails on a bad setup. Before paying, confirm the tutor uses a stable platform (Zoom, Google Meet), a digital whiteboard or screen-sharing, and provides lesson recordings. Ask whether they share notes as PDFs and whether quizzes are given after class. A tutor teaching from a shaky phone camera with no board is a gamble — good tools are part of good teaching.

6.Match teaching style to your child

Some students need strict discipline and weekly tests; others shut down under pressure and need encouragement first. Ask the tutor how they handle a student who doesn't do homework, or one who is too shy to ask questions. Then ask your child after the demo: "Did you feel comfortable asking questions?" The best tutor on paper is useless if your child dreads the class.

7.Watch for red flags

Walk away if you see any of these: guaranteed marks or "100% result" promises, pressure to pay 3–6 months in advance, refusal to give a demo, no reviews anywhere, or lessons that are just the tutor reading slides aloud. Another subtle one: tutors who are always available at any hour — the best teachers have full schedules, and that is actually a good sign.

Final thoughts

Choosing an online tutor is a decision you can get right in one week if you follow this checklist: demo lesson, specific questions, real reviews, written fees, tech check, style fit, and zero red flags. Start with a shortlist of verified tutors on UstadhHub, take two or three demos, and let your child's reaction be the final vote.
