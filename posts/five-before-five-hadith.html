Five Before Five: A Timeless Hadith on Time and Blessings

R Razia Azeem · 19 Oct, 2026

One hadith captures Islam's entire philosophy of time: use your youth, health, wealth, free time, and life before old age, sickness, poverty, busyness, and death take them away. Here is the hadith with its verified source — and what each of the five means for your daily life.

In this article
  1. The Hadith and Its Verified Source
  2. 1. Your Youth Before Your Old Age
  3. 2. Your Health Before Your Sickness
  4. 3. Your Wealth Before Your Poverty
  5. 4. Your Free Time Before Your Busyness
  6. 5. Your Life Before Your Death — A Practical Plan

Hadith

Time is the one blessing no one can store, borrow, or buy back. The Quran opens one of its shortest chapters with an oath by time itself: "By time, indeed, mankind is in loss, except those who believe and do righteous deeds and advise each other to truth and advise each other to patience" (Quran 103:1-3). And the Prophet ﷺ compressed the whole philosophy of time into a single piece of advice — given, beautifully, as personal counsel to one man.

1.The Hadith and Its Verified Source

Abdullah ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to a man while advising him:

اغْتَنِمْ خَمْسًا قَبْلَ خَمْسٍ: شَبَابَكَ قَبْلَ هَرَمِكَ، وَصِحَّتَكَ قَبْلَ سَقَمِكَ، وَغِنَاكَ قَبْلَ فَقْرِكَ، وَفَرَاغَكَ قَبْلَ شُغْلِكَ، وَحَيَاتَكَ قَبْلَ مَوْتِكَ

"Take advantage of five before five: your youth before your old age, your health before your sickness, your wealth before your poverty, your free time before your busyness, and your life before your death."

Source: Reported by al-Hakim in al-Mustadrak, hadith no. 7846 — graded Sahih (authentic); also reported by al-Bayhaqi in Shu'ab al-Iman with a sound chain. Narrator: Abdullah ibn Abbas (RA).

Notice the setting: the Prophet ﷺ was advising one man, face to face. This is not a public sermon — it is intimate counsel, which makes it feel as though it is addressed to each of us personally. And the word he chose, ightanim ("take advantage"), means to seize a gain eagerly — these five are treasures, and they are all temporary.

2.Your Youth Before Your Old Age

Youth is the season of energy, memory, and courage — the time when worship, learning, and good habits take root most deeply. The Quran describes the arc of life honestly: "Allah is the One who created you from weakness, then made after weakness strength, then made after strength weakness and white hair" (Quran 30:54). Strength is a loan, not a possession.

And youth will be questioned: the Prophet ﷺ said that on the Day of Resurrection, a person's feet will not move until he is asked about his life and how he spent it, and about his youth and how he used it (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2416). The practical lesson is simple — do not postpone deen for "later." Learn the Quran now, build the prayer habit now, serve your parents now. The worship you plant in youth shades you in old age.

3.Your Health Before Your Sickness

Health is the blessing we notice only when it leaves. The Prophet ﷺ named it plainly: "There are two blessings which many people lose: health and free time" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6412). A healthy body makes every worship easier — standing in prayer, fasting, caring for family, earning halal provision.

Using health "before sickness" means two things: thank Allah for it daily (see The Virtue of Gratitude), and spend it in obedience while you can — because the reward of good deeds done in health continues even when illness later prevents them. And when sickness does come, meet it with sabr: the believer's health and his illness are both, by Allah's mercy, a means of reward.

4.Your Wealth Before Your Poverty

Wealth is a trust, and its test is giving. The Quran urges: "And spend [in the way of Allah] from what We have provided you before death approaches one of you" (Quran 63:10). The hadith pairs wealth with poverty to remind us that provision fluctuates — the hand that gives in plenty is the hand that is rewarded when it has little.

The most beautiful part: charity outlives the giver. The Prophet ﷺ said: "When a man dies, his deeds come to an end except for three: ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, or a righteous child who prays for him" (Sahih Muslim 1631). Every rupee given sincerely today can keep earning reward after the giver is gone — wealth spent for Allah is the only wealth that never runs out.

5.Your Free Time Before Your Busyness

Free time is the quietest blessing — and the easiest to waste without noticing. The Prophet ﷺ paired it with health as the two most squandered gifts (Sahih al-Bukhari 6412). Busyness will come: work, family, responsibilities. The hours you have today, with nothing urgent pressing, are a gift that will not return in the same form.

The Quran gives the formula for free time: "So when you have finished your duties, then stand up for worship, and to your Lord direct your longing" (Quran 94:7-8). Guard at least one portion of your free hours for what lasts — Quran recitation, learning, dhikr, family, and rest taken with intention. An hour spent scrolling leaves nothing; an hour spent with the Quran leaves light.

6.Your Life Before Your Death — A Practical Plan

The fifth gathers the other four: your life before your death. Every one of the five is a countdown, and the only sane response is a plan. Five small daily habits that put this hadith into practice: 1. Begin the day with intention — ask Allah to bless your youth, health, wealth, time, and life today. 2. Spend your peak energy on worship — pray on time, read even a page of Quran. 3. Give something daily, however small — charity in ease is training for generosity always. 4. Protect one free hour from waste — learning, dhikr, or family, with your phone away. 5. End the day with accounting (muhasaba): did I use today's five, or lose them? When a difficult season comes, see also When Your Dua Feels Unanswered.

Start today: pick one of the five you have been neglecting and give it to Allah this week — an hour of your free time, a portion of your wealth, your morning energy for Fajr. To keep walking this path, keep reading on the blog and reach out anytime via Connect — may Allah let us use our five before our five run out. Ameen.
