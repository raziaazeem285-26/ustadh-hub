Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA): The Fourth Rightly Guided Caliph

When the Prophet ﷺ left Makkah in the dark of night, one young man slept in his bed so the assassins waiting outside would not know he was gone. Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA): raised in the Prophet's own house, the first child to believe, the hero of Khaybar, the husband of Fatimah, the father of Hasan and Husayn — and the fourth Rightly Guided Caliph. His life runs through the whole story of Islam, from the first revelation to the caliphate itself.

1.The Boy Raised in the Prophet's House

Ali was the son of Abu Talib — the Prophet's uncle and protector — and of Fatimah bint Asad, a woman the Prophet ﷺ loved like a mother. He was born in Makkah around 600 CE, about ten years before the first revelation, according to the historians' estimate.

In a year of hardship, when Abu Talib's large family strained his means, the Prophet ﷺ and his uncle al-Abbas took the children into their own homes to ease the burden. Ali came to the Prophet's house — and he never really left it. He grew up at the Prophet's table, in the Prophet's care, learning at the Prophet's side. When the revelation began, the boy who had been raised in that house was ready: the historians record that Ali was the first child to accept Islam, about ten years old, believing at once, without hesitation.

2.The Night in the Prophet's Bed

Thirteen years later, on the night of the Hijrah, the Quraysh had agreed to kill the Prophet ﷺ in his sleep. The Prophet ﷺ left Ali sleeping in his bed, wrapped in his green cloak, so that the men watching the house would think he was still inside. Ali knew exactly what he was doing — he was the decoy, and the assassins were outside. He slept anyway.

Then Ali stayed behind in Makkah after the Prophet ﷺ had gone, for a task only he could be trusted with: returning the deposits — the amanah — that the people of Makkah had entrusted to the Prophet ﷺ. The very men who wanted to kill the Prophet had left their wealth in his keeping — and Ali was the one trusted to hand it all back. Then he set out on foot, and caught up with the Prophet ﷺ at Quba. For the full story of that journey, read The Hijrah: A Journey That Changed History.

3.The Sword at Badr, Uhud, and the Trench

Ali fought in every great battle of the Prophet's life. At Badr, in 2 AH, he was one of the three champions in the opening duel — Hamza, Ali, and Ubaydah against Utbah, Shaybah, and Walid (Sahih al-Bukhari 3965) — and he killed Walid. For the full story of that day, read The Battle of Badr: When 313 Stood Against a Thousand.

At Uhud, when the archers left their post and the Muslim line broke, Ali was among the few who stood firm around the Prophet ﷺ as the battle turned. At the Trench, when Amr ibn Abd Wudd — the Quraysh champion, a giant of a man in armour — leapt the trench and challenged the Muslims to single combat, it was Ali who answered him, and defeated him. The seerah accounts agree on the pattern: whenever the moment was hardest, Ali was the one who stepped forward.

4.Khaybar: The Banner of the Beloved

The most famous day of Ali's life came at Khaybar, in 7 AH. The fortress of the Jews of Khaybar resisted every attack, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "Tomorrow I will give this flag to a man through whose hands Allah will give us victory. He loves Allah and His Apostle, and he is loved by Allah and His Apostle." That night, everyone wondered who it would be. In the morning, they all came hoping to receive it — and the Prophet ﷺ asked, "Where is Ali ibn Abi Talib?"

Ali was suffering from trouble in his eyes. They sent for him; the Prophet ﷺ spat in his eyes and prayed for him, and he was cured as if he had never had any trouble. Then the Prophet ﷺ gave him the flag. Ali said, "O Allah's Messenger! I will fight with them till they become like us." And the Prophet ﷺ answered with words that every Muslim who carries a message should memorise: "Proceed and do not hurry. When you enter their territory, call them to embrace Islam and inform them of Allah's rights which they should observe, for by Allah, even if a single man is led on the right path by Allah through you, then that will be better for you than the nice red camels" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4210).

"Even if a single man is led on the right path by Allah through you, then that will be better for you than the nice red camels." — Sahih al-Bukhari 4210

The banner went to the man who loved Allah and was loved by Allah — and the first order with it was not "fight", but "invite".

5."Like Aaron to Moses"

At Tabuk, in 9 AH, the Prophet ﷺ set out on the hardest expedition of his life and left Ali as his deputy in Madinah. Ali said, "Do you want to leave me with the children and women?" The Prophet ﷺ replied, "Will you not be pleased that you will be to me like Aaron to Moses? But there will be no prophet after me" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4416). Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, who heard it himself, repeated it so firmly that he put his fingers on his ears and said: "Yes, and if not, let both my ears become deaf" (Sahih Muslim 2404a).

At Ghadir Khumm, on the way back from the Farewell Pilgrimage, the Prophet ﷺ said: "For whomever I am his Mawla then Ali is his Mawla" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3713, graded Sahih by Darussalam). Mawla here means friend, ally, helper — the mainstream understanding is closeness and love, and the Prophet ﷺ himself added: "there will be no prophet after me."

The Quran describes the same closeness in general terms:

إِنَّمَا وَلِيُّكُمُ ٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱلَّذِينَ يُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَيُؤْتُونَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَهُمْ رَٰكِعُونَ

Quran 5:55 — "Your ally is only Allah, His Messenger, and those who believe — those who establish prayer and give zakah while bowing."

The classical commentators report that Ali once gave his ring in charity while bowing in prayer — that is their attribution, not a sahih chain, and the verse itself is general: it describes every believer who prays and gives. Ali was, in every sense, among the foremost of them.

6.A Home of Light: Fatimah, Hasan and Husayn

Ali married Fatimah (RA), the Prophet's beloved daughter. Their home was poor in the things of this world — a hand-mill, a leather water-skin, a bed of palm fibre — and rich in the things that matter. When Fatimah complained of the suffering the hand-mill caused her, and captives were brought to the Prophet ﷺ, she went to ask for a servant. The Prophet ﷺ came to them that night, while they had gone to bed, and sat between them until Ali felt the coolness of his feet on his chest. "Shall I teach you a thing which is better than what you have asked me?" he said. "When you go to bed, say Allahu Akbar thirty-four times, and SubhanAllah thirty-three times, and Alhamdulillah thirty-three times, for that is better for you both than a servant" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3706). That Tasbih of Fatimah is still recited by millions of Muslims every night.

The commentators report that this verse was revealed about Ali and Fatimah's household, who gave away their food for three nights running:

وَيُطْعِمُونَ ٱلطَّعَامَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِۦ مِسْكِينًۭا وَيَتِيمًۭا وَأَسِيرًا

Quran 76:8 — "And they give food, in spite of love for it, to the needy, the orphan, and the captive."

And at the Mubahalah — the mutual imprecation with the Christians of Najran — the Quran says: "let us call our sons and your sons, our women and your women, ourselves and yourselves" (Quran 3:61). The commentators say "ourselves" was Ali, "our sons" were Hasan and Husayn, and "our women" was Fatimah: the whole household of the Prophet ﷺ, standing as one. Of the two boys the Prophet ﷺ said: "Al-Hasan and Al-Husain are the chiefs of the youths of Paradise" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3768, graded Sahih by Darussalam).

7.The Fourth Caliph — and Five Lessons for Us Today

After Uthman's martyrdom in 35 AH, the community turned to Ali, and he became the fourth caliph. He moved the capital to Kufa, in Iraq — and his caliphate, from 35 to 40 AH, was torn by civil war: the Battle of the Camel, then Siffin, then the Khawarij. This post will not re-litigate the disputes between the Companions. The position of the scholars of Ahl al-Sunnah is to withhold judgement, to honour them all, and to remember what Allah said of the foremost of them: "Allah is pleased with them and they with Him" (Quran 9:100) — and Ali was among the foremost of the emigrants and the helpers. For Uthman's life and caliphate, read Uthman ibn Affan (RA): The Generous Caliph Who Gave Us One Quran.

On 19 Ramadan 40 AH, as Ali went out for the Fajr prayer in Kufa, he was struck by the Kharijite Ibn Muljam with a poisoned sword. He died two days later, on 21 Ramadan 40 AH (661 CE). The histories record his last words: "By the Lord of the Kaaba, I have succeeded." He was buried in Najaf, near Kufa, according to the histories — a city that still bears his name to this day.

Five lessons for us today:

1. Grow up close to the truth. Ali was raised in the Prophet's house, and the company he kept made him who he was. Choose your company the way he chose his — or the way it was chosen for him: close to the Quran, close to prayer, close to good people.

2. Courage is for the right moment. The bed on the night of the Hijrah, the duel at Badr, the trench, the banner at Khaybar — Ali never sought danger, but he never ran from it either. Bravery is not the absence of fear; it is standing firm when standing firm is what Allah asks of you.

3. Invite before you fight. The banner at Khaybar came with an order: call them to Islam first. One person guided is better than the red camels — better than any worldly gain. Whatever your cause, start with the invitation, not the argument.

4. Love the Prophet's family as he taught. Ali's closeness to the Prophet ﷺ was real — "like Aaron to Moses", "for whomever I am his Mawla" — and it was closeness, not prophethood. Love the family of the Prophet ﷺ, honour them, follow their example — and never add to what the Prophet ﷺ himself said.

5. Honour all the Companions, and leave their disputes to Allah. The caliphate of Ali was a time of division, and the scholars' guidance is the safest one: withhold judgement, honour them all, and pray for them. The Ummah's unity today is built on that restraint.

The boy who slept in the Prophet's bed became the man who carried his banner, married his daughter, and led his Ummah. Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA) — the first child to believe, the hero of Khaybar, the fourth Rightly Guided Caliph — and a life that still teaches us, fourteen centuries later, what it means to stand with the truth from the first day to the last. If this story moved you, share it — and say a prayer for the man who never left the Prophet's side.
