Marriage in Islam: Half of Faith, Built on Love and Mercy

R Razia Azeem · 26 Nov, 2026

When a man and a woman stand before witnesses and say “I accept”, two strangers become family. Marriage in Islam is not a ceremony or a party — it is half of your religion, a garment for your soul, and the place where Allah puts love and mercy.

In this article

  2. Marriage Is Half of the Religion
  3. “Marry, for It Lowers the Gaze”
  4. Choose Religion First
  5. A Garment for One Another

Daily Life

When a man and a woman stand before witnesses and say "I accept", two strangers become family. Allah calls marriage one of His signs — right beside the creation of the heavens and the earth. It is not a ceremony, a party, or a social expectation. It is half of your religion, a garment for your soul, and the place where love and mercy are meant to live.

1.A Sign Among His Signs

The Quran opens the subject of marriage not with a rule but with a wonder:

لِّتَسۡكُنُوۤا۟ إِلَیۡهَا وَجَعَلَ بَیۡنَكُم مَّوَدَّةࣰ وَرَحۡمَةًۚ

Quran 30:21 — "that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you love and mercy."

"And among His signs is that He created for you spouses from among yourselves, that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you love and mercy. Indeed, in that are signs for a people who give thought" (Quran 30:21). Notice the order. First comes sakinah — tranquillity, rest, the feeling of being home. Then mawaddah — love. Then rahmah — mercy. A marriage is meant to be, first of all, a place of rest. After a long day, you come home to a person, not a performance. And notice who places the love and mercy there: "He placed between you." Love in a marriage is not something you manufacture by effort alone; it is something Allah puts between two hearts.

The same beginning is given in Surah An-Nisa:

خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفۡسࣲ وَ ٰ⁠حِدَةࣲ وَخَلَقَ مِنۡهَا زَوۡجَهَا

Quran 4:1 — "He created you from a single soul, and from it He created its mate."

"O mankind, fear your Lord, Who created you from a single soul and created from it its mate, and dispersed from both of them many men and women" (Quran 4:1). Husband and wife come from the same soul.

2.Marriage Is Half of the Religion

Anas ibn Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever Allah grants a righteous wife, He has helped him with half of his religion. So let him fear Allah regarding the other half" (al-Hakim, al-Mustadrak 2681). Al-Hakim graded its chain sound, and al-Albani graded it sound in Sahih at-Targhib (1916) — though some scholars, including Ibn Hajar and al-Iraqi, graded it weak, so the wording is quoted with that difference of opinion stated plainly.

Why half? Because marriage guards what destroys a person fastest: the gaze, the desire, the loneliness that pushes people toward what is forbidden. A married person has a lawful home for what an unmarried person must fight alone. And it is not only about guarding — it is about building. The married person learns patience, sacrifice, forgiveness and generosity every single day, in a classroom no book can replace. Half of the religion is protected by the marriage itself; the other half is protected by taqwa — fearing Allah in how you treat the person you married. Marriage does not finish your religion; it protects half of it, and the other half is still your job.

3."Marry, for It Lowers the Gaze"

The Prophet ﷺ urged marriage early and plainly. Abdullah ibn Mas'ud narrated: "We were with the Prophet ﷺ while we were young and had no wealth. So Allah's Messenger ﷺ said, 'O young people! Whoever among you can marry, should marry, because it helps him lower his gaze and guard his modesty, and whoever is not able to marry, should fast, as fasting diminishes his sexual power'" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5066). Read that again: the companions he spoke to were young and had no wealth. He did not tell them to wait until they were rich. He told them to marry if they could, and to fast if they could not.

And marriage is his own way. Three men came to the houses of the Prophet's ﷺ wives asking about his worship, and when they heard how he worshipped, they thought it too little — so one vowed to pray all night forever, one to fast all year, and one to stay away from women and never marry. The Prophet ﷺ came to them and said: "By Allah, I am more submissive to Allah and more afraid of Him than you; yet I fast and break my fast, I do sleep and I also marry women. So he who does not follow my tradition in religion, is not from me" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5063). The middle path — prayer and sleep, fasting and eating, worship and marriage — is the Sunnah.

For the gaze itself — why it matters and how it is guarded — read Lowering the Gaze: The Shield That Guards Your Heart.

4.Choose Religion First

So what should you look for in a spouse? Abu Hurairah narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said: "A woman may be married for four reasons: for her property, her status, her beauty and her religion, so try to get one who is religious, may your hand be besmeared with dust" (Sahih Muslim 1466). Wealth fades. Status changes. Beauty ages. Religion is the one quality that grows more valuable with time.

The same advice is given for choosing a husband. Abu Hurairah narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "When someone whose religion and character you are pleased with proposes to (someone under the care) of one of you, then marry to him. If you do not do so, then there will be turmoil in the land and abounding discord" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1084). Abu Isa himself recorded a dispute over this narration's chain — the version through al-Layth is mursal — so it is cited here with that caveat.

Character is the part people skip. A person can pray and still be cruel. A person can fast and still be dishonest. Look at how a potential spouse treats people who can do nothing for them — the waiter, the driver, their own parents. That is the character you will live with.

5.A Garment for One Another

The Quran gives marriage its most beautiful image in a single sentence:

هُنَّ لِبَاسࣱ لَّكُمۡ وَأَنتُمۡ لِبَاسࣱ لَّهُنَّۗ

Quran 2:187 — "They are a garment for you and you are a garment for them."

"They are a garment for you and you are a garment for them" (Quran 2:187). Think about what a garment does. It covers you — your faults, your weaknesses, the parts of you that you would not want exposed. It protects you. It beautifies you. And it is close to you — closer than anything else you own.

That is what a spouse is meant to be. You cover each other's faults; you do not advertise them to your family or your friends. You protect each other — her honour is your honour, his dignity is your dignity. And you make each other better: the right spouse makes you pray more, speak more gently, give more generously. If your marriage is doing the opposite — if it makes you smaller, harsher, further from Allah — something has gone wrong, and it is worth fixing before it breaks.

6.Live With Them in Kindness

Allah commands the husband directly:

وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِٱلۡمَعۡرُوفِۚ

Quran 4:19 — "And live with them in kindness."

"And live with them in kindness. For if you dislike them — perhaps you dislike a thing and Allah makes therein much good" (Quran 4:19). Kindness is not the decoration of a marriage; it is the command. And the verse is realistic: it admits you may dislike something about your spouse — and still tells you to live with kindness, because Allah may have placed much good in the very thing you dislike.

Aishah narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The best of you is the best to his wives, and I am the best of you to my wives" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3895, graded Hasan Gharib Sahih). Your character at home, where no one is watching, is your real character.

And he warned, gently and firmly, about how a wife is to be treated. Abu Hurairah narrated that Allah's Messenger ﷺ said: "Treat women nicely, for a woman is created from a rib, and the most curved portion of the rib is its upper portion, so, if you should try to straighten it, it will break, but if you leave it as it is, it will remain crooked. So treat women nicely" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3331). The point is the gentleness. She is not a problem to be fixed by force. She is a trust to be handled with care — and the same gentleness is owed by a wife to her husband, for the Quran gives both of them rights: "And women have rights similar to those against them, in a fair manner" (Quran 2:228).

Marriage does not cancel the rights of parents — it adds new rights beside them. For that balance, read The Rights of Parents in Islam.

7.A Simple Nikah, a Blessed Beginning

If marriage is half of the religion, then its beginning should be simple. The mahr — the gift the husband gives his wife — is her right, not a price and not a competition. "And give the women their dower as a gift" (Quran 4:4). It can be small. When Abdur-Rahman ibn Auf (may Allah be pleased with him) married, the Prophet ﷺ asked him how much mahr he had given. He said: gold equal to the weight of a date stone. And the Prophet ﷺ said to him: "Give a banquet, even if with one sheep" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167). A date-stone's weight of gold, and a feast of one sheep. That was enough.

Compare that with what weddings have become: halls booked a year ahead, clothes that cost a year's salary, debts that follow a couple into their first year of marriage. None of that is in the Sunnah. A wedding that begins in debt and showing off does not begin with barakah.

So keep it simple. A mahr she is happy with. A walimah, even a small one. Witnesses, an announcement, and a dua. The money you save is the first deposit in the home you are building together. And the simplicity itself is worship: it says that this marriage is about two people and their Lord, not about an audience.

1. If you are unmarried and able, do not delay without reason. The Prophet ﷺ urged marriage for the young (Sahih al-Bukhari 5066) — it guards the gaze and the heart. Talk to your parents, make a plan, and trust Allah with the provision.

2. If you are choosing, choose religion and character first. Wealth, status and beauty are real, but they fade; the religious spouse is the one who will still be kind when everything else is gone (Sahih Muslim 1466).

3. If you are married, be the garment. Cover your spouse's faults, protect their honour, and make them better. For the patience this takes when things are hard, read The Virtue of Patience (Sabr). Live with kindness — "and live with them in kindness" (Quran 4:19) — even on the days you dislike something, because Allah may have placed much good in it.

"The best of you is the best to his wives, and I am the best of you to my wives." — the Prophet ﷺ (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3895)

Marriage is half of your religion because it is where your religion becomes real: in the kitchen, in the argument, in the apology, in the quiet kindness no one sees. Allah placed love and mercy between two hearts — your job is to protect what He placed there. May Allah bless every marriage with sakinah, mawaddah and rahmah, and grant every unmarried believer a righteous spouse. Ameen.
