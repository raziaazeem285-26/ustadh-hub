Surah Ad-Duha: Your Lord Has Not Forsaken You

R Razia Azeem · 8 Nov, 2026

When the silence felt like abandonment, Allah answered with eleven verses — 'Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor has He detested you.'

In this article
  1. The Silence Before the Surah
  2. “By the Morning Brightness” — Two Oaths
  3. “Your Lord Has Not Forsaken You” — The Promise
  4. Three Blessings You Already Have
  5. Three Commands That Follow

Quran

There are seasons in a believer's life when the heavens feel silent. You pray, and no answer seems to come. You wait for a sign, a relief, a door to open — and nothing moves. In that silence, a terrible thought creeps in: perhaps Allah has turned away. Perhaps He has forgotten you. Fourteen centuries ago, the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) lived through exactly that silence — and Allah answered it with eleven short verses that have comforted believers ever since. This is Surah Ad-Duha, the surah of the morning brightness.

Surah Ad-Duha is the 93rd surah of the Quran, a short Makkan surah of eleven verses, revealed when revelation had paused and the Prophet's enemies were whispering that his Lord had abandoned him. Allah's reply was not a rebuke. It was reassurance — addressed, through the Prophet, to every believer who has ever felt forsaken.

1.The Silence Before the Surah

Jundub ibn Abdullah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Prophet (peace be upon him) once fell ill and did not stand for the night prayer for a night or two. A woman came to him — the commentators identify her as Umm Jamil, the wife of Abu Lahab — and said, "O Muhammad, I do not see but that your Satan has left you." Then Allah revealed the opening of Surah Ad-Duha: "By the morning brightness, and by the night when it covers with darkness. Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor has He detested you" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4983; a similar narration in Sahih Muslim 1797 says two or three nights).

Notice what the hadith actually says. The Prophet was ill. He missed Tahajjud for a night or two — the prayer he loved most — and in that small gap, the taunt arrived. And the pause was real: revelation had stopped coming for a time. The reports differ on exactly how long the pause lasted, and the Quran does not fix a number — so we will not invent one. What matters is what the pause felt like, and what Allah said about it.

Some commentaries record that Khadijah (may Allah be pleased with her) spoke similar words — but hers were the words of a loving wife, worried for her husband, wondering aloud whether Allah was displeased. The same sentence can be a taunt or a worry, depending on the heart that says it. Allah answered them both with the same three verses.

2.“By the Morning Brightness” — Two Oaths

Allah opens the surah with an oath: "By the morning brightness, and by the night when it covers with darkness" (Quran 93:1-2). Ad-Duha is the forenoon — the morning after the sun has risen high, when its light is full and warm. The night, by contrast, is still and covering. Allah swears by opposites: light and dark, morning and night.

Why swear by the morning? Because the morning is the surah's whole message in one image: every night ends, the sun rises, and the world is lit again. Just as the morning follows the night without fail, relief follows hardship without fail. The Quran says the same thing in the very next surah: "For indeed, with hardship [will be] ease. Indeed, with hardship [will be] ease" (Quran 94:5-6). Surah Ad-Duha and Surah Ash-Sharh are companions — one says "you have not been forsaken," the other says "ease is already on its way."

And notice: Allah swears by the morning — not by the night. The night is mentioned second, only as the thing that passes. The surah does not end in the dark. It begins with the light, and the light is what it is about.

3.“Your Lord Has Not Forsaken You” — The Promise

Then comes the verse that has carried millions of hearts: "Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor has He detested you" (Quran 93:3). Two denials, and both matter. "Forsaken" — Allah has not left you or abandoned the work He began with you. "Detested" — He is not angry with you, not punishing you by His silence. The pause was not rejection. It was a pause.

"Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor has He detested you. And the Hereafter is better for you than the first [life]. And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be satisfied." — Quran 93:3-5, in meaning

"And the Hereafter is better for you than the first [life]" (Quran 93:4). This is the long view: the present moment — the illness, the taunt, the silence — is not the measure of your life. What is coming is better than what has passed. Allah measures your life by its end, not by its hardest middle.

Then the promise: "And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be satisfied" (Quran 93:5). The classical commentators read this promise broadly. Among its meanings, they say, is the intercession Allah would grant the Prophet for his ummah on the Day of Judgement — Allah would keep giving until he was satisfied. The promise is not "you will get what you asked for" — it is bigger: you will be given until you are satisfied.

وَلَسَوْفَ يُعْطِيكَ رَبُّكَ فَتَرْضَىٰ

Quran 93:5 — "And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be satisfied."

4.Three Blessings You Already Have

Before Allah gives the commands of the surah, He reminds the Prophet of three blessings — and the reminder is a method. When you feel forsaken, do not start by counting what is missing. Start by counting what was already given.

"Did He not find you an orphan and give [you] refuge?" (Quran 93:6). The Prophet's father, Abdullah, died before he was born. His mother, Aminah, died when he was six. His grandfather, Abdul Muttalib, died when he was eight. By every human measure, he was the most alone child in Makkah — and Allah gave him refuge, first with his grandfather, then with his uncle Abu Talib, who protected him for the rest of his life.

"And He found you lost and guided [you]" (Quran 93:7). "Lost" here does not mean misguided in sin — the Prophet was protected from that all his life. It means he did not yet know the Book or the details of faith before revelation came to him. Allah says the same thing elsewhere: "You did not know what is the Book or [what is] faith, but We have made it a light by which We guide whom We will of Our servants" (Quran 42:52, in meaning). Guidance is a gift, not an achievement.

"And He found you poor and made [you] self-sufficient" (Quran 93:8). The orphan who owned nothing was enriched — through his work, through the trade he managed, and through Khadijah (may Allah be pleased with her), whose wealth and whose support carried the early years of his mission. Self-sufficiency here is not only money. It is the contentment that stops a person from begging and complaining — the richness of the soul: true richness is richness of the soul (Sahih al-Bukhari 6446; Sahih Muslim 1051).

Three blessings, three sentences, no argument. The surah does not debate the taunt. It answers it with the Prophet's own life: look at what has already happened to you. If Allah carried you then, He has not dropped you now.

5.Three Commands That Follow

Comfort, in this surah, is not the end but the beginning. Allah follows the three blessings with three commands — each turns a blessing received into a duty owed.

فَأَمَّا الْيَتِيمَ فَلَا تَقْهَرْ وَأَمَّا السَّائِلَ فَلَا تَنْهَرْ وَأَمَّا بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثْ

Quran 93:9-11

"So as for the orphan, do not oppress [him]" (Quran 93:9). The one who was an orphan must now protect orphans. The blessing becomes a responsibility: the person Allah sheltered must shelter others. The Prophet lived this command to the letter — he said, in meaning, that he and the one who cares for an orphan will be as close in Paradise as two fingers held together (Sahih al-Bukhari 6005). Read more in our article on caring for the orphan in Islam.

"And as for the petitioner, do not repel [him]" (Quran 93:10). The one who asks — the beggar at the door, the person who needs help — must not be turned away harshly. The Prophet, who had once been poor himself, knew what a harsh refusal feels like. If you cannot give, give a kind word: the Quran says a kind word and forgiveness are better than charity followed by hurt (Quran 2:263, in meaning).

"But as for the favour of your Lord, report [it]" (Quran 93:11). And finally: speak about the blessing. Gratitude that stays silent is incomplete — the surah commands the believer to mention Allah's favour, to tell it, to let others hear it. Not to boast, but to bear witness. The person who counts his blessings aloud reminds himself — and everyone listening — that the Giver is still giving. For more on this, read our article on the virtue of gratitude.

6.How to Live Surah Ad-Duha When You Feel Forsaken

The surah was revealed for the Prophet, but the Quran is guidance for all of us — and its method works for any believer in a silent season. Here is how to carry it, starting today.

First, recite it — slowly, and often. Eleven verses take less than a minute. Read them in the morning, when the sun is up: the morning came again today, as it always does. That is the whole argument of the surah, and you can watch it happen every single day.

Second, count three blessings the way the surah counts three. Write them down: one shelter you were given, one guidance you received, one provision that arrived when you needed it. The surah's method is not positive thinking — it is evidence. Your own history is the proof that you have not been forsaken.

Third, pair it with its companion surah. Read Surah Ash-Sharh (94) right after it — "For indeed, with hardship [will be] ease" (Quran 94:5-6) — and let the two surahs answer each other: you have not been forsaken, and ease is already with the hardship.

Fourth, keep one small deed going. The most beloved deeds to Allah are the most consistent, even if they are small (Sahih al-Bukhari 6464). In a silent season, the temptation is to stop everything until the feeling returns. Do the opposite: hold one small habit — two rak'ahs of Duha prayer in the forenoon, the very time this surah is named after — and let the habit hold you. Our guide to the Duha prayer and its reward explains how to begin.

Fifth, turn the comfort outward. The surah's last three verses are commands, not suggestions: protect an orphan, answer a petitioner kindly, speak of Allah's favour. The fastest way out of feeling forgotten is to make someone else feel remembered.

One honest note: you will find claims online about fixed rewards for reciting this surah a set number of times. No authentic hadith fixes such a number — so take the surah for what it certainly is: Allah's own words of reassurance, revealed when His Prophet needed them most.

The night the Prophet missed Tahajjud, his enemies said his Lord had left him. Allah's answer still stands, and it is addressed to you as surely as it was addressed to him: the morning has come, the night has passed, and your Lord has not forsaken you. "Do not despair of the mercy of Allah" (Quran 39:53, in meaning). The morning always comes — and the One who brings it has not forgotten you.
