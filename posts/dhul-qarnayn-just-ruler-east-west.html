Dhul-Qarnayn: The Just Ruler of the East and the West

R Razia Azeem · 21 Nov, 2026

Three stories into Surah Al-Kahf — the Sleepers, the Two Gardens, Musa and Khidr — the surah tells a fourth. A king who ruled from the rising of the sun to its setting, and when he built the greatest barrier on earth, he said only: this is a mercy from my Lord.

Seerah & Stories

Three stories into Surah Al-Kahf — the Sleepers, the Two Gardens, Musa and Khidr — the surah tells a fourth. A king who ruled from the rising of the sun to its setting, given every means on earth, who used them all for justice. And a barrier of iron and copper that still stands in the Quran's telling, waiting for the Day Allah promised. This is the story of Dhul-Qarnayn — "the one of the two horns" — the story that completes Surah Al-Kahf. For the surah's virtue and its four stories in brief, read Surah Al-Kahf: A Light from One Friday to the Next.

1.The Question the Quraysh Asked

The classical commentators report how this story reached the Quran. The Jews of Madinah told the Quraysh to test the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ with three questions: the youths who disappeared in ancient times, the man who travelled to the east and the west, and the spirit. He said he would answer them — and the answer came down as Surah Al-Kahf itself. The youths were the Sleepers of the Cave; the traveller was Dhul-Qarnayn. And the surah answers with a story, not a biography: the Quran cares less for his name and dates than for what his power teaches.

وَیَسۡـَٔلُونَكَ عَن ذِی ٱلۡقَرۡنَیۡنِۖ قُلۡ سَأَتۡلُوا۟ عَلَیۡكُم مِّنۡهُ ذِكۡرًا

Quran 18:83 — "And they ask you about Dhul-Qarnayn. Say: I will recite to you about him a mention."

His name means "the possessor of the two horns". The scholars offer explanations: the two horns of the sun — the east and the west, both of which he reached; two braids of hair; or two horns of his crown. The Quran gives no explanation, so we do not settle it. What matters is what he did with the power Allah gave him.

2.A King Given Every Means

The story begins with a sentence that explains everything that follows: "Indeed, We established him upon the earth, and We gave him a means to everything" (Quran 18:84, in meaning).

Two words carry it. Tamkeen — We established him: his kingdom was not seized, it was given. And sabab — a means: roads, resources, knowledge, armies, the way to every place he needed. Every means came from Allah. Then comes the next verse, only three words long: "So he followed a way" (Quran 18:85, in meaning). The means were given — but the king still had to travel. The Quran repeats those same three words at the start of each journey (18:85, 18:89, 18:92): power is not a chair you sit on; it is a road you walk. For the same lesson in a different life, read Humility (Tawadu'): Strength in Lowering Yourself Before Allah.

3.The Journey West — the Setting Sun

His first journey took him to the far west, where the sun appeared to set: "Until, when he reached the setting of the sun, he found it setting in a spring of dark mud, and found near it a people. We said, 'O Dhul-Qarnayn, either you punish [them] or adopt among them [a way of] kindness'" (Quran 18:86, in meaning).

Allah ordered neither punishment nor kindness — He gave the king a choice, and watched what a just ruler would do with it. His answer is the charter of Islamic justice in one verse: "He said, 'As for one who wrongs, we will punish him. Then he will be returned to his Lord, and He will punish him with a terrible punishment. But as for one who believes and does righteousness, he will have the best reward, and we will speak to him from our command with ease'" (Quran 18:87-88, in meaning). The wrongdoer is punished — but worldly punishment is not the end, because he will be returned to his Lord for a greater account. The believer is rewarded, and the ruler's own words to him are easy — yusra — gentle, not harsh. Justice first, mercy in the method, and the final judgement left to Allah.

4.The Journey East — People With No Shelter

Then he followed a way again — the same three words — to the opposite end of the earth: "Until, when he came to the rising of the sun, he found it rising on a people for whom We had not made against it any shield" (Quran 18:90, in meaning). A people with nothing between them and the sun. And the Quran adds one line that frames the whole story: "Thus. And We had encompassed [all] that he had in knowledge" (Quran 18:91, in meaning). Allah knew everything about him — every journey, every decision, every use of the means He had given. The most powerful man on earth was still entirely inside Allah's knowledge.

5.The People Between Two Mountains

His third journey took him to a pass between two mountains, where he found a people who could hardly understand speech — and they had a desperate request: "They said, 'O Dhul-Qarnayn, indeed Gog and Magog are [great] corrupters in the land. So may we assign for you an expenditure that you might make between us and them a barrier?'" (Quran 18:94, in meaning). Gog and Magog — Ya'juj and Ma'juj — were corrupters who raided and ruined the land, and these mountain people lived in fear of them. The Quran returns to them near the end of time: "Until when [the dam of] Gog and Magog has been opened and they, from every elevation, descend" (Quran 21:96, in meaning). They offered to pay the king — weak, frightened people asking a strong ruler for help. What he did next is the heart of the story.

6.The Barrier of Iron and Copper

He refused their money. "He said, 'That in which my Lord has established me is better, but assist me with strength; I will make between you and them a dam'" (Quran 18:95, in meaning).

قَالَ مَا مَكَّنِّی فِیهِ رَبِّی خَیۡرࣱ فَأَعِینُونِی بِقُوَّةٍ أَجۡعَلۡ بَیۡنَكُمۡ وَبَیۡنَهُمۡ رَدۡمًا

Quran 18:95 — "That in which my Lord has established me is better, but assist me with strength; I will make between you and them a dam."

What Allah gave him was better than what they could pay — so he would not sell protection. But he did not do it alone. He asked for their hands, not their wealth: "Bring me sheets of iron" — until, when he had levelled them between the two mountain walls, he said, "Blow" — until, when he had made it [like] fire, he said, "Bring me, that I may pour over it molten copper" (Quran 18:96, in meaning). Iron, then fire, then molten copper — a wall sealed so hard that "they were unable to pass over it, and were unable to pierce it" (Quran 18:97, in meaning). The Quran describes the work step by step, because this was real work, not magic: iron, bellows, fire, copper — a whole community working beside its king. Power used for Allah, built by everyone's hands.

The Prophet ﷺ once came to his wife Zaynab bint Jahsh in a state of fear and said: "None has the right to be worshipped but Allah. Woe to the Arabs from an evil that has drawn near. Today an opening has been made in the barrier of Gog and Magog like this" — making a circle with his thumb and forefinger. She asked, "O Messenger of Allah, shall we be destroyed even though there are pious people among us?" He said: "Yes, when wickedness increases" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3346). Its opening is one of the signs of the Hour — a warning that no number of good people can hold back a society that lets wickedness spread.

7."This Is a Mercy from My Lord"

He had built the greatest barrier on earth, and he took no credit: "He said, 'This is a mercy from my Lord, but when the promise of my Lord comes, He will make it level, and ever is the promise of my Lord true'" (Quran 18:98, in meaning).

قَالَ هَـٰذَا رَحۡمَةࣱ مِّن رَّبِّیۖ فَإِذَا جَاۤءَ وَعۡدُ رَبِّی جَعَلَهُۥ دَكَّاۤءَۖ وَكَانَ وَعۡدُ رَبِّی حَقࣰّا

Quran 18:98 — "This is a mercy from my Lord, but when the promise of my Lord comes, He will make it level, and ever is the promise of my Lord true."

Compare him with Pharaoh, who said, "I am your lord, the most high" (Quran 79:24, in meaning). One king built a wall and said, "This is a mercy from my Lord." The other built an empire and said, "I am your lord" — the difference between the two kinds of power. Dhul-Qarnayn knew his barrier would not last forever: when Allah's promise comes, He will level it to the ground. "And We will leave them that day surging over each other, and [then] the Trumpet will be blown, and We will assemble them in [one] assembly. And We will present Hell that Day to the disbelievers, on display" (Quran 18:99-100, in meaning).

Was he a prophet? The scholars differ; the majority view is that he was a righteous, just king — not a prophet. A narration reported in al-Hakim's al-Mustadrak (3682), which al-Hakim graded sahih on the condition of the Two Shaykhs, records the Prophet ﷺ saying he did not know whether Dhul-Qarnayn was a prophet or not. And was he Alexander the Great? Some commentators said yes; others disagreed. The Quran gives no name, no dates, and no places — so this post does not settle it. What matters is what the Quran teaches:

1. Power is a trust, not a possession. Every means he had was given (18:84). Whatever you have — wealth, knowledge, authority — was given, and it will be asked about.

2. Use the means, then travel. Three times the Quran says "so he followed a way" — Allah gives the means; you must still walk the road.

3. Justice with mercy. Punish the wrongdoer, reward the believer, and speak to people with ease (18:88). Harshness is not strength; ease is.

4. Do not sell protection. "That in which my Lord has established me is better" (18:95) — help given for Allah's sake is never for sale.

5. Ask for hands, not wealth. He refused their money and asked for their strength — a community that builds together owns what it builds.

6. Take no credit. "This is a mercy from my Lord" (18:98) — the moment you say "I built this", you have become Pharaoh.

7. Remember the end. The barrier will fall when Allah's promise comes — every wall and empire in this world has a date on it; only Allah's promise is true.

"This is a mercy from my Lord, but when the promise of my Lord comes, He will make it level, and ever is the promise of my Lord true." — Quran 18:98

Dhul-Qarnayn is the last of the four stories of Surah Al-Kahf — the Sleepers, the Two Gardens, Musa and Khidr, and now the just king. Read the surah this Friday and find all four: faith, wealth, knowledge, power — the four trials of this world, and the Quran's four answers. If this story moved you, share it with someone who holds any power — at home, at work, in the community — and remind them of the king who reached the ends of the earth and still said, "This is a mercy from my Lord."
