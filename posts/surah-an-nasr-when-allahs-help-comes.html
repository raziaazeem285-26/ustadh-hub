Surah An-Nasr: When Allah's Help and Victory Come

R Razia Azeem · 25 Nov, 2026

Three verses. That is all it takes for the Quran to close the greatest mission in human history. Surah An-Nasr — the Help, the Victory — is the last complete surah ever revealed, sent down when the work was done: the city conquered, the people entering Islam in crowds, the religion perfected.

Quran

Three verses. That is all it takes for the Quran to close the greatest mission in human history. Surah An-Nasr — the Help, the Victory — is the last complete surah ever revealed. It was sent down when the work was done: the city conquered, the people entering Islam in crowds, the religion perfected. And its message is not what you would expect at the moment of victory. It does not say "celebrate". It says: glorify your Lord — and seek His forgiveness. For the surah's companion on that Farewell Pilgrimage, read Farewell Sermon: The Prophet's ﷺ Last Message.

1.The Last Complete Surah

Surah An-Nasr is the 110th surah of the Quran: three verses, revealed in Madinah, in the thirtieth juz. Ibn Abbas (RA) reports that it was the last surah to be revealed in full — "no complete surah was sent down after it" (Sahih Muslim 3024). One careful note: individual verses were still revealed after it, such as "Today I have perfected for you your religion" (Quran 5:3). The reports that Surah At-Tawbah was the last surah revealed (Sahih al-Bukhari 4364, 4605, 4654) are understood by the scholars to mean parts of it, not the whole surah. An-Nasr stands alone as the last complete surah.

It is also called Surah At-Tawdi' — the Farewell — because it bids farewell to the Prophet ﷺ. Abdullah ibn Umar (RA) reports that it was sent down during the Farewell Pilgrimage, in the middle of the days of Tashriq at Mina, and that after it the Prophet rode his camel and gave his well-known sermon (reported in Jami' at-Tirmidhi and others). The classical commentator al-Qurtubi records that only about eighty days passed between these revelations and the Prophet's death. Three verses, eighty days, and then the mission was complete.

2."When Allah's Help and the Victory Come"

The surah opens with a promise stated as a certainty:

إِذَا جَاۤءَ نَصۡرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡفَتۡحُ

Quran 110:1 — "When the help of Allah and victory come."

Al-fath — the victory, the opening — is understood by the commentators, with complete agreement, to be the conquest of Makkah. Allah had already promised it: "Indeed, We have given you a clear victory" (Quran 48:1, in meaning). The scholars differ only on the timing of this surah's revelation. The words "when there comes" suggest it was revealed before the conquest, as a promise of what was coming; the report of Ibn Umar places its proclamation at the Farewell Pilgrimage, two years after the conquest. The two can stand together: the Prophet ﷺ may have recited and proclaimed the surah on that occasion, so the Companions associated it with the Farewell. What is not in doubt is the event itself. Thirteen years of persecution in Makkah, eight years of exile and war from Madinah — and then, in Ramadan of the 8th year after the Hijrah, the city opened its gates, and the Prophet entered it with his head bowed low in humility, reciting the verses of victory.

Notice the order of the verse: the help comes first, and the victory second. The victory is not the Prophet's achievement. It is Allah's help arriving — and the victory follows. When help comes, the believer's first job is to name its source correctly.

3."And You See the People Entering Allah's Religion in Multitudes"

The second verse describes a sight the Prophet ﷺ had waited twenty-three years to see:

وَرَأَیۡتَ ٱلنَّاسَ یَدۡخُلُونَ فِی دِینِ ٱللَّهِ أَفۡوَاجࣰا

Quran 110:2 — "And you see the people entering into the religion of Allah in multitudes."

Afwaja — in troops, in crowds, group after group. The year after the conquest, the 9th year after the Hijrah, became known in the seerah as the Year of Delegations: tribe after tribe came to Madinah, whole tribes at once, to enter Islam — not one by one, not in secret, but in crowds, openly, without a battle fought. Compare that with the beginning. In the first years, the believers could be counted on two hands, meeting in secret in the house of al-Arqam. Now the verse says: you will see it — not hear about it, not hope for it — see it with your own eyes. And he did. Before he died, he saw the crowds. The promise was kept in his lifetime, in front of him.

The early years looked like failure: a handful of believers, mockery, boycott, exile. The crowds came at the end — and they came because the door had stayed open through all of it. The city he had entered as a migrant was the city the tribes now came to, seeking the message he had carried there.

4."So Glorify Your Lord... and Seek His Forgiveness"

Then comes the command — the only command in the surah, and the whole point of it:

فَسَبِّحۡ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡهُۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابَۢا

Quran 110:3 — "So exalt [Him] with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him. Indeed, He is ever Accepting of repentance."

At the moment of victory, the command is not to celebrate, not to boast, not to rest. It is tasbih and istighfar — glorify your Lord, and seek His forgiveness. Aisha (RA) reports what the Prophet ﷺ did with this verse: "When Surat An-Nasr was revealed, the Prophet did not offer any prayer except that he said in it: 'Subhanaka Allahumma Rabbana wa bihamdika, Allahummaghfir li' — Glory be to You, O Allah, our Lord, and praise be to You; O Allah, forgive me" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4967). In every prayer after this surah came down, the same words. Victory did not make him proud. It made him pray more.

Why istighfar at the moment of success? Because success is the moment when the heart is most likely to forget. In hardship, you remember Allah because you need Him. In victory, you are tempted to remember yourself. The surah closes the gap before it opens: the moment the help arrives, turn to the One who sent it, and ask forgiveness — for the shortcomings in the work, for the pride that success breeds, for every moment the victory made you feel it was yours. And the verse ends with the reason it is safe to do so: "Indeed, He is ever Accepting of repentance" — Tawwab, the One who turns back to His servant again and again, however often the servant turns to Him.

5.The Sign of the Farewell

Umar (RA) understood what the surah really meant — and he tested the Companions to see if they did. Ibn Abbas (RA) narrates: Umar asked the people about Allah's words, "When comes the help of Allah and the victory." They replied, "It indicates the future conquest of towns and palaces." Umar said, "What do you say about it, O Ibn Abbas?" He replied, "This surah indicates the termination of the life of Muhammad. Through it he was informed of the nearness of his death" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4969; the same account is repeated in Sahih al-Bukhari 4970). The victory was the sign. The mission was complete: the help had come, the people had entered in multitudes, the religion had been perfected on the plain of Arafah — "Today I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favour upon you" (Quran 5:3, revealed at Arafah, as reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 45). There was nothing left for the Messenger to do. So Allah told him, gently, that his time had come.

Ibn Abbas reports that after this surah the Prophet began to labour for the Hereafter with an intensity he had never shown before (reported in Musnad Ahmad and the books of tafsir). The last months of his life were the most worshipful of all — more prayer, more tasbih, more istighfar. He knew what the surah meant, and he answered it the way the surah told him to: with praise and with seeking forgiveness, right to the end.

6.What Victory Teaches Us

The surah is three verses about one victory — but its teaching is for every victory, including yours. The first lesson is the hardest for a successful person to hear: "And victory is not but from Allah" (Quran 8:10, in meaning). The help came first; the victory followed. Your degree, your business, your child's hifz — the help arrived before the result did. Name it correctly, or the victory will start to feel like your own achievement.

The second lesson is that the test of success is harder than the test of hardship. Hardship keeps you close to Allah because you have nowhere else to go. Success gives you somewhere else to go — your own ego. That is why the Prophet's response to the greatest victory of his life was not a parade but a prayer, and why the conquest of Makkah itself was an act of mercy, not revenge: the general amnesty — "Go, for you are free" — is reported in the seerah (Ibn Hisham), and the gentleness behind it is the same gentleness Allah described in him: "So by mercy from Allah, you were lenient with them" (Quran 3:159, in meaning). The crowds came in multitudes because the door was open and the hand was gentle — not because it was forced. Patience carried him through the hardship, as we explored in The Virtue of Patience: How Sabr Turns Hardship into Reward; humility carried him through the victory. Most people only train for one of them.

7.Five Lessons for Your Own Victories

1. Attribute every success to Allah first. Before you say "I did it", say "this is from Allah". The help came first; the victory followed. The habit of naming the source keeps the blessing from turning into pride.

2. Answer success with tasbih and istighfar. The Prophet's practice after An-Nasr was to say in every prayer: "Subhanaka Allahumma Rabbana wa bihamdika, Allahummaghfir li" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4967). Make it part of your own prayer — especially after good news. Success is the moment to seek forgiveness, not the moment to forget it.

3. Remember the farewell. Every mission ends. The Prophet's ended eighty days after this surah, and he spent those days in worship. Your own work will also be completed one day — the question is what remains when it does. Build something that outlasts you.

4. Let success make you softer, not harder. The conqueror of Makkah entered with his head bowed and freed his enemies. If your victory makes you harsh, you have misunderstood it.

5. Keep inviting — the crowds come at the end. For twenty-three years the believers were a handful; then they came in multitudes. Do not judge your da'wah, your teaching, or your family's tarbiyah by the early years. Stay gentle, stay consistent, and leave the timing to Allah. The verse says "you will see" — and He kept that promise in the Prophet's lifetime.

"When the help of Allah and victory come, and you see the people entering into the religion of Allah in multitudes, then exalt [Him] with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him. Indeed, He is ever Accepting of repentance." — Quran 110:1-3

Three verses. Read them tonight — the help, the crowds, the command — and ask yourself: when Allah's help arrives in your life, what will you do with it? The Prophet's answer was praise and forgiveness, right to the end. Make it yours. If this surah steadied your heart, share it with someone waiting for their own victory — the help comes first, and the victory follows.
