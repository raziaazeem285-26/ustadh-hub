Prophet Lut (AS): The City That Would Not Listen

R Razia Azeem · 16 Nov, 2026

One man stood alone against a whole city that had invented a sin no one before them had committed — and the morning the cities were turned upside down.

Seerah & Stories

One man stood alone against a whole city that had invented a sin no one before them had committed. This is the story of Prophet Lut (AS) — the nephew of Ibrahim, the angels who came as guests, and the "strong support" he never knew he already had.

1.The Nephew of Ibrahim

Lut (AS) was the nephew of Ibrahim (AS). When Ibrahim was commanded to leave his land, Lut believed in him and went with him: "And Lut believed him. And he said, 'Indeed, I will emigrate to my Lord. Indeed, it is He who is the Exalted in Might, the Wise'" (Quran 29:26, in meaning). Allah delivered them both to a blessed land: "And We delivered him and Lut to the land which We had blessed for the worlds" (Quran 21:71, in meaning).

Then Allah gave Lut his own mission, to the cities of his people — the people tradition knows as Sodom and Gomorrah, by the Dead Sea. The Quran does not name the cities; it calls them simply "the people of Lut", and the Dead Sea location is the scholars' view, not a Quranic statement. What the Quran does say, in ten surahs and some eighty verses, is what those people did — and what happened to them. For the story of his uncle, read Prophet Ibrahim (AS): The Friend of Allah.

2.A Sin No One Had Done Before

Lut's warning was unlike any before it — and so was the sin. "And [We had sent] Lut when he said to his people, 'Do you commit such immorality as no one has preceded you with from among the worlds?'" (Quran 7:80, in meaning).

وَلُوطًا إِذۡ قَالَ لِقَوۡمِهِۦۤ أَتَأۡتُونَ ٱلۡفَـٰحِشَةَ مَا سَبَقَكُم بِهَا مِنۡ أَحَدࣲ مِّنَ ٱلۡعَـٰلَمِینَ

Quran 7:80 — "And [We had sent] Lut when he said to his people, 'Do you commit such immorality as no one has preceded you with from among the worlds?'"

The Quran names the sin plainly: "Indeed, you approach men with desire, instead of women. Rather, you are a transgressing people" (Quran 7:81, in meaning). And it was not a hidden sin: they robbed travellers on the roads and committed their evil openly, in their gatherings: "Indeed, you approach men and obstruct the road and commit in your meetings [every] evil" (Quran 29:29, in meaning). A whole city had taken a wrong and made it normal.

Their answer was always the same: "Bring us the punishment of Allah, if you should be of the truthful" (Quran 29:29, in meaning). They did not argue or ask for proof — they simply demanded the punishment, as if it would never come. It is the same demand 'Ad made to Hud (Quran 7:70). For Hud's story, read Prophet Hud (AS): The Wind That Brought Down a Mighty Nation.

3.The Guests Who Were Angels

Then the guests came. The angels had first visited Ibrahim (AS) with glad tidings of a son, then travelled on to Lut. "And when Our messengers came to Lut, he was distressed for them and felt for them great discomfort and said, 'This is a distressful day'" (Quran 11:77, in meaning). He did not know they were angels — only that young men had arrived in a city where such guests were in danger.

He was right. "And his people came hastening to him, and before [this] they had been doing evil deeds" (Quran 11:78, in meaning). The whole city rushed to his door, demanding he hand over his guests. And Lut, with no army and no supporters — only his own household — stood in the doorway and offered them the lawful way instead: "He said, 'O my people, these are my daughters; they are purer for you. So fear Allah and do not disgrace me concerning my guests. Is there not among you a man of reason?'" (Quran 11:78, in meaning). The scholars explain that by "my daughters" he meant lawful marriage — the pure way instead of the foul one; some add that a prophet is like a father to his people, so it can also mean the community's women. Either way, the offer was the same: the lawful door, not the unlawful one.

The mob answered: "They said, 'You have already known that we have not concerning your daughters any desire, and indeed, you know what we want'" (Quran 11:79, in meaning). They did not want wives — they wanted the guests. And Lut stood alone in his doorway, one man against a city.

4."Would That I Had Strength"

It was then that Lut said the words the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ would remember centuries later: "He said, 'If only I had against you some power or could take refuge in a strong support'" (Quran 11:80, in meaning).

قَالَ لَوۡ أَنَّ لِی بِكُمۡ قُوَّةً أَوۡ ءَاوِیۤ إِلَىٰ رُكۡنࣲ شَدِیدࣲ

Quran 11:80 — "He said, 'If only I had against you some power or could take refuge in a strong support.'"

He wished for strength — a powerful family, a tribe, a fortress — to stand behind while he stood for the truth. And the Prophet ﷺ, when he recited this verse, said: "May Allah have mercy on Lut; he certainly used to seek refuge in a strong support" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3372, in meaning). Another narration puts it more plainly: "May Allah forgive Lut: he wanted to have a powerful support" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3375, in meaning) — and the support he already had was Allah. Lut thought he was standing alone. He did not know that the "strong support" — al-rukn al-shadid — was already holding him. The believer who stands for the truth never stands alone. The support is not a tribe or a fortress. The support is Allah.

5.The Night Escape

Then the guests revealed themselves. "They said, 'O Lut, indeed we are the messengers of your Lord; [therefore], they will never reach you. So set out with your family during a portion of the night and let not any among you look back — except your wife; indeed, she will be struck by what strikes them. Indeed, their appointment is [for] the morning. Is not the morning near?'" (Quran 11:81, in meaning).

The angels told him to leave in the dark with his family, walking behind them: "So set out with your family during a portion of the night and follow behind them and let not anyone among you look back, and go on to where you are commanded" (Quran 15:65, in meaning). And they were saved before the dawn: "Indeed, We sent upon them a storm of stones, except the family of Lut; We saved them before dawn" (Quran 54:34, in meaning).

But his wife was not saved. "Except his wife; indeed, she is of those who remain behind" (Quran 15:60, in meaning). She had never left them in her heart. Allah gives her as a warning, paired with the wife of Nuh: "Allah presents an example of those who disbelieved: the wife of Noah and the wife of Lot. They were under two of Our righteous servants but they betrayed them" (Quran 66:10, in meaning). Two prophets could not save their own wives — no one is saved by being near a prophet. Each soul is saved by its own faith. For Nuh's story, read Prophet Nuh (AS): 950 Years of Patience and the Ark.

6.The Morning the Cities Were Overturned

When the morning came, the punishment arrived. "And when Our command came, We made the highest part [of the city] its lowest and rained upon them stones of baked clay, piled up, marked from your Lord. And it is not far from the wrongdoers" (Quran 11:82-83, in meaning).

فَلَمَّا جَاۤءَ أَمۡرُنَا جَعَلۡنَا عَـٰلِیَهَا سَافِلَهَا وَأَمۡطَرۡنَا عَلَیۡهَا حِجَارَةࣰ مِّن سِجِّیلࣲ مَّنضُودࣲ

Quran 11:82 — "And when Our command came, We made the highest part [of the city] its lowest and rained upon them stones of baked clay, piled up."
"And when Our command came, We made the highest part [of the city] its lowest and rained upon them stones of baked clay, piled up, marked from your Lord. And it is not far from the wrongdoers." — Quran 11:82-83

The cities were turned upside down — "So the shriek seized them at sunrise. And We made the highest part [of the city] its lowest and rained upon them stones of hard clay" (Quran 15:73-74, in meaning). A storm of stones fell — "Indeed, We sent upon them a storm of stones, except the family of Lut; We saved them before dawn" (Quran 54:34, in meaning). The rain was not water but stone: "And We rained upon them a rain [of stones]. Then see how was the end of the criminals" (Quran 7:84, in meaning). The Quran does not say how the cities were lifted — it simply says Allah made their highest part their lowest. The commentators describe the angel lifting them, but the Quran leaves the means to Allah: cities that raised themselves above every law were brought lower than the ground they stood on.

And the ruins were left where travellers could see them. "And indeed, you pass by them in the morning and at night. Then will you not reason?" (Quran 37:137-138, in meaning). The Quran tells the people of Makkah, who passed the ruins on their trade journeys to Syria, that the sign is still on the road: "And indeed, it is on a road [still] traveled" (Quran 15:76, in meaning). The low, lifeless land is still there, and the road still passes it.

7.Five Lessons from Lut's Story for Your Life Today

1. No one is saved by proximity. Lut's wife lived in a prophet's house — and it did not save her, because her heart had stayed with the city. Being born in a Muslim family does not save anyone. Each soul must believe for itself.

2. Standing alone is still standing. Lut had no army, no tribe, no supporters — only his household — yet he stood in his doorway. The whole city was wrong and one man was right, and Allah saved the one man. Numbers never decide the truth.

3. The strong support is already there. Lut wished for a powerful support, and the Prophet ﷺ said he already had one. When you stand for what is right and feel alone, remember al-rukn al-shadid: Allah is the strong support, holding you before you even asked. For the full teaching, read Tawakkul: Trusting Allah After You Have Done Your Part.

4. When a sin becomes the culture, the warning grows louder. The people of Lut did not hide their sin — they committed it openly, and demanded the punishment as a joke. A society that normalises what Allah forbade and mocks those who warn is walking the same road. The Quran's question still stands: "Then will you not reason?" (Quran 37:138, in meaning).

5. The door of repentance stays open until the morning comes. Lut warned his people for years; they demanded punishment in return. The morning did come — and only then was the door closed. Our morning has not come yet. Say Astaghfirullah today, and turn back. For the door that never closes, read Repentance (Tawbah): The Door That Never Closes.

Lut (AS) warned a city that had invented a sin; the city answered with a challenge. The angels came as guests, the family left in the night, and by sunrise the cities were upside down. The ruins are still on the road, and the Quran still asks: will you not reason?
